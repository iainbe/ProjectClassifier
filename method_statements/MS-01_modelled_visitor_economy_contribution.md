## When to use
When a buyer needs a defensible annual economic contribution for an attraction, institution or heritage offer, where the available data are official visitor volumes and spend plus operator and stakeholder evidence, and a measured counterfactual is not available.

## Steps
1. Split visitors into groups using official tourism volume and spend estimates (for example domestic day, domestic staying and inbound staying).
2. For each group apply two stated judgements: the share who engage with the offer and the share of their spending that depends on it.
3. Deduct a stated reserve for spending not securely received inside the study boundary.
4. Deduct a separate displacement allowance for other local activity crowded out.
5. Convert the remaining spending to GVA with a local visitor-economy conversion.
6. Add combined supplier and household effects with a stated multiplier.
7. Test one assumption at a time and present the results as scenarios, not confidence intervals.

## Data needed
Official visit and spend estimates for the boundary geography, operator and trade returns, stakeholder interviews, and a GVA conversion and multiplier with a stated source.

## What we can claim
A modelled working estimate of annual contribution, always stated with its boundary, price basis and modelled status. The calculation is reproducible and each adjustment has a distinct purpose.

## What we cannot claim
- A measured or causal impact, or a confidence range.
- An independent split between indirect and induced effects.
- Growth or decline against an earlier study that used a different measure (for example turnover against GVA).
- Any uplift from future events that has not been evidenced.

## Limits found in practice
The largest group (inbound staying visitors) carried about two-thirds of the attributed spending, so its engagement and trip-purpose judgements dominate. Engagement and attribution shares are model judgements, not survey results.

## Fit and gaps for T22-VAIMP
Gives an attribution and additionality structure for headline economic measures. It is built around visitor spending, not outcomes for creative careers, practices or businesses, so it answers only part of the V&A's key questions 3 to 6.
