- **Safe to say:** "We evaluated the programme using the client's own monitoring data and reported what the evaluation found. The programme's effects are the programme's; our contribution was the evaluation."

## Bid-ready paragraph (generic; fill the [slots])

For [programme and client] we carried out [an interim / a final] evaluation using the client's programme data. We compared delivery with targets, tested how far headline ratios depend on a single item, and assessed institutional and ecosystem change using monitoring data and stakeholder evidence. Where an earlier evaluation existed we compared the two positions and stated where the measures differed. We reported forecasts as forecasts. Programme effects are the programme's; our contribution was the evaluation.

## Steps
1. Obtain the client's programme management and financial data and record what is client-supplied.
2. Compute target-against-actual measures (leverage, cost per indirect job, recoupment, trainee placements).
3. Test concentration: how much of a headline ratio comes from the largest single item.
4. Assess institutional and ecosystem change with monitoring data and stakeholder testimony, keeping programme effects separate from our contribution.
5. Where an interim evaluation exists, state where the interim and final bases differ before comparing them.
6. Report forecasts as forecasts and state whether a later source confirms them (for example the interim Development Fund forecast, which the final evaluation did not confirm: C-G2-041, expired).

## What we cannot claim
- That we caused the programme's effects.
- That modelled indirect jobs are counted jobs.
- That a forecast was achieved without a later source.
- Growth between interim and final unless the measures are like-for-like.
- That interim figures are final outcomes: the interim evaluation reports interim and forecast positions.
