## When to use
When a buyer needs to know what a publicly funded programme delivered against its targets and what the evidence says about wider change, using the programme's own monitoring data plus stakeholder evidence.

## Steps
1. Obtain the client's programme management and financial data and record what is client-supplied.
2. Compute target-against-actual measures (for example leverage, cost per indirect job, recoupment, trainee placements).
3. Test concentration: how much of a headline ratio comes from the single largest item, and what the rest give on their own.
4. Assess institutional and ecosystem change with monitoring data and stakeholder testimony, keeping programme effects separate from our contribution.
5. Where an interim evaluation exists, compare interim and final positions on the same basis and say where they are not like-for-like.
6. Report forecasts as forecasts and state whether a later source confirms them.

## What we can claim
That we evaluated the programme and what the evaluation found, worded as "the evaluation found". Figures are the client's data, analysed by us.

## What we cannot claim
- That we caused the programme's effects.
- That modelled indirect jobs are counted jobs (they come from the client's agreed model).
- That a forecast was achieved without a later source.
- Growth between interim and final unless the measures are like-for-like.

## Limits found in practice
The final Production Fund evaluation's own interim-to-final table was not like-for-like, one production supplied about a third of all spend, and production rows summed to 858 jobs against a stated 859. These are recorded in the claim notes.

## Fit and gaps for T22-VAIMP
Shows disciplined programme evaluation and honest handling of forecasts. It evidences delivery against targets and reported ecosystem change, not long-run effects on individual creative careers, which the V&A's questions 3 and 4 ask for.
