Stage 1 is complete. The spillover and strategic option value toolkit has been built, tested against 10 pilot projects and 5 pilot tenders, and calibrated. The codebook has been revised from v0.1 to v1.0 incorporating 8 calibration changes identified during the pilot.

- Spillover/option value framing is not universally applicable. Some tenders (LCR Film, Derby) are conventional deliverables where leading with spillover methodology may have contributed to losses.

## 3. Key findings

### 3.1 Methodological signature

Fifth Sector has a identifiable methodological signature across its Creative Economy work:

1. **Convergence-economy analysis** — identifying creative-digital-technical convergence opportunities that conventional sector classifications miss. Present from CDEC (2012) through NE Scotland (2025).

2. **Strategic option value / gated sequencing** — designing interventions as sequenced investment decisions with explicit decision gates. Present in NE Scotland (year 1-7 sequencing), MITIH (three-phase investment sequence), ManMet FGTG (pilot-as-option), and multiple tenders.

3. **LinkedIn-based workforce mapping** — using LinkedIn as a large-scale occupational survey to identify freelance and micro-enterprise workers that official data misses. Developed with Curator Technologies, reused across 5+ projects.

4. **Multi-source reconciliation** — triangulating ONS/BRES/IDBR with LinkedIn, Beauhurst, Companies House, and The Data City to produce more complete pictures than any single source.

5. **Social network analysis** — identifying bridging organisations and ecosystem structure. Applied in MITIH (2025); newer but demonstrated.

6. **Counterfactual benchmarking** — using comparator geographies (Nottingham for NE Scotland, national averages for Kirklees) to contextualise findings.

7. **Rigour review (/deepthink, /blindspot)** — self-critical scrutiny of proposals and evidence. Applied to TQH; not yet a standard practice across all work.

### 3.2 Evidence strength

The evidence base has clear strengths and limitations:

**Strengths:**
- Method application is well-documented in recent projects (NES, MITIH, Liverpool DCI, Kirklees)
- The methods are genuinely distinctive — few competitors combine convergence analysis, LinkedIn workforce mapping, SNA, and option value sequencing
- Null and negative findings are preserved (Liverpool DCI: "no evidence of effective clustering"; NE Scotland: "absence of durable connection infrastructure")

**Limitations:**
- Most claims are REPORTED (deliverer's own account) with DESCRIPTIVE attribution
- No project provides independently corroborated outcomes or causal estimates
- Two projects are bid-stage only (CoSTAR, Creative City) — their claims are forecasts
- Two projects were delivered as BOP associates (GBSLEP, WMCA) — attribution to Fifth Sector is qualified
- Post-intervention tracking is absent across all projects — outcomes are modelled/estimated, not observed after delivery
- The "200+ firms" figure from the methodology brief is not directly evidenced in any pilot project

### 3.3 Competitive advantage assessment

The pilot supports a qualified competitive advantage claim:

**Defensible claims:**
- Fifth Sector has a distinctive methodological signature combining convergence analysis, workforce mapping, SNA, and option value sequencing
- These methods have been applied across multiple projects over 13+ years (2012-2025)
- The gated sequencing approach is a genuine design pattern, not a retrospective label
- The rigour review process (/deepthink, /blindspot) is a level of self-critical scrutiny competitors are unlikely to match

**Claims that cannot yet be made:**
- That the methods have produced independently corroborated spillover outcomes (no project provides this)
- That Fifth Sector's methods have diffused to external firms (PRODUCT spillover) — only internal method reuse is evidenced
- That option value has been monetised (no project provides a monetary option valuation)
- That Fifth Sector's contribution caused specific client outcomes (attribution is DESCRIPTIVE, not CAUSAL_ESTIMATE)

### 3.4 Website implications

The website can position spillover analysis and strategic option value as a critical success factor and source of competitive advantage, but only with careful qualification:

**Can say:**
- Fifth Sector uses convergence-economy analysis, workforce mapping, social network analysis, and gated investment sequencing as distinctive methods
- These methods identify value that conventional approaches miss
- The methods have been applied across multiple UK creative economy projects since 2012
- Fifth Sector's evidence standards include preserving null findings and distinguishing documented evidence from hypotheses

**Cannot say (yet):**
- That specific spillover outcomes have been independently corroborated
- That option value has been monetised
- That Fifth Sector's methods have diffused to external firms
- That specific client outcomes were caused by Fifth Sector's contribution

**Website implementation requires:**
- Permission clearance for any named client evidence
- Clear separation of documented evidence from methodological capability claims
- No comparative claims against competitors without evidence
- Staged implementation with test/rollback planning (separate approval required)

## 6. Recommendations for G2 decision

### 6.1 Proceed to full portfolio screening

The toolkit is calibrated and ready. The pilot has demonstrated that the classification system works across a range of project types (mapping, bid support, evaluation, advisory) and tender types (RFP, ITQ, direct engagement, RFQ).

**Proposed scope for full screening:**
- All 91 Creative Economy projects in the CSV (subject to reconciliation with the database)
- Plus substantively related adjacent work (innovation, technology, skills, tourism, place)
- Record borderline projects with rationale for inclusion/exclusion
- Produce a coverage map showing which projects have evidence available and which are gaps

### 6.2 Prioritise deeper case studies

From the pilot, the strongest candidates for deeper case studies (with client permission) are:

1. **NE Scotland** — strongest example of convergence analysis, option value sequencing, and four-tier modelling
2. **MITIH Createch** — strongest example of SNA application and three-phase investment sequence
3. **Kirklees CI** — demonstrates methodological durability (repeat commission) and multi-source reconciliation
4. **Liverpool DCI** — demonstrates scenario analysis, options framing, and null findings preservation

### 6.3 Begin permission clearance for website evidence

The website cannot be updated without client permission. Begin the permission process now for the strongest cases to avoid delaying implementation later.

### 6.4 Do not begin website implementation

Website implementation requires separate approval. The evidence base supports a qualified competitive advantage claim, but the qualification matters. Draft website content should be prepared for review before any implementation.

### 6.5 Record tender outcomes systematically

The pilot tender assessment is based on only 2 lost bids. Build a systematic record of all tender outcomes (win/loss, reasons, pricing, methodology used) to calibrate the bid/no-bid framework.

### 6.6 Consider an evaluation programme

The pilot confirms that most claims are REPORTED with DESCRIPTIVE attribution. To strengthen the evidence base, consider designing an evaluation programme that:
- Tracks outcomes 6-12 months post-delivery
- Uses comparator geographies for counterfactual analysis
- Independently corroborates key claims
- Values option outcomes monetarily where feasible

This would require separate approval and resourcing.
