## Cross-tender synthesis

### Patterns identified

1. **The strongest tenders for spillover/option value framing are those where the buyer explicitly asks for it.** British Council (T01) names spillovers, option value, and multipliers in the RFP. TQH (T02) asks "what would be exposed if Queen's Hall could not modernise" — an option value question. These tenders allow Fifth Sector to lead with its distinctive methodology.

2. **Tenders for conventional deliverables (EIA, data strategy) do not support spillover/option value framing.** LCR Film (T04) and Derby (T05) are conventional EIAs/data strategies. Leading with Foresight/spillover methodology in these contexts may have contributed to the losses. The toolkit should include a "spillover relevance gate" in the scorecard.

3. **Gated sequencing is a consistent Fifth Sector design pattern.** All five tenders use some form of staged/gated design (British Council 6 phases, TQH 3 options, BwD 2 stages, LCR Film 3 phases, Derby implied). This is a genuine methodological signature.

4. **The Circuit/Foresight platform is a differentiator but also a risk.** It provides genuine analytical capability that competitors may not have, but it also risks being seen as a solution looking for a problem if the tender does not explicitly call for it.

5. **The /deepthink and /blindspot rigour review applied to TQH is itself a competitive advantage.** No competitor is likely to apply this level of self-critical scrutiny to their own proposals. This methodology should be documented as a reusable practice.

6. **Existing client relationships matter.** British Council (T01) and LCR (T04) both benefit from existing relationships. The toolkit should record relationship status as a bid/no-bid factor.

7. **Pricing strategy varies.** BwD (£9,999) and LCR Film (£9,750) are deliberately competitive "foot in the door" pricing. TQH (£27,500-£47,500) is value-based. British Council pricing is unknown. The toolkit should record pricing strategy and its relationship to win rate.

### Codebook calibration issues identified

1. **The tender scorecard needs a "spillover relevance gate"** — an explicit assessment of whether the buyer's language supports spillover/option value claims. If not, the bid should not lead with them.

2. **The scorecard should distinguish "platform-led" from "method-led" bids.** Platform-led bids (Foresight as the differentiator) carry different risks than method-led bids (Green Book, causal DAG as the differentiator).

3. **The "bid recommendation" field should capture retrospective outcomes for lost bids.** The toolkit should record why bids were lost to calibrate future bid/no-bid decisions.

4. **The "win themes" section needs a "permission status" check.** Themes drawn from pilot project assessments must respect the commercial reuse status recorded in the project assessment.
