EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## Factors that shape the Beatles economy

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
|  | The heritage offer has broadened since the original study, but individual attractions have different histories. Admissions, company turnover, street footfall and survey responses measure different activity. They cannot be combined into a count of unique Beatles visitors, nor does a rise at one venue establish a rise everywhere.Lockdown closures and recovery also interrupt the comparison with the earlier baseline.Visitor value depends on the whole itinerary. International pilgrims, domestic breaks, event visitors, cruise passengers and coach groups have different opportunities to stay, eat, shop and visit paid attractions. Free photo stops can still support retail or hospitality spending; a longer visit does not automatically produce a higher retail return. | The fifteen journey profiles explain these mechanisms and identify questions for measurement. They are overlapping archetypes, not fifteen additional populations to total.Geography shapes both capture and pressure. The centre combines attractions with dense hospitality provision; the southern route passes through residential streets and free heritage locations. Supplied licensing and food-business records show nearby provision and shared catchments, not a measured Beatles uplift for those businesses.Interviews repeatedly identify stopping, access, toilets and resident concerns. These warrant a distinct infrastructure discussion rather than an assumed future economic benefit. | The cultural evidence concerns learning, authenticity, living musical participation and local belonging as well as tourism. The summit's environmental, storytelling and technology themes connect these benefits to the quality of the visit. Announced exhibitions and the April 2028 films provide reasons to examine readiness.The annual estimate includes no automatic film, anniversary or cruise-expansion uplift.The most useful next evidence would distinguish trip motivation, itinerary and nights from attraction awareness; establish where excursion and package payments are received; and connect admissions, footfall, hotel and spend series through time. The closing chapter sets out these unresolved questions and the basis for recurring measurement.It does not prescribe a funded intervention or a new attraction business case. |

CHAPTER 01

# Why this update, and what it measures

Liverpool BID and the Beatles Legacy Group commissioned this update to understand the present economic and cultural contribution of Beatles heritage tourism.

In this chapter The commissioned question / Scope and definitions / How the evidence is used

CHAPTER 01

## The commissioned question

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
|  | Liverpool BID and the Beatles Legacy Group commissioned this update to establish what Beatles heritage tourism contributes to Liverpool today and to provide an evidence base for the city's tourism and music sectors. The earlier assessment was based principally on 2014 data and published in November 2015.New attractions, different visitor routes and forthcoming cultural activity make an updated baseline useful. This report brings the annual economic estimate together with the cultural experience, the places where spending occurs and the pressures described by operators, guides and destination partners.The central question is how much economic activity Liverpool gains because of its Beatles heritage. That is wider than the receipts of dedicated attractions. A visit can involve a guided tour, an evening of live music, a hotel stay, meals, shopping and local transport. | The commercial relationships extend through travel agents, cruise excursion organisers, coach companies and accommodation providers. The report therefore follows the whole trip, while asking how much of it is attributable to the Beatles rather than to Liverpool's other attractions, football, business or visiting friends and relatives.The earlier study made this diffuse economy visible through businesses, suppliers and wider visitor spending, alongside cultural and institutional evidence. This update retains that breadth. It does not assume that cultural identity, education or residents' enjoyment cease to matter when they cannot be assigned a reliable monetary value.Equally, it does not add a speculative brand valuation or a future film uplift to the current annual economic figure. Cultural contribution and commercial contribution are related, but they are not interchangeable accounts. | Figure 3 How the evidence informs the assessmentAnalytical framework. CHAPTER 01

## Scope, definitions and the evidence frame

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
|  | Geography and evidence frameThe intended economic boundary is Liverpool City, not the whole Liverpool City Region. The annual model is expressed in 2025 prices and uses the latest held tourism denominators: 2025 domestic day and staying estimates, and 2024 inbound visits with spending adjusted to 2025 prices. The title's 2026 date describes the assessment, not a completed 2026 visitor outturn. Detailed STEAM reports can provide further destination context, but access to a screen-shared report or a promised extract is not equivalent to a received and reconciled dataset.Four evidence streams contribute to the assessment. Official tourism estimates supply the annual volume and expenditure frame. Operator and commercial returns describe attraction activity, sales channels and the trading environment. Interviews and summit material explain motivation, itinerary formation, local spending and operational constraints. Spatial and event analysis shows the relationship between heritage anchors, nearby hospitality and changes in movement around particular dates. | Visits and interpretationVisits are trips, not unique people over the year. Attraction admissions, coached groups and street-counter movements are different units again. One tourist can buy several tickets, pass a counter more than once and revisit Liverpool. Engagement means taking part in Beatles-related activity; attribution means the share of spending that would not have happened without that heritage offer. Someone can enjoy a Beatles attraction without the Beatles being the principal reason for travelling to the city.Each answers a different question. Supplied admissions can describe a venue's trajectory but cannot identify all Beatles-motivated city trips. A published tariff establishes an available price, not what every visitor bought. An interview can provide a credible mechanism for an overnight coach itinerary without establishing how common it is citywide. Qualitative evidence is therefore used to inform transparent model judgements and alternative scenarios, rather than being discarded or turned into invented population statistics. | GVA and the audit trailVisitor expenditure is the amount spent on the trip. Turnover is a business's sales. Gross value added, or GVA, measures the value generated through production after intermediate inputs are accounted for. Direct GVA arises in the businesses serving the attributable visitor spending. Indirect effects arise through suppliers and induced effects through the subsequent spending of incomes. The current model estimates those wider effects together; it does not support an independently measured split between them.The working model and calculations sit alongside this narrative. The technical evidence pack retains detailed formulas, source locators, alternative assumptions and the complete journey cards. The earlier page-led report is preserved as an appendix rather than treated as the main report's structure. Provider-specific disclosure conditions remain applicable: supplied information is not automatically cleared for public attribution.This is a baseline assessment of the existing Beatles visitor economy: a modelled annual contribution supported and challenged by observed activity and stakeholder evidence. Its value is the combination of a usable economic answer, a recognisable account of the visitor experience and explicit questions for further investigation. |

## The economic bridge

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Figure 11 From attributed visitor expenditure to GVAReproducible arithmetic with model uncertainty visible. £m expenditure/GVA; stages explicitly distinguished. | From £211.3 million of attributable expenditure, a 20% residual out-of-area allocation leaves £169.1 million. ## Wider effects and uncertainty

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
|  | The multiplier recognises local supplier and household effects beyond the businesses serving visitors directly. Liverpool's music-economy multiplier of around 1.9 is relevant comparative evidence, but a music production system is not the same sector mix or geography as hotels, restaurants, retail, tours and transport.The central 1.30 comes from a local visitor-economy precedent. Category mix, procurement and local settlement evidence could support a more tailored calculation; the present model cannot manufacture a separate induced figure or add a second brand halo on top of spending already counted. | Holding every other input fixed, the saved 30% residual-allocation case produces £58.5 million and zero further residual allocation produces £83.6 million. Those figures show the consequence of one assumption. They are not confidence limits or an observed range of the true contribution.Attribution, inbound spending, displacement and conversion also matter. Tested alternatives belong beside the working answer, with their changed assumptions stated; they should not be represented as statistical uncertainty without an appropriate probability model. | Figure 12 Sensitivity to individual modelling assumptions£m modelled GVA; individual scenarios, not confidence intervals. |

## Technology and social media

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
|  | Digital accessTechnology can introduce the story, support access and help visitors navigate, but its value depends on what it changes in the experience. Summit participants described an iPad view of upstairs rooms for visitors unable to climb stairs and discussed live translation, including the difficulty of preserving local meaning. Digital material can open a route into Beatles history for people who have not yet travelled.
