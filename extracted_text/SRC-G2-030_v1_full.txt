Executive Summary
The Liverpool City Region Production Fund represents a transformative investment in the region's screen industries, delivering exceptional economic, cultural and social returns. Between 2020 and 2024, the Fund invested £2.82 million across ten high-value productions, generating £24.5 million in direct local spend—a return of £8.69 for every £1 invested, significantly exceeding the target of £8 per £1.
The Fund has demonstrably catalysed sustained growth across Liverpool City Region's screen ecosystem. 
Film days across the region have increased from 710 in 2014/15 to a peak of 1,608 in 2018/19, sustaining over 1,200 days annually even through pandemic disruption. 
Regional inward investment has grown from £8.7 million in 2014/15 to £47 million in 2023/24, representing more than a five-fold increase over the decade. 
What began as a financial mechanism to attract productions has evolved into a comprehensive engine for screen sector development, stimulating the growth of production hubs across the city region, enabling the Liverpool Film Office to transition from a permits service to a fully-fledged screen agency. 
The Fund has successfully:
Created 859 indirect full-time equivalent jobs at an average cost of just £3,284 per job
Placed 56 trainees across funded productions, building the local talent pipeline
Achieved 41% recoupment to date (against a 30% target) with 55% anticipated by 2025
Garnered over 60 award nominations and 19 industry awards. including four BAFTAs and two International Emmys
Established production hubs across all six LCR boroughs, with significant growth in crew base, facilities, and production companies region-wide
The Fund's impact extends beyond economics—it has enhanced Liverpool's global profile, developed indigenous talent, and strengthened the entire regional production ecosystem. Based on the Combined Authority's approval, the Fund will continue through June 2027 with reinvestment of recouped funds, maintaining its focus on high-value production while enhancing support for indigenous production companies.
1. 	Introduction and Background
Purpose and focus of the evaluation
This evaluation assesses the Liverpool City Region (LCR) Production Fund, established by Liverpool City Region Combined Authority (LCRCA) and delivered by Liverpool City Council's (LCC) Liverpool Film Office (LFO). Launched in April 2020, the Fund aimed to attract high-value film and TV productions to the region, stimulate local economic growth, support skills development, and enhance Liverpool's profile as a filming destination. The evaluation informs LCRCA's Gateway Review and provides evidence supporting the Fund's continuation beyond December 2024.
The intervention (project description)
The LCR Film & Television Production Fund was designed to develop the screen industries across the Liverpool City Region through two interlinked elements:
Enhancement and expansion of the Liverpool Film Office (LFO) to deliver service across the entire LCR, promoting locations, facilities, and services while optimising service to external investors
Establishment of a Content Fund to attract high-value projects, enable targeted attraction of production and facilities companies, and improve opportunities for indigenous companies
Liverpool City Region Combined Authority provided £2 million in initial capital funding to support investment in film and television projects shooting in the region and development funding for new projects with strong LCR-based creative elements. £200,000 in revenue funding created four full-time roles to extend LFO operations across all LCR boroughs. Recapitalisation in October 2021 added £1 million to the Fund with completion extended to December 2024.
Liverpool Film Office transformation
A significant success story emerging from this evaluation is the transformation of Liverpool Film Office from a traditional permit-issuing authority to an influential screen development agency. This evolution has enabled LFO to:
Provide comprehensive production support beyond regulatory services
Develop strategic industry relationships with commissioners and producers
Establish cross-sector collaborations with economic development, tourism, and education
Coordinate screen industry development across all six LCR boroughs
Position Liverpool as a globally recognised filming destination
Support sector specific training initiatives and developing new access routes into the TV and film industries for local residents ADDED 
This transformation is evident in the metrics: LFO now directly supports between 240-340 productions annually across LCR, generating between 1,200-1,600 filming days per year. As referenced in the Government's English Devolution White Paper (page 73), LFO has become a model for regional screen agency development.

2.2 	Recoupment and reinvestment performance
The Fund's investment model requires productions to repay the investment through a negotiated share of sales revenues and net profits. This approach has proven highly successful, with Fund managers securing advantageous recoupment positions (often in first position or joint first position with distributors for international sales), plus profit shares of 4-10% in perpetuity.
As of October 2024, the Fund has recouped £1,143,287 (41% of total investment), significantly exceeding the target of 30%. By end of 2025, projections indicate 55% recoupment (£1,539,898). This success allows for sustainable reinvestment in future productions, creating a virtuous cycle of screen investment across the region.
The Combined Authority has approved reinvestment of recouped funds (approximately £1.5 million) to extend the Fund through June 2027. This continuation will enable four additional investments over the next 18 months while the business case is developed for potential further funding from April 2026.
2.3 	Development Fund impact
In April 2020, as an agile response to the COVID-19 pandemic, £250,000 of the capital fund was reallocated to provide development investments to LCR-based producers and writers. This emergency pivot proved critical when the sector was completely shut down, providing indigenous companies and individuals with vital income and development capital. The Development Fund attracted 64 applications before closing in December 2020. While modest in scale, this intervention provided crucial support during the industry's darkest hours and helped sustain local creative talent through the pandemic. (See Appendix C, below)

3. 	Wider Economic and Cultural Value
3.1 	Skills and Professional Development
The Fund has made significant contributions to skills development and workforce capacity across Liverpool City Region. Through the 56 trainee positions created directly through funded productions, many individuals have established sustainable careers in the industry. However, Liverpool's skills development approach is evolving rapidly through participation in Screen Alliance North, which represents the UK's most innovative cross-regional skills collaboration. This alliance model has achieved exceptional diversity outcomes (46% working-class, 32% ethnically diverse participants) and comprehensive pathway development that surpasses individual regional approaches.
The integration of trainee placements with funded productions has proven particularly effective. As one large-scale producer noted: 
"The training programmes that LFO offers are a model of good practice and really helped us resource the productions in increasingly effective ways, helping people get a foothold in industry and step up. People working on productions were really getting an education out of it."

3.4 	COVID-19 Resilience and Recovery
The Fund played a critical role during the pandemic, when the screen sector in Liverpool became one of the first industries to safely resume operations under strict COVID protocols. This early restart provided economic lifelines to multiple sectors:
Hotels that would otherwise have remained closed were able to accommodate production crews
Suppliers and service providers maintained operations through production contracts
Freelancers who fell through gaps in government support schemes found employment on productions
Productions including Time and Help, which filmed during the pandemic, collectively spent £250,000 on COVID safety measures, creating additional jobs while ensuring safe working conditions. These productions not only provided immediate economic value but also demonstrated Liverpool's resilience and capability to deliver under challenging circumstances.
The statistics illustrate this recovery effect. After dropping to 718 filming days in 2020/21 during peak pandemic restrictions, filming days rebounded to 1,210 in 2021/22 and maintained strong levels thereafter, with 1,481 days in 2022/23 and 1,602 days in 2023/24.
3.5	Emerging skills infrastructure
Liverpool's skills development landscape is poised for transformation through two key initiatives:
Screen Alliance North: As a founding member of this cross-regional alliance, Liverpool benefits from shared resources, coordinated training programs, and industry-wide best practices. The alliance's comprehensive pathway system (Connected Campus through Connected Opportunities) provides more robust career development than any single regional approach.
Liverpool Screen Academy Proposal: Founder and CEO of Hat Trick Productions, Liverpool-born Jimmy Mulville's proposal for a Liverpool-based screen academy, modelled on the successful Londo  Independent Screen Academy, could provide transformative infrastructure for 16+ education. With Department for Education discussions underway and potential private investment from the Friedkin Group, this initiative could:
Train 50-150 students annually in practical screen skills
Generate £9-27mn economic impact over five years
Anchor major infrastructure development at Littlewoods
Create sustainable pipeline from education to employment
The combination of Screen Alliance North's collaborative model and a dedicated Liverpool Screen Academy would position the city region as a national leader in screen skills development. Strategic implications of such a development could include:
For Liverpool Film Office
Further leverage current Screen Alliance North programme, and make a stronger case for potential funding extensions, to maximise participation in collaborative programmes
Champion Screen Academy: Actively support Jimmy Mulville's Liverpool Screen Academy proposal through political and industry channels
Integrate approaches: Ensure Production Fund training requirements align with Screen Alliance North pathways
For Combined Authority
Strategic investment: Consider Screen Academy as infrastructure investment, not just education
Political advocacy: Support Department for Education discussions
Private sector engagement: Facilitate discussions with Friedkin Group and other investors
For Industry Development
Pipeline integration: Connect Screen Academy graduates directly to Production Fund opportunities
Industry commitment: Secure first-job guarantees from major productions
Infrastructure synergy: Use Screen Academy as anchor for broader Littlewoods development
This combination has the potential to provide both collaborative regional benefits and dedicated local infrastructure. The key is coordinated development across all three elements, with the Screen Academy offering itself as critical skills infrastructure, serving as a transformative catalyst for the entire ecosystem.
4. Regional Ecosystem Development
4.1 	Transformation of Liverpool Film Office 
The Fund has enabled Liverpool Film Office to transform from a traditional permit-issuing department to a comprehensive screen development agency. This evolution has significantly expanded LFO's regional reach and impact. Before the Fund, LFO operated primarily within Liverpool proper, focusing on processing permits and managing filming logistics. With the £200,000 revenue funding to create four FTE positions, LFO expanded its operations across all six LCR boroughs, establishing coordinated service provision region-wide. 4.2 	Development of Regional Production Hubs
One of the Fund's most significant achievements has been catalysing the development of production hubs throughout the City Region, reducing dependence on Liverpool city centre and creating more balanced economic growth. Emerging production hubs include:

Halton (The Heath) - Transformed into a significant drama production centre, with filming days increasing from 1 day in 2019 to 126 days by 2022/23
St Helens (Alexander Business Park) - Emerging as a specialist location for high-value productions, with the Peaky Blinders film utilizing Pilkington's Glass

4.3 	Infrastructure Development
The Fund has directly influenced infrastructure development across LCR, addressing key gaps in production capacity. While The Depot (supported by separate funding but complementary to the Fund) has provided large-scale sound stage capacity, the Fund has supported smaller-scale infrastructure development through its investments:

5. 	Path Forward (2024-2027)
5.1 	Agreed extension model
In July 2024, the Liverpool City Council Cabinet approved the submission of a Project Change Request Form to LCRCA that will:
Re-invest up to £1.5 million of returns from the Fund's existing arrangements in film and TV productions, under the terms of the existing grant funding agreement
Extend the completion date to 30 June 2027
This extension maintains the Fund's proven investment model while allowing for strategic enhancements to address emerging needs. The model will continue to focus on attracting high-value productions while incrementally increasing support for indigenous production companies.
5.2 	Operational improvements
Based on feedback from producers and stakeholders, several operational improvements will be implemented during the extension period:
Documentation and guidance - Development of a comprehensive handbook providing clear guidance for production teams at all levels, addressing the common issue of junior production staff being unfamiliar with Fund criteria
Streamlined reporting - Standardised templates and processes to reduce administrative burden
Enhanced local supplier integration - Expanded directories of local suppliers, crew, and services
Post-production capacity development - Working closely with Molinare to develop local post-production capability and training
These improvements will address the administrative friction identified by some producers while maintaining the Fund's core strengths—its competitive financial offer and the comprehensive support provided by Liverpool Film Office.
6. Conclusions and Recommendations
Conclusions
The Liverpool City Region Production Fund has unequivocally exceeded its objectives, delivering exceptional returns on investment:
£2.82 million investment has generated £24.5 million in local spend—a ratio of £8.69:£1
859 indirect FTE jobs created at an average cost of just £3,284 per job
56 trainees placed on funded productions, strengthening the local talent pipeline
41% recoupment achieved to date, with 55% anticipated by 2025
Critical acclaim demonstrated through 19 major industry awards
Beyond these quantifiable outcomes, the Fund has transformed Liverpool Film Office from a permit-issuing department to a comprehensive screen development agency, catalysed growth across all six LCR boroughs, and enhanced Liverpool's global profile as a premier filming destination. 
The statistics tell a compelling story of sustained growth. Film days across LCR have increased from 710 in 2014/15 to maintain consistently over 1,200 days annually since 2018/19, even through pandemic disruption. Regional inward investment has grown from £8.7 million in 2014/15 to £47 million in 2023/24—more than a five-fold increase over the decade. Importantly, the Fund has demonstrated adaptability and resilience, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic when it pivoted to support development activity and facilitated some of the first productions to resume filming under COVID protocols.
Recommendations
Continue the Fund through June 2027 as approved, using recouped funds to maintain momentum while preparing for further funding applications
Implement the planned operational improvements, particularly the comprehensive handbook for production teams to ensure consistent understanding of Fund criteria
Enhance post-production capacity by supporting Molinare's expansion plans and facilitating training opportunities in post-production specialisms
Consider small-scale studio development (10-14,000 square feet) to meet the needs of lower budget TV productions, complementing The Depot's larger facilities
Explore potential for enhanced indigenous producer support within the existing Fund model, potentially through earmarked development allocations
Maintain the expanded regional focus, continuing to build production hubs across all six LCR boroughs
Prioritise Screen Academy Development: Support Jimmy Mulville's Liverpool Screen Academy proposal through:
Advocacy with Department for Education for approval
Facilitation of location discussions, particularly Littlewoods site
Coordination with private investors including Friedkin Group
Integration with existing Screen Alliance North pathways
Alignment with Production Fund training requirements
The Liverpool City Region Production Fund represents an exemplary model of targeted public investment generating substantial economic, cultural and social returns. Its extension through 2027 will enable continued growth and development of the LCR screen ecosystem, building on its demonstrated success and addressing emerging opportunities and challenges.

Conclusion
While higher indigenous grant allocations offer significant social and environmental benefits, they come with substantial economic trade-offs.
A graduated approach starting with 10% allocation allows for capacity building and model refinement while maintaining the Fund's proven economic impact. 
The 30% scenario represents an optimal balance for long-term transformation.
50% allocation should only be considered once indigenous sector capacity is fully established.
The key to success lies not in the percentage allocated, but in the quality of implementation, support infrastructure, and integration with broader sector development objectives. A well-implemented indigenous grant program, even at modest scale, could deliver transformative social and cultural benefits while building the foundation for Liverpool's long-term screen sector sustainability.
