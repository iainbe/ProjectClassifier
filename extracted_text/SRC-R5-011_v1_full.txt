Executive summary
What
A sector-led delivery vehicle for cultural development in Derby
An independent body with its own aims and governance
A mechanism to help deliver Vibrant City priorities for Culture, the City Centre Vision and Culture Strategy.
Why
To make Derby City Centre a vibrant, safe and inclusive cultural destination that benefits all its communities
To bring about greater equity and opportunity to participate in culture and creative industries
To provide leadership for the cultural sector on key strategic objectives
To support and (where needed) lead on bids for the cultural and creative community
To build on the legacy of the City of Culture 2025 bid and 2029 ambition
To continue the momentum of creative regeneration in Derby
How
Establish Culture Derby – a “cultural compact” bringing together the city’s people, communities, artists, cultural organisations, creative businesses, schools, colleges and the university, private sector and social organisations.
Deliver #OpenDerby - curating and supporting creative businesses and local communities to access city centre unused space for their work and development
Support Cultural Disruption
Diversify the cultural and creative workforce
Deliver an annual Youth Summit to ensure that the voice of younger audiences and artists is properly represented in Derby
Secure and manage relationships with other UK and Global Cities for cultural exchange
Outcomes:
Making sure that Derby behaves differently from neighbouring cities in promoting culture
Culture Derby Manifesto
People
We spoke to people across Derby to find their views on how culture could help regenerate Derby’s city centre, help recovery of businesses from the impact of lockdowns and better reflect all Derby’s people and communities. We found a huge appetite amongst cultural organisations, creative businesses and communities to play a greater and more involved role in development of the city centre. We also identified the need for more democratic access to resource to enable new kinds of work to flourish, and for the cultural sector and creative workforce to become much more diverse. 
Places
Culture Derby reimagines our city with culture at its heart. We have identified a portfolio of vacant spaces across the city and recommended a framework which will see them openly and fairly allocated for cultural use. 
Programming of these spaces will incentivise local artists and communities to pursue their own development, focusing on building a resilient cultural sector, not spectacle. Public agencies and private landowners will work together to signpost and promote them – making sure that people know where to go and are reassured that it will be safe.
Participants
Derby’s existing cultural offer is not representative of the diversity of its people and communities. Culture Derby sets out a manifesto of disruption through inclusion. By improving people’s access to cultural activity in local and community hubs as a first step to wider engagement through co-creation and co-production, The Manifesto aims to make Derby’s cultural offer representative of all our communities.  This will add to the work of existing arts organisations, making Derby a destination of choice and making culture the focus of a dynamic and diverse city centre offer.
Perception
Culture Derby recognises that you need to change the way people do things to change the way people see things.
Culture Derby will initiate and drive the processes of cultural disruption and transformation – but delivery of its ambitions calls for vision and leadership to be aligned with the activities of the City Centre Regeneration Partnership, Derby Live, Marketing Derby, Cathedral Quarter and St Paul’s Quarter Business Improvement Districts and VisitDerby to define the narrative and coordinate the marketing and support for an inclusive cultural offer.

Cultural Masterplan
The Cultural Masterplan sets out priorities for long-term growth. The Culture Derby Manifesto translates those priorities into action, starting with the people-led aspects:
Your Cultural Heart
More People
More Disruption
#OpenDerby
A Stronger Sector
Your Cultural Heart
The centre of the city belongs to all and is connected to the cultural activity in peoples’ homes and communities. Proposals for Your 15 Minute Cultural City bring public, private and third sector stakeholders together to articulate Derby as a compact urban centre with an accessible, connected cultural ecosystem, celebrating every element of culture - world-class theatre, creative workspace, a broad food offer and eclectic music venues, eye-catching public art, UNESCO heritage and world-beating street dance - within an easy 15-minute cycle or walk.
More people
A successful cultural city needs a successfully and sustainably mixed and connected city centre in which to live, work and play. Weaving diversity and inclusion through all strands of activity and across protected characteristics, including people from lower socio-economic groups, Derby can provide a case study of how to present and encourage greater diversity and inclusion in cultural participation, programming and leadership.
More disruption
The city centre urgently needs growth in and wider range of cultural activity. Cultural disruptions – a programme of facilitated initiatives and installations, commissioned independently, which surprise and engage local communities, and create eye-catching content – will be a visible demonstration of change. 
#OpenDerby 
Public, private and third sector partners have identified some 500,000 sq ft of underused space in the city centre, One of the first acts of Culture Derby will be to secure and market this space locally and nationally for creative use. The resulting cultural takeovers will see under-represented creative practitioners and groups take control of underused parts of the city centre, make connections and democratise space, – making the city centre a platform for showcasing and developing the culture and creative practices of all Derby’s communities
A stronger sector
Development of a broader and more representative board (in the form of a Cultural Compact – see below) will be underpinned by new cross-sector Cultural Policy Taskforce. This will bring together those who set and implement policies (such as licensing) with those who are affected by them to align the ambition for cultural regeneration with a permissive regulatory environment.

Potential to attract new investment, catalysed by Derby’s shortlisting for City of Culture 2025

Evidence of what cultural producers, creative businesses and communities across Derby want

CULTURE DERBY – A CULTURAL COMPACT FOR THE CITY
A strategically driven delivery vehicle for cultural transformation of Derby, Culture Derby will be:
Creative: an advocate and guardian of ways of doing and seeing things differently
Entrepreneurial and market aware - a mechanism to help deliver the objectives of the Culture Derby Manifesto, Cultural  Masterplan and related aspects of the City Centre Regeneration plan
Independent: at arms-length from the Council, with its own terms of reference, aims, business plan and governance, managed by an independent board made up of members of the cultural sector, university, other private sector businesses as well as the Council
Operating model and principles
For the Compact, form must follow function. In order to deliver ambition, quality and dynamism in Derby’s cultural offer, it must be enabled to challenge what has gone before. That means it will look and behave differently from previous committees:
With equality, diversity and inclusion at its core – including socio-economic and children & young people
Delivering behaviour change (in environmental responsibility and health & wellbeing) as well as cultural renewal
Scope of Culture Derby extends beyond “art and culture” into what engages young people – including sport, innovation and the STEAM agenda
Establishing Culture Derby will be a visible commitment to maintain momentum from City of Culture 2025 bid. Culture Derby will set out its early priorities toward achieving wider ambitions for:
Your Cultural Heart
More People
More Disruption
#OpenDerby
A Stronger Sector
Recommendations for the operating model draw on the experience of other Compacts (including Liverpool, Wakefield, Rushmoor), cultural Leadership groups and consortia (Manchester, Sunderland, Sheffield) and Creative People & Places projects.
The preferred option at this point is:
An unconstituted body, drawing resource and support from the Council
Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) between Council and key partners (University, private sector and other social organisations)
Culture Derby will make recommendations to and work in partnership with
City Centre Regeneration Partnership
Derby Live and Visit Derby
Cathedral Quarter  and St Paul’s Quarter Business Improvement Districts
Mainframe, Derby CAN and existing programmes;
Council-led work on Creative Cities – UNESCO and City of Culture 2029 bids
Representation
The Compact board must embody and drive increased diversity in membership and leadership of the cultural sector and creative industries. That means it will recruit for people and skills, not prior board experience.
Governance
As an interim solution to allow Culture Derby to quickly mobilise the Manifesto priorities, we suggest appointing a non-Council chair and two vice-chairs (one a development role) to work with MoU partners to recruit rest of board. 
That board will also, over time, decide whether and when it can look to evolve into some form of social enterprise (charity, CIC, coop or similar)
Implementation
Getting on with it and being seen to deliver is critical. 
Keep it simple and focused on clear priorities (defined under the Task & Finish groups detailed below) 
Culture Derby is not the accountable body for all elements of the manifesto but will provide both heart and brain to delivery of the Cultural Masterplan and Culture Strategy
Working in partnership to strengthen existing networks (eg, Mainframe, Derby CAN) rather than displacing them

Culture Policy and Licensing Taskforce
The taskforce will work with partners across the Council and public services to bring about a more permissive and open environment for formal and informal cultural activity across the city, including planning, licensing and policing. 
It will start by dealing with issues on a case-by-case basis, (eg, licensing for live music events), set in context of longer-term objectives:
Develop better relationships between cultural partners and business sector
Preparation, planning and delivery of a bid for UK City of Culture 2029
Co-ordinate support for UNESCO Creative Cities Bid
Establish Film Office to coordinate support for filming in the city
