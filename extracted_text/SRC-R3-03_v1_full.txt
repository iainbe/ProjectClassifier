Here's a structured summary and key insights from the workshop conversations, based on the transcript document:

🔑 Key Highlights
High value placed on bid writing guidance. Participants especially appreciated practical, “how-to” presentations, such as Vicente’s, which provided tangible advice on writing winning bids.
“Show me a winning bid… succinct advice… there’s a reason that went down well.” – Vicente
Strong ecosystem presence. Having key stakeholders like GMCA, Growth Hub, universities, and businesses in one room created a valuable platform for collaboration and mutual understanding.
“The strongest element… was the whole Manchester ecosystem working together.” – Vicente
Networking was a success, especially in allowing newer participants to connect with established figures and potential collaborators.
“It was great to grow my network… I loved being pushed out of my comfort zone.” – Adam Adamu
Practical follow-ups and slide sharing were appreciated and helped extend the value of the session post-event.
“Sharing the slides was useful… I shared them with my finance director.” – Michael Schmidt

⚠️ Key Lowlights
IP issues with universities were a recurring blocker. Many SMEs found university IP policies inflexible and poorly understood.
“The SME would have had to give up all the IP and license it back… for an SME, that is just, it’s ridiculous.” – Mandi Banks Gatenby
“They wanted to own all the IP… I’ve been on both sides… there’s a lot more nuance to that.” – Vicente
Presentation visuals and tech issues. Some slides were too small to be read and technical issues hampered delivery.
“Steph was one of the most interesting, however the projector refused to cooperate.” – Adam Adamu
“The visuals were rather too small… people behind us must have had even more problems.” – Michael Schmidt
Lack of tailored content for different experience levels. Some content was too basic for experienced attendees or poorly timed in their funding journey.
“The how-to was useful… but would have been more useful before we had a bid.” – Mandi Banks Gatenby

🧠 Insights by Category
🎓 Universities’ Role
Critical but unclear. Universities are vital partners but often move slowly, have complex internal processes, and are perceived as difficult to collaborate with, especially due to rigid IP rules.
Recommendations included more explicit guidance on engaging universities early, knowing who to contact, and demystifying red lines.
“Would a speaker from a university have helped? Yes – especially to demystify how IP and processes actually work.” – Vicente
“MMU seems more agile… UOM not self-aware of what it’s like for SMEs.” – Mandi Banks Gatenby

🧾 Presentations
Vincent’s session praised for clarity and practical tips.
Steph’s content strong but marred by tech issues.
Participants wanted more real-world case studies of successful bids, step-by-step journeys, and better visuals.
“Have clients talk about the whole process, from discovering the opportunity to making the application.” – Michael Schmidt

🤝 Networking
Highly valued for enabling connections between universities, SMEs, and innovation support bodies.
Participants desired a mix of sector-specific and stage-aligned networking, possibly with breakout groups.
“You could have done breakout groups with a focus on risk, partnering, etc.” – Mandi Banks Gatenby
“Put smaller companies with specialists in smaller companies… more hands-on.” – Michael Schmidt

📣 Marketing & Attendance
Low business turnout despite a good venue and intent. Attendees suggested:
Clearer communication of the event’s value
Inclusion of notable speakers to boost draw
Better segmentation by sector and stage
Sharing success stories in promotions
“You could easily have 20+ businesses… but people need an allure beyond what’s on the website.” – Vicente
“Academics don’t hear about these things… and we’re not targeting them, but still.” – Mandi Banks Gatenby

📩 Follow-Up and Action Items
Provide application review support post-session.
“Have one person designated to review applications before submission.” – Adam Adamu
Revisit IP-related concerns through facilitated discussions or myth-busting content.
Improve pre-session transparency and prep, such as:
Knowing who will attend
Bringing in-progress applications
Pre-session tasks
Consider format changes for future workshops:
Smaller breakout groups
Themed sessions based on business stage or sector
More practical, hands-on activities
“Working on a specific bid area in a breakout would be really valuable.” – Mandi Banks Gatenby

📌 Summary
The workshop was informative, collaborative, and practical, but it needs refining in areas like university engagement, marketing, and content tailoring. Participants want more hands-on support, peer examples, and clearer navigation through IP and governance barriers. Strengthening pre- and post-workshop support and segmenting content by experience level and sector could significantly boost impact.
