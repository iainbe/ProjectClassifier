NMP Horizons case studies: codex

Research conducted by The Audience Agency and post-graduate researchers from University of Plymouth under the direction of The Fifth Sector produced 20 case studies of projects featuring digital applications in a marine environment, which addressed key themes including:

	•	Conservation and sustainability of marine environments.
	•	Marine cultural heritage and the sustainable development agenda.
	•	Co-design and co-creation of audience experiences.
	•	Development of heritage skills in a marine environment.
	•	Digital engagement.
	•	Gaming and virtual experiences.

These projects shared several features:

	•	Behavioural change was a shared outcome of many of the successful projects – not always in the way original set out in the research.
	•	Governance: there is often a disconnect between the ‘owners’ of public (marine) spaces and the people that use (or should be) using them: this has an immense cost in lost economic, social and natural value.
	•	Good projects evolve and celebrate being in a continual state of change – R&D, prototyping and learning are all critical to engagement, whereas an attempt to produce overly ‘finished’ digital products can alienate audiences.

They also demonstrated six key benefits or opportunities. We have grouped the case studies under those six headings below. (TAA case studies are numbered in line with that document).

	•	Move from user-centred design to co-creating the project with local communities, ensuring that they have agency and decision-making power over what happens.

	•	8: Science Museum Community in Residence
	•	13: Old Weather
	•	14: Digitally Democratising Archives
	•	XL Caitlin Seaview Survey Project

	•	Everything is R&D. Innovation is at the core of the experience for communities and audiences as much as for researchers. Create a flexible digital framework which changes as your audiences engage and learn.

	•	2: Unpath’d Waters
	•	9: iMARE CULTURE

	•	Plan for sustainability before you start. Even better, associate yourself with and help to sustain an existing programme of work, rather than starting from scratch.
	•	2: Unpath’d Waters (part of Towards a National Collection)
	•	13: Old Weather (part of Zooniverse)

	•	Diversity and inclusion are inseparable from sustainability – it’s useful to think about how these agendas are linked through the UN’s Sustainable Development goals.

	•	Maritime Asia Heritage Survey Project 

	•	Digital is not always the solution – but it complements and binds together all other forms of engagement. Digital heritage requires authentic engagement with local and excluded communities, which also means making the offer accessible to all.
