## 1. Purpose and method

- **Brief:** update the 2016 Beatles economic and cultural impact assessment for the Beatles Legacy Group, and inform the linked feasibility question of a possible new Beatles visitor experience.
- **Constraint:** ~£10,000, desk-based, ~8 weeks. **No fieldwork budget**, so no neutral-location visitor surveys, and no direct measurement of visitor motivation.
- **Method:** improve the traceability and grading of stakeholder evidence; distinguish measured data (E1–E3) from verbal estimates and impressions (E4–E5); double-source material claims; report as an aggregated, anonymised whole.
- **Standing rule:** any updated headline figure is an *informed, outline estimate*, not a measurement, unless stronger primary evidence arrives.
- **Structural precedent adopted:** the AMION Eurovision 2023 evaluation (LCRCA-commissioned, same data stack — BID footfall, L1, card spend, STEAM, mobile data) is the city's own accepted template. The final report should mirror its executive-summary cascade table, its RAG-rated source table, its explicit disbenefits section, its two-stage design (desk-based stage now; measured-attribution stage later), and its case-study appendix. Register §M lists the seventeen borrowable assets and the one gap it exposes — AMION had 3,500 visitor surveys for attribution; we have none until the LCRDP data lands. Two assets deserve emphasis: AMION counted **non-attending visitors** (21% of Eurovision visitors came for the event but attended nothing ticketed — the accepted-method precedent for counting free-touchpoint Beatles visitors), and its mobile-phone data was **commissioned by LCRCA from Place Informatics** — if that licence is still live, it is a possible origin/motivation proxy without fieldwork (ask Wyatt, 16 Sept).

## 2. The 2016 baseline: now verified at source

The full 48-page report is held (`2016 Beatles Legacy report.pdf`). What it actually says:

| Component | Turnover | Jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Direct Beatles-specific activity | ~£39m | ~690 |
| Indirect | ~£15.64m | ~276 |
| Catalytic visitor spending | ~£155.3m | ~5,021 |
| **Gross** | **~£210m** | **~5,990** |
| **Net (after deadweight, leakage, displacement, multipliers)** | **£81.9m** | **2,335** |

Attribution assumptions used: **5% of day visitors and 20% of staying visitors** Beatles-motivated; ~30% leakage (per English Partnerships 2008 additionality guidance). Survey basis cited: 65% of overseas staying visitors and 43% of UK staying visitors named the Beatles as a key reason (LEP benchmarking, 2012 vintage).

**Internal tension to flag:** the report's conclusion claims "an estimated half of all international visitors come at least in part because of the Beatles", which is materially stronger than the 20% staying-visitor assumption actually modelled. Any update must take a defensible position rather than inherit the ambiguity.

The model should carry an explicit post-2028 uplift scenario, labelled as projection. The 21 May practitioner summit (~35–40 attendees, transcripts held) reached the same conclusion independently — its consensus was "a window between now and then to better organise ourselves".

## 4. The attribution problem: the binding constraint

The study's own finding is that no Beatles motivation dataset exists anywhere in the evidence base; every interview to date has been consistent with that (no stakeholder has produced one, and Gallagher confirmed he had repeatedly sought the trail analytics precisely because nothing else exists). Specifically:

- **Volume is measurable; motivation is not.** We can count attraction visitors, cruise excursionists, and footfall. We cannot yet say what share of city-wide visitor spend is Beatles-driven.
- **The Liverpool ONE figure needs care.** The ~23% crossover is (a) footfall, not spend; (b) a *geographic* proxy: people near Pier Head or Mathew Street are not necessarily Beatles-motivated (Mathew Street is a nightlife district; Pier Head is the waterfront); (c) unverified pending data.
- **Footfall growth ≠ spend growth.** L1's summer 2026: footfall +6–7%, spend flat, with visitors arriving in large groups, not all spending. Any model that scales spend off footfall inherits this error.
- **Beatles Week uplift cannot be isolated** at L1; the late-August back-to-school spike swamps it. Do not claim a Beatleweek retail effect.
- **Two assets could narrow the gap:** (i) Beatles Story booking-point data (origin + "how did you hear", promised ~20 Sept); (ii) LCRDP/Anna Rees on-site "reason for visiting" surveys run at Liverpool ONE, with raw data to be requested via Natalie Wyatt. If "Beatles" is a coded response option, this is the nearest thing to fieldwork evidence in existence.
- **The Spencer interview (14 Sept) inverts the value ranking the study has carried:** overnight coach-package visitors (Trafalgar/Globus/Contiki/Shearings-type) stay in hotels, dine, shop and pay for guiding — while cruise passengers eat on board and carry little spend ashore. The high-value axis is staying-vs-day, not cruise-vs-land; and the coach market has **no arrival count anywhere in the evidence base** — it is invisible to official data. First direct motivation answer of the study: her client base "come here specifically for the Beatles". MMT booking data (McDermott, 23 Sept) is the natural test. Register §L.
- **Spencer also supplies a necessary dissent** on the L1 crossover proxy: Mathew Street footfall includes weekend stag/hen traffic with no Beatles motivation — "half the people there couldn't name all four". The 23% crossover figure should carry this dilution caveat explicitly.
- **A third route may exist without fieldwork:** the Eurovision evaluation used Place Informatics mobile-phone data (commissioned by LCRCA) to isolate additional visitors from baseline footfall. If LCRDP still holds that licence, it could provide origin/dwell data around Beatles sites — to be raised at the 16 Sept Wyatt interview.
- **Free-touchpoint visitors are countable under the accepted method:** AMION's Eurovision evaluation included ~21% of visitors who came for the event but attended no ticketed event. The 2016 report's paid-attraction frame excluded the equivalent Beatles visitor; the update can include them with methodological cover, subject to the data above.

## 5. Overnight-visitor levies: context, not attribution

Two distinct mechanisms; do not conflate.

- **ABID Charge (current):** £2/room/night; city-centre BID footprint only; **£45,000 rateable-value threshold**; ~**85 levy-paying businesses**. Year-1 actual levy income **£1,630,637** vs the "£9.2m over two years" projection. **BID's own documents conflict; resolve before quoting**. £1.27m sat in reserves at year-end. Claimed effects (+9.4% occupancy when subvention occurs; +8% RevPAR) are BID-attributed, not independently verified.
- **Mayoral Overnight Visitor Levy (proposed):** announced 10 Sept 2026; government requires a **percentage of accommodation cost**, not a flat fee; region-wide incl. short-term lets/Airbnb; mayoral estimate ≤**£18m/yr**; expected post-Dec 2027 when ABID ends; **rate unannounced**; **no rateable-value threshold** in the framework. A small-provider threshold (nights-let / low-cost / low-turnover basis) was consulted on but its outcome is unconfirmed. National exemptions: temporary accommodation, shelters/refuges; local discretion for e.g. campsites.
- **Analytical relevance:** the ABID base misses the accommodation the Beatles market disproportionately uses (STLs, small independents, non-centre). The levy figures are **public-revenue estimates, not Beatles-attributable impact**; use as context only.
- Reference implementation: Edinburgh 5% of accommodation cost net of VAT, 5-night cap, all accommodation types (live July 2026). Glasgow 5% Jan 2027; Aberdeen 7% Apr 2027.

## 6. Feasibility-relevant findings

- **Capacity ceiling is real:** Beatles Story runs 60 per 15-minute slot; cruise growth cannot be absorbed within current hours; a larger/new site is in its five-year strategy. Demand pressure is corroborated, not hypothetical.
- **Commercial headroom is narrower than headline turnover suggests** [detail: §10.1 CONF].
- **A cautionary precedent exists:** the British Music Experience still trades but has never gained traction (listed-building signage limits, one-visit structure, no marketing budget) [detail: §10.2 CONF].
- **Rights-holder engagement is conditional:** the governing distinction is free/non-commercial versus paid use [detail: §10.3 CONF].
- **A credible new entrant and a rights-holder investment posture change exist simultaneously** [detail: §10.4 CONF: HIGHLY SENSITIVE; see also `260914 CONFIDENTIAL - Strategic Implications Note.md`].
- **Governance appetite is uneven:** operators' trust is improving post-summit, but civic and institutional support is oriented to large events rather than slow groundwork [detail: §10.5 CONF]. Refinement from the AMION read: the city already has event-delivery machinery (LVEN, Music Board, SAG/JAG documented in the Eurovision report) — the gap is not absent machinery but no standing coordination for a *permanent* draw.
- **Employment and GVA constants are now available on accepted terms** (register M15): STEAM's £96,781 visitor-spend-per-FTE, Experian's £38,120 GVA-per-FTE, and the 1.30/1.35/1.40 geography multipliers are what LCRCA accepted for Eurovision. If an updated figure is computed, using these constants is more defensible than the 2016 report's 2008-vintage additions.
- **A plausible archive model exists:** university digitisation plus museum front-end plus loans (cf. The Beat Goes On 2008, ~90% borrowed material). Constraints: estates may monetise privately as custodianship passes generations; parity problem ("I'm not doing it if they're not doing it").
- **Media-moment sensitivity is documented, not hypothetical** (summit, register K1): McCartney's "Dungeon Lane" album created a new pilgrimage site within weeks — guides already run tours there; Carpool Karaoke did the same for the Philharmonic; Harrison's childhood-home area is seeing unannounced walk-ups. The 2028 films will create pressure points faster than infrastructure adapts.
- **No gateway exists** (summit, K4): practitioners independently proposed a "Beatles Hub"; production companies have no directory or contact point into the city; Visit Liverpool is paid membership while Music City is free-upload. The coordination gap is practitioner-felt, not just our analysis.
- **Guide regulation is practitioner-demanded and has failed before** (summit, K3): council enforcement capacity was the blocker; room consensus for code of conduct, insurance, DBS. LCRCA's Sarah Lovell suggested the Destination Partnership as convenor — a live thread for the Wyatt interview.
- **Penny Lane has a failed-funding precedent** (summit, K2): Development Trust coach-parking plans died unfunded; the field is a council asset in administration; participants floated overnight-levy income as the funding route.

## 7. Caveats register: what must NOT be claimed

1. Do not quote **+64% footfall** without the like-for-like camera caveat.
2. Do not conflate the **ABID £2 charge** with the **mayoral percentage levy**; do not present the £45k RV threshold as applying to the mayoral levy; do not present the £18m as settled or Beatles-related.
3. Do not imply a **second cruise terminal exists**; it is planned and permit-dependent. The Isle of Man Ferry Terminal (2024) is a different facility.
4. Comparator attribution rates (Nashville 60%, Hamburg 15–20%, Austin 25%) remain **unverified**; illustrative only or drop. Memphis has a specified population but no located source.
5. The 2016 "half of international visitors" claim and the 5%/20% model assumptions are **in tension**; present the range honestly.
6. NT weekly/daily figures are **pixel-digitised estimates** constrained to exact card totals; label as `image_digitised_estimate`, never as measured.
7. ABID yield: **£1.63m actual vs £9.2m/2yr projection**. Unresolved conflict in BID's own documents.
8. LCRCA's own releases cite both **£6.8bn/55k jobs** and **£6.25bn/60m visitors**. Cite carefully, note vintage.
9. Cruise excursion volumes (~27,080) are a **self-declared ~85%-accurate estimate**; reconciliation against the ship-level schedule is outstanding.
10. Football/event cross-visitation is **observable but unproven**; treat as plausible spillover, not measured impact.
11. Stakeholder figures carry **commercial incentives** (attraction operators, cruise port, BID); grade accordingly.
12. **[CONF] items never appear in shared outputs.** The 4 September stakeholder interview (§10.4, implications note) carries extreme-caution handling: unattributed trend statements only in the report; no names, budgets, projects, or relationship detail may be quoted or paraphrased in any circulated document.
13. **Summit transcripts anonymise speakers** (Otter "Speaker N"). Do not attribute statements to named participants without a full-transcript read; the room was also self-selecting practitioners, not a representative sample.
14. **The 88.8m footfall figure is BID-scope only** (resolved via Williams, register J3): Liverpool ONE and Albert Dock feed a separate combined Springboard "Liverpool benchmark". Do not present 88.8m as citywide, and do not add L1's figures to it without checking for counter overlap.
15. **Geography splits carry a respondent-perception error**: AMION's own caveat — survey respondents do not know LA vs LCR boundaries. Any Liverpool/LCR split we present inherits this; footnote it (their convention: state what is and isn't adjusted whenever comparing figures across bases).
16. **Do not import AMION's additionality rates as ours**: their leakage 28% and displacement 20% are survey-derived for a nine-day event. They are the accepted-constant alternative to the 2016 report's 2008-vintage rates, but must be labelled as such, not treated as measured for the Beatles context.
17. **Employment figures in "one-year temporary FTEs"**: AMION's honest unit for event impacts. Any Beatles jobs figure should state whether it is permanent FTEs, one-year equivalents, or spend-supported employment — the units are not interchangeable.

## 8. Confidence classification (deepthink)

- **SUPPORTED:** all volume-growth directions; cruise doubling; NT ~2.7x; the absence of any motivation dataset; footfall≠spend divergence.
- **PLAUSIBLE:** retail spillover at meaningful scale (23% crossover, if verified); staying-visitor spend premium; Beatles excursion share of cruise activity.
- **UNKNOWN:** any replacement point figure; the mayoral levy rate and threshold outcome; Beatles share of city-wide spend.
- **UNSUPPORTED if claimed:** a Beatles-attributable city-wide retail figure; Beatleweek uplift; precise updated headline number.

## 10. Confidential annex: internal only, do not circulate

Items below were given in confidence or contain third-party commercial judgements. They inform strategy and feasibility reasoning only. **Never quote, paraphrase, or reference them in any shared output.**

- **10.1 Beatles Story margins** [Gallagher]: "turns over a lot of money but margins are wafer thin"; copyright and overhead costs invisible at headline level. Consistent with Di Glover's account of licensing costs and competitive pressure. Implication: high gross turnover is not commercial headroom; feasibility options should not assume the incumbent can fund expansion alone.
- **10.2 BME precedent** [Gallagher]: Harvey Goldsmith offered the British Music Experience to NML pre-pandemic; NML declined on the numbers. Implication: a national-museum rescue is not a fallback for a struggling attraction.
- **10.3 Apple engagement terms** [Gallagher]: NML received music/film rights free for the non-commercial "In the Town Where I Was Born" experience; Apple's posture is support for authentic storytelling but "don't want you riding on our coattails... when it comes to money, they want to make it." Implication: free vs paid status governs Apple cooperation; a commercial experience pays full licensing.
- **10.4 Rights-holder posture and competing project** [HIGHLY SENSITIVE]: the detailed picture now sits in `260914 CONFIDENTIAL - Strategic Implications Note.md` (primary interview, 4 Sept). In outline: rights-holder engagement with Liverpool has materially strengthened in the past year; a 2028 screen-release event is expected to drive a step-change in demand; and a credible new entrant could reshape the incumbent attraction economics. Gallagher's earlier remark that a confidential project "will probably blow the Beatles Story out of the water" is consistent with this picture. Implication: feasibility recommendations must plan for investment arriving faster than governance capacity, and for incumbents' economics to deteriorate further.
- **10.5 Governance judgements** [Gallagher]: Laura Pye is passive on BLG operationally (though active on visitor-economy infrastructure via ABID and the levy endorsement); Claire McColgan/Culture Liverpool favour tentpole events over groundwork; university archives operate a gatekeeper culture and the Beatles MA is under threat; Holly holds the Sean Lennon relationship. Implication: a neutral data compact is the obvious fix but needs a non-Culture-Liverpool host and a senior sponsor.
