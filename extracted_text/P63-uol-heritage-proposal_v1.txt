Approach and methodology
Our proposal reflects an approach and methodology developed over several years:
	•	I – Inception: establishing shared understanding of the project and agreement of milestones and outcomes
	•	D – Discovery: 
	•	Review the existing HSS Heritage CPD offer
	•	Provide evidence of credibility, affordability and value for money
	•	Identify the capacity required to deliver a module based CPD offer
	•	Map the size of the potential market for module based CPD across pre-agreed sectors
	•	Review other CPD delivery in University of Liverpool
	•	E - Engagement: Interviews and workshops with project partners and external stakeholders to :
	•	Generate insight as to what are the most relevant and marketable CPD opportunities are for agreed sectors
	•	Generate understanding as what is attractive to the agreed sectors in terms of duration, accreditation and cost, ensuring a commercially viable offer
	•	A – Analysis: submission of draft proposal and joint working with partners to refine the proposition, business case and financials
	•	Map and prioritise potential module content
	•	Benchmark the proposed CPD module offer against existing competitor provision
	•	R – Realisation: drafting and finalisation of the report i
	•	Draft report for review and comment
	•	Presentation to steering group to review and agree amendments
	•	Final report (Word and pdf document)

Relevant recent experience
Manchester Metropolitan University – School of Digital Arts (SODA) market demand study
The Fifth Sector was procured to carry out to test demand and come up with recommendations to  shape the type, and format of the industry support (continued professional development) for the initial business engagement phase of SODA, a £35m initiative to address convergence of creative and digital technology. The scope of the brief called for us to:
	•	Review existing market intelligence on the Digital, Creative and Media Sector, identify current skills gaps, training needs and key industry challenges. 
	•	Test and verify skills gaps, training needs and key industry challenges with key (pre agreed) industry forums and stakeholder groups.  
	•	Provide recommendations on course content and format by key subject area and sub sector.  
Although SODA primarily addresses needs of creative content and service provider businesses, the services had wider applications across a range of other industry sectors. In addition, the requirements of one of the main funders (Greater Manchester Combined Authority) required that SODA had to demonstrate how it was addressing inclusive growth and increasing diversity within the creative and digital workforce.
