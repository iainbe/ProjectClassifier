BRIEFING: WESTERN BALKANS CREATIVE ECONOMY MINISTERIAL MEETING
Classification: OFFICIAL
Date: 28 May 2024
Location: Kotor, Montenegro
Attendees: Culture Ministers from Western Balkans, UK Special Envoy, EU Commissioner, British Council Leadership

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Western Balkans Creative Economy Ministerial Meeting has successfully established a strategic framework for regional cooperation in cultural and creative industries. Key outcomes include a joint declaration on creative economy development, announcement of new British Council funding, and commitment to integrated policy approaches linking culture, tourism, and economic growth. The meeting demonstrated strong regional appetite for UK-led initiatives and EU alignment on creative sector development as a driver of youth employment and economic diversification.
Strategic Significance: This initiative positions the UK as a leading partner in Western Balkans development while advancing EU accession readiness through creative economy capacity building.

KEY OUTCOMES AND COMMITMENTS
Joint Declaration
Ministers endorsed a comprehensive declaration establishing:
	•	Regional creative economy cooperation framework
	•	Shared principles for cultural heritage monetisation
	•	Youth employment targets through creative sector development
	•	Digital innovation integration across cultural industries
Financial Commitments
	•	British Council Fund: New multi-year funding programme announced for creative economy capacity building
	•	World Bank Engagement: Confirmed technical assistance for youth employment initiatives
	•	EU Alignment: Commissioner Micallef confirmed continued EU support for regional creative sector development
Policy Integration
Ministers committed to:
	•	Embedding creative economy strategies within national economic plans
	•	Developing culture-tourism synergies for economic impact
	•	Establishing measurable impact frameworks for creative sector investment

STRATEGIC THEMES AND REGIONAL PRIORITIES
Economic Diversification
Strong consensus emerged on creative economy as a vehicle for economic diversification beyond traditional industries. Ministers presented compelling evidence of creative sector potential, with particular emphasis on:
	•	Successful financial support for growth: Johni Newbiggin OBE provided an overview of the origins and evolution of the Creative Economy and examples of successful interventions
	•	Film Industry development: Success stories from regional productions
	•	Festival economy: Dokufest case study demonstrating place-building and international cooperation
	•	Digital Creative skills: Screen Yorkshire model for youth employment and skills development
	•	Blue Economy: University of Plymouth’s experience of place-based regeneration of the UK’s National Marine Park
Cultural diplomacy and Identity
Ministers emphasised creative economy's role in:
	•	Promoting national identity through cultural exports
	•	Strengthening regional cooperation through shared cultural initiatives
	•	Building international partnerships through creative collaboration
Youth employment focus
Consistent priority across all presentations on creative sector's capacity to:
	•	Provide employment opportunities for young people
	•	Develop digital and creative skills aligned with modern economy needs
	•	Create pathways for youth retention in the region

UK STRATEGIC INTERESTS ADVANCED
Soft power 
	•	UK positioned as lead partner in regional creative economy development
	•	British Council's expanded role reinforces UK cultural influence
	•	Creative economy expertise showcases UK sectoral leadership
Economic opportunities
	•	Potential for UK creative industry partnerships and co-productions
	•	Skills development programmes creating UK-aligned talent pipeline
	•	Tourism and cultural exchange opportunities
Regional stability
	•	Creative economy development supports broader stability objectives
	•	Youth employment initiatives address migration pressures
	•	Cultural cooperation strengthens regional integration

RISK ASSESSMENT AND MITIGATION
Implementation Challenges
Risk 1: Varying capacity across regional partners for creative economy development
Mitigation: British Council technical assistance and phased implementation approach
Risk 2: Limited private sector engagement in creative industries
Mitigation: World Bank involvement in developing investment frameworks
Political Considerations
Risk 3: Potential for cultural initiatives to become politicised
Mitigation: Focus on economic outcomes and youth employment benefits

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FOLLOW-UP ACTION
Immediate (0-3 months)
	•	Finalise British Council funding parameters and launch application processes
	•	Establish working group for joint declaration implementation
	•	Coordinate with EU on complementary programming to avoid duplication
Medium-term (3-12 months)
	•	Develop monitoring framework for creative economy impact measurement
	•	Facilitate UK-Western Balkans creative sector partnerships through trade missions
	•	Support pilot projects demonstrating culture-tourism integration
Long-term (12+ months)
	•	Evaluate regional creative economy progress against baseline metrics
	•	Consider expansion of UK creative economy cooperation to other regions
	•	Integrate outcomes into broader Western Balkans partnership framework

CONCLUSION
The meeting successfully established the UK as the leading international partner for Western Balkans creative economy development. Strong ministerial engagement and concrete commitments provide a solid foundation for sustained cooperation. The initiative aligns with broader UK objectives of supporting regional stability, economic development, and EU integration while creating opportunities for UK creative industries. The combination of strategic vision, practical implementation mechanisms, and multi-stakeholder engagement positions this initiative for significant impact on regional development and UK influence in the Western Balkans.
