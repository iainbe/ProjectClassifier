
Executive summary
A Grant Funding Agreement (GFA) between Liverpool City Region Combined Authority and Liverpool City Council, dated 14 December 2018, granted £2 million in capital funding to support investment in film and television projects shooting in Liverpool City Region.
A further £200,000 in revenue funding was agreed to create 4 FTE posts within the Liverpool Film Office, providing capacity to extend the operation of LFO to all parts of LCR.
Subsequent to the GFA, and in response to the COVID-19 emergency which saw filming shut down completely between March and July 2020, a change control to the original Project was agreed to move up to £250,000 from the Production Fund into development funding for new projects with a strong LCR-based creative element.
Despite the absolute disruption to the sector imposed by the COVID-19 pandemic, and the reduction in capital available to invest in production which resulted from the creation of the Development Fund,  Liverpool City Region Production Fund (‘the Fund’) has met all its agreed outputs and exceeded many of them. 
Liverpool Film Office (LFO) has been extremely successful in leveraging the available capital to deliver increased levels of inward investment, higher levels of spend in Liverpool City Region (LCR) economy, increased employment (both new and safeguarded jobs), new training opportunities and improved terms for potential recoupment of capital deployed. The national and international profile of LCR as a filming location has been further raised and its competitive position in respect of other cities, regions and nations strengthened.
LFO has not only generated revenues from the operation of the Fund to sustain its operational costs, but also generated tens of thousands of pounds in fees for the six local authorities.
In operation, the Fund secured match investment from all major UK broadcasters (BBC, ITV and Channel 4). Projects in receipt of capital funding are on course to achieve higher levels of LCR spend than those targeted in the Investment Policy – a ratio of £6.73 for every £1 of funding, as against a target of 3:1 for independent films and 5:1 for high end TV. Qualifying productions also spend more money within LCR than non-funded productions as a proportion of their total budget. The six projects supported by the Fund have contributed 455 Indirect jobs, against a target of 280. In addition, the Development Fund has supported 17 LCR companies and safeguarded a further 43 indirect FTE jobs.
Current market conditions - including levels of excess demand for studio facilities for film and high-end television production in the UK – contain potential for continued capital investment to unleash a ‘virtuous circle’ of demand and growth. The strength of the film & TV sector has encouraged the Combined Authority and Liverpool City Council to invest in new capital infrastructure – the so-called ‘pop up’ studios. Recapitalisation of the Fund is one way to help maximise demand for and utilisation of these facilities, underpinning the return on capital already deployed and creating a demand pathway for the proposed development of permanent studio facilities on Edge Lane, adjacent to the Littlewoods Studios scheme. This would in turn provide sustainability and growth to the investors, employers, skilled workers and new entrants in production and production services across LCR.
LFO’s market engagement has identified that the most significant business opportunity for LCR comes from developments within the high-end TV drama market, with production budgets typically circa £1m+ per episode. In comparison with feature films, these productions also offer greatly increased prospects of further LCR spend from returning series. Demand from high-end TV has been accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic, which played a role in driving audiences for original content to both established broadcasters and to subscription streaming services, resulting in a burgeoning and highly competitive international TV drama sector. This  opportunity is fully aligned with the approved business case and would require no changes to the Project in order to deliver the anticipated economic, social and cultural benefits as set out in the Grant Funding Agreement (GFA).
Five out of six projects in receipt investment from the Fund have been incoming productions. The Development Fund has supported projects almost exclusively from LCR-based producers and talent, which form part of a ‘pipeline’ of projects seeking capital funding in the next two years. Recapitalisation would therefore support and accelerate development of indigenous production companies and cement benefits of this round of funding. 
Taking into account performance of the Fund to date, market opportunities identified by LFO and confirmed by independent industry sources and the anticipated success of the Development Fund in pump priming indigenous production, the recommendation of the interim evaluation is that recapitalisation of the Production Fund should be considered along with changes to its scale, duration and remit. This would provide a platform for sustainable and accelerated growth, including a greatly increased opportunity to project LCR as a cultural hub on a global stage and measurable economic and social benefits from increasing diversity and inclusion in the sector’s workforce. It would also underpin other investment in new capital facilities for the sector in the form of the ‘pop up’ studios.
Background
The LCR Film & Television Production Fund was launched in April 2020 to, 
‘Further develop the film, TV and related digital industries across the Liverpool City Region (LCR). It comprises two mutually supportive and interlinked elements:
Enhancement and expansion of the long-established and highly successful Liverpool Film Office (LFO) to deliver a service across the whole of the LCR promoting locations, facilities and services and delivering an improved and optimised service to external investors; and
Establishment of a Content Fund to attract high value projects to film in LCR, enable the targeted attraction of key production and facilities companies and improve opportunities for indigenous companies to secure work.’
Liverpool City Region Combined Authority (LCRCA) provided £2m in initial capital funding to support:
Investment in film and television projects shooting in Liverpool City Region 
Development funding for new projects with a strong LCR-based creative element
A further £200,000 in revenue funding within the same GFA has created 4 FTE posts to extend the operation of LFO to all parts of LCR.
Aims of the evaluation
The evaluation aims to capture the inputs, activities, outputs, outcomes and impacts for the capital funding elements of the programme to date. It focuses on the capital element of the Project but notes that the revenue outputs (4 FTE jobs) have been achieved. An economic impact assessment, which will form part of a final evaluation, will take place at a later date.

Approach and methodology
Defining the boundaries (objective and scope) of the Project, the interim evaluation looked to establish the economic and social value resulting from the funding. It includes:
A review of the Project to evaluate its performance against the Investment Policy and the outputs set out in the GFA, to
Attract new investment to LCR
Safeguard and create new jobs in film & TV production
Safeguard jobs in the wider economy (in particular, the visitor economy)
Create new opportunities for the LCR film and television supply chain 
Scenarios for recoupment of some or all of the capital deployed through sales revenues and a share of net profits
Initial appraisal of equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI) outcomes
Evaluation of the impact of the Development Fund, which resulted from a change control to the initial GFA in response to the COVID-19 emergency, in stimulating new investment from LCR-based production companies
Analysis of pipeline projects and horizon scanning of future opportunities to inform up to three scenarios for future investment
The final version of this report will include case studies of successful applicants and production service providers to provide a level of qualitative assurance to the quantitative research findings.
The final report will also include:
Assurance of selection process: number and source of applications; identification of panel members; shortlisting and selection of projects
Interviews with key stakeholders: all relevant actors will be identified who either will be affected by the activities within the scope or who influence the project (either positively or negatively)
A ‘counterfactual’ case study of a Qualifying Production whose application for funding was rejected by the assessment panel
Benchmarking the success of the Project against a case study of another UK region

1.2 Indirect benefit
Working in partnership Marketing Liverpool’s Convention Bureau, LFO offers an accommodation introduction service to productions. This has been crucial in supporting the hotel sector during the pandemic. Marketing Liverpool reported that the ADR (average daily rate) and REVPAR (revenue per average room) increased by 5.2% in the Autumn, directly attributed to film & TV production. Film & TV related-business in 2020 was worth £2.1m and generated 25,000 bed nights, thus safeguarding hundreds of local jobs.
LFO’s business support for film & TV clients searching for ancillary studio and office space has also made a significant positive impact on the local economy. Looking at the whole of 2020, the LFO team generated almost £225,000 of revenue for both public and private sector from LCR-based studio and office accommodation. New strategic commercial opportunities expected to crystallise around new productions in early 2021 include Arnold Clark, Liverpool Innovation Park and the M&S Arena.
The Fund is self-financing via the management fee it charges productions on its investments. The Fund leverages further (free) expertise and relationships into the Film Office via the Fund Advisor, external industry assessors and panel and the partners on its investments (ie: BBC, ITV, C4, CBBC, production companies, Screenskills etc).  As a result, the role of the LFO has moved from provider to partner.
The productions supported by the Fund and the additional 250 film & TV projects shot in Liverpool in 2020 brought £424k in fees into LCC and LCR via locations, accommodation, paid for council services etc. The more production is attracted to LCR, the more income is generated.
Not yet calculated is the additional indirect benefit of film and television tourism to LCR, both as the primary reason to visit and to reinforce the existing cultural destination offer:
‘For locations, destination marketing organisations (DMOs), and local and regional authorities, these productions do not just bring spend during production but the potential for tourism spending that could continue long after the cameras have been packed away’. 

1.3	Recoupment
The Fund’s Investment Policy targets recoupment of 30% of capital deployed from a negotiated share of sales revenues and net profits from the productions in which it invests. If achieved, recoupment:
Increases ROI and improves VFM for the initial public investment
Provides capital to be reinvested in new productions, or in the further development of the sector in LCR
All applications for funding needed to include a detailed report on the sales and distribution plan of the finished production and an offer to LFO of its share of sales revenues and net profits (if any). 
One of the notable successes of LFO’s management of the Fund has been its ability to improve, through negotiation, its recoupment position within individual projects. In four out of the six investments made to date, the Fund is in first or joint first position (pari passu with the distributor, or with a defined ‘corridor’) for recoupment from international programme sales. In addition, LFO has negotiated a share of net profits of between 4% and 10% in perpetuity - payments which are made only once all the investors have recouped their initial investment in full.
All six of the productions are for UK broadcasters who have pre-purchased the right to premiere the programme in the UK; sales to other territories, therefore, follow the first UK broadcast, meaning that revenue can take some time to come through. The recoupment profile of the Fund takes into account the current production, delivery, UK broadcast and distribution cycle of the six projects, with substantive revenues unlikely to be received before end of 2022.  The tables below summarise the projected overall recoupment using the sales estimates for each production provided by the relevant distributor. Distributors typically provide a spread of  ‘base’, ‘mid’ and ‘high’ estimates that are reflective of both known and unknown market conditions.

1.4	Development Fund
The Development Fund was established in April 2020 as an ‘agile’ response to the COVID-19 emergency. A change to the GFA was agreed to set aside £250,000 of the capital fund in the form of development investments to LCR-based producers and writers. This addressed the reality that, until DCMS agreed COVID-safe protocols for recommencement of filming in Summer 2020, the sector was completely shut down and those companies and individuals had no other source of income to support jobs or provide development capital for new projects. The Development Fund continued to attract interest until the scheme was closed at the end of December attracting a total of 64 applications.  
The Development Fund and its administration has allowed LFO to begin to shape some strategic and potentially significant new collaborations between LCR-based producers and writers.

1.6	Process evaluation
All eligible applications were assessed by the Fund Advisor on behalf of the Fund Manager and in accordance with published Guidelines for the Fund. 
Each assessment considered, inter alia:
The extent to which the project responds to the strategic priorities of the Fund including the likely economic and cultural benefits to the LCR
The creative merits/strengths of the project
The abilities and track record of the core team (director, producer, writer) and any identified partners, and their creative, technical and managerial capacity to deliver the project
The viability of the proposed budget, schedule and finance plan
The market potential of the project, and the likelihood of recouping the Fund’s investment and generating a financial return
The availability and levels of private sector/other public sector investment leveraged by the Fund’s proposed investment, together with the terms and conditions of such
The extent to which the project makes a demonstrable contribution to greater diversity
The degree to which the project would be a distinctive addition to the Fund’s balanced investment portfolio
All assessments will include necessary corporate, financial and State aid due diligence including:
Certificate of incorporation, Mem and Arts, credit check on the legal entity, last 2 years of audited accounts (if available) in order to confirm eligibility and financial viability of applicant
Full analysis of State aid received from both private and public sources (including any EIS, CVS or VCT) in order to ensure compliance with GBER levels;
A search for disqualified company directors
Full analysis of the estimated LCR production and employment spend by the project
A full risk assessment and analysis of likely recoupment/returns
Applications from seven qualifying productions were evaluated by a panel consisting of a mix of LCR stakeholders and industry experts.
The process seems to have been robust, as all six of the QPs approved for funding have gone into production. Further qualitative research will be carried out to understand whether there are any improvements that could be carried out should the Project be recapitalised.
2.	Horizon scanning and gap analysis
2.1	Pipeline
The Fund continues to generate increased interest from producers. Analysis of pipeline projects and horizon scanning of future opportunities (see Figure 7) shows that:
There is potential demand for more than £6m in capital funding to leverage £35m of LCR spend from 14 productions
7 of these projects have been seeded by the Development Fund
Those projects from LCR-based producers are forecasting that investment from the Fund would generate more than twice the leverage (measured by spend within LCR) of incoming productions (7.8:1 versus 3.7:1)
2.2	New market opportunities
Operation of the first round of the Fund highlighted the importance of having a grant offer in attracting opportunities within the burgeoning  streaming and pay TV sectors (eg, Netflix, Sky Atlantic, Amazon, Apple TV+, Disney+), whose business model does not a fit with a third party equity investment. Additional support towards select large scale productions over a 3-5 year period would align with upcoming infrastructural developments, such as the ‘pop up’ studios and the Littlewoods studio facility, whilst being fully aligned with the approved business case in delivering the anticipated economic, social and cultural benefits of public intervention.
A pilot for a grant-funded project along those lines – Sexy Beast - received change control approval in 2020 but the project did not go ahead for other reasons. The investment profile of the producers’ submissions suggested that the grant funding would have realised significantly greater return on capital deployed, in the form of indirect outputs in jobs, production spend, hotel spend, fees etc, than the average for this round of the Production Fund - an investment ratio of 10:1, as opposed to an average of 6.76:1 in QPs funded to date. To give this further context, , the highest investment ratio achieved by any single Fund investment to date was just over 8:1.

2.3	Future additionality
In response to increased demand for studio facilities to complement LCR’s strong offer, a proposal for two new temporary pop up studios was approved by LCC’s Cabinet on 9th October with full planning consent received early December 2020. Groundworks commenced in December and the scheme is expected to be complete in late spring 2021. The pop ups will be owned by LCC and managed through the LFO. The press announcement and subsequent coverage has generated interest from the film & TV sector as studio space still continues to be at great demand due to the ongoing protocols for global film & TV production during pandemic.
Provision of ‘pop up’ studio facilities will:
Help reduce ‘leakage’ of production spend to permanent facilities outside LCR for those productions using LCR as a location
Allow LFO to attract more, and a wider range of productions for which the ability to have a single production base throughout pre-production and principal photography is important both in financial and creative terms
Provide LCR-based crew, performers, producers and writers with increased employment opportunities within the city region
Encourage LCR-based producers and writers to develop material which can take advantage of the studio facilities, making for a more diverse range of output and potential market opportunities for ‘home grown’ content
Help signal demand for and stimulate investment in existing and new production services in LCR
Allow LCR to meet demand for studio space for both feature films and high-end TV series, presenting more opportunities to attract ‘returning’ series which deliver sustained demand and proportionately higher returns for the region
2.4	Recovery, sustainability and growth of LCR’s content production and production services sectors
Continuation and potential expansion of the fund would also provide the opportunity to develop LCR’s film & TV production ecology in other ways, all of them important for the future sustainability and growth of the sector:
Addressing gaps in production services (including supply of motor vehicles for film & TV; specialist lighting services; on-set catering; and post-production services)
Lack of this capacity in LCR results in leakage of potential employment and social benefits on existing productions. Although there are local companies with the capability to expand to meet these requirements, and other companies which might potentially provide inward investment to create new LCR-based ventures, without greater certainty around future levels of demand it is unlikely that these investments will crystallise, particularly post-COVID-19 when businesses may have to utilise more of their available cash and any reserves as working capital to address impact of the pandemic.2.5	Increasing diversity and inclusion of the sector workforce
More work is needed to address imbalances and inequalities in the film and television workforce
Developing higher-level skills, in particular in production and script editing but also at ‘Head of Department’ (HOD) grades in all related craft skills, to give LFO more leverage in insisting on local recruitment at these grades as part of the funding package, and offering incoming productions greater confidence in their ability to find a larger proportion of crew (and therefore, to spend more of the production budget) within LCR
Sustaining and increasing the number of trainee and entry level opportunities in productions, supporting retention of graduates from the city region’s universities and specialist training providers and providing clear skills pathways for young people and those looking to move career to exploit opportunities within content production sectors

2.6	Risks of failing to manage demand
The Fund has resulted in a clear increase in interest in LCR as a production destination, particularly for high-end TV and returning series which generate sustainable and long-term benefit for the city region. Analysis of those projects in already in receipt of investment, and those which have expressed interest in the Fund, illustrates that:
The investment ratio achieved was in many cases higher than that proposed in the original expression of interest, due to LFO’s increased ability to leverage the value of the Fund in negotiation with producers to obtain better terms in terms of LCR spend, jobs, trainees and recoupment
There is a strong ‘pipeline’ of enquiries about funding which would generate a sustainable flow of new investment to LCR, and with it, opportunities to recover, strengthen and grow the film and television sector

3.	Scenario planning
3.1	Counterfactual – ‘do nothing’
This scenario assumes that there is no new capital investment available from April 2021. In that case:
Recoupment of investment from the first round of the Fund would not be sufficient nor predictable enough to be able to offer continued incentives for filming in LCR
Return on existing productions with a returning element may be sub-optimal should producers choose to reduce levels of LCR spend, or relocate entirely, in the absence of follow-on Production Fund investment
The opportunity to follow through on Development Fund investments by being in a position to offer equity funding to LCR-based producers to help secure other investment will be lost
Opportunities to secure a larger share of the growing international market for high-end TV will be lost, and with it opportunities to leverage further capital investment in the ‘pop up’ studios and Littlewoods in a way that would add to the sustainability and profitability of those ventures
Competition from other cities, regions and nations looking to exploit the current high levels of demand for facilities for film and television may mean that growth of the sector in LCR is significantly curtailed
3.2	Recapitalise the fund for a further £2m
A prompt decision on recapitalisation of the Fund for a further two years would allow LFO to retain its existing operational structure and to take advantage of proven existing demand, which includes a pipeline of new productions from indigenous producers stimulated by Development Fund investment. In the ‘mid case’ scenario for recoupment, that would also allow for the first receipts from sales to be used to increase the total amount of funding available for new and continuing productions, although it is unlikely that substantial recoupment flows could be harnessed in this way before 2023.
The risk here is that the Fund’s available capital would be exhausted before the end of the funding period – the interruption to production occasioned by COVID-19 represents a distortion of the expected investment profile for the current Fund. That may have a number of adverse and unintended consequences, including:
Accentuating shortages in key skills and production services capacity as investors look to benefit from short-term availability of fund before capacity has been developed to meet that demand
Reduced levels of demand from inward productions just at the point that studios capacity becomes available
Loss of opportunity to strengthen LCR’s production base and bring forward a strong slate of new projects from indigenous producers and writers, supported by seed capital from the Development Fund
Reduced levels of production spend and reduced levels of fees to LFO, LCC and other local authorities
A ‘stop-start’ process of investment further undermining confidence of investors and employees within LCR, incurring the risk of an outflow of talent developed under the current Fund and undermining attempts to secure employment in the sector
Lack of confidence in future investment and employment opportunities making it still harder to increase diversity within the LCR film and television sector
3.3	Recapitalise and expand the Production Fund 
There is a significant opportunity to build upon and multiply the benefits of investment in the Production Fund by:
Increasing the capital funding on offer to £4 million over two years
Extending the length of the project 
Including an automatic option for reinvestment on condition that KPIs around demand, outputs, return on investment and recoupment are met
Including an option to offer grant funding (on a case-by-case basis) to attract ‘marquee’ film and ultra- high-end TV productions
Providing investment for a further iteration of the Development Fund, coterminous with the Production Fund
Increasing coordination between LFO, employers, the Growth Platform and business support and skills brokers in the six local authorities to determine clear skills pathways for creative, business, financial, craft and production services skills (including digital) for new entrants to the sector and identify resource for skills and continuing professional development within existing budgets, and signposting through LFO to the capability to deliver that within both public and private sector
