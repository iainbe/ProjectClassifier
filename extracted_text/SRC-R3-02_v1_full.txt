Strategic Analysis: Greater Manchester R&D Funding Process Optimisation
This analysis builds on feedback from the initial “From Good To Great” workshop held at Man Met in May 2025. It complements direct feedback with a comprehensive business process analysis that positions GM Combined Authority to argue for devolved funding variations as a better way to improve outcomes.
Current State vs. Desired State 
Underlying Process Gaps 
The workshop feedback revealed three critical systemic issues that mask deeper structural problems:
1. Pre-Qualification Gap
Current: SMEs enter funding competitions without adequate business case validation
Impact: High failure rates, wasted resources, poor ROI on ecosystem support
Evidence: "The how-to was useful... but would have been more useful before we had a bid"
2. Strategic Alignment Gap
Current: Irregular, seemingly subjective call timing disconnected from regional priorities
Impact: Missed opportunities, reactive rather than proactive innovation pipeline
Evidence: Participants struggling with timing and relevance of available funding
3. Supply Chain Investment Gap
Current: Large companies limited to 50% cost recovery, creating collaboration barriers
Impact: Reduced private sector leverage, weakened innovation ecosystems
Evidence: Implicit in university-SME IP collaboration challenges

Business case for GMCA to vary delivery
These recommendations are based on the evidence of increasing devolution of authority for R&D investment and build on the existing MOU between GMCA and Innovate UK.
1. Pre-Qualification Service Model
Proposed Variation: GM-operated pre-qualification gateway
Rationale: Improvement in success rates through validated business cases
Evidence: Workshop feedback on timing and preparation gaps
ROI: Reduced wasted applications, higher quality submissions, better ecosystem ROI
2. Strategic Call Programming
Proposed Variation: Regular (quarterly/six-monthy) themed calls aligned to GM strategic priorities
Rationale: Predictable pipeline supports business planning and collaboration
Evidence: Current irregular timing creates missed opportunities
Impact: Increase in application volume through improved visibility and planning
3. Enhanced Collaboration Terms
Proposed Variation: 
Large company cost recovery increased (to 65%-75%) for GM ecosystem projects to support development of supply chain related propositions
Flexible IP frameworks for university-SME partnerships (which assume SME ownership of IP is in ‘first position’ and University ownership of IP relates normally only to direct spin-outs)
Rationale: Addresses supply chain investment barriers and university collaboration friction
Evidence: "SME would have had to give up all the IP and license it back... ridiculous"
Impact: Unlocks private sector co-investment, strengthens innovation clusters
Implementation Framework
Phase 1: Pilot Programme (6 months)
Advanced Manufacturing and Materials focus
20 SMEs through pre-qualification process
Baseline metrics: application success rates, time-to-funding, collaboration formation
Phase 2: Sector Expansion (12 months)
Include Life Sciences and Green Tech
University IP framework pilots
Supply chain collaboration incentives
Phase 3: Full Devolution Case (18 months)
Performance data collated to make case for full devolved authority
Demonstrated ROI and ecosystem impact
Scalable model for other Combined Authorities

Negotiation Strategy with Innovate UK
Value Proposition: GM as innovation funding laboratory
Risk Mitigation: Pilot approach with clear success metrics
Scalability: Model for other Combined Authorities
Efficiency Gains: Reduced administrative burden on Innovate UK through local pre-qualification
Evidence Base: 
Workshop feedback indicated that both SMEs and larger companies would benefit from some or all of these variations, supported by ecosystem stakeholder alignment (GMCA, Growth Hub, universities, businesses all present and engaged). However, as we were not briefed to ask for direct feedback on potential changes to current pipeline and onboarding processes, the companies’ expression of areas for improvement was limited.
This framework positions GM Combined Authority to argue for meaningful devolved variations that address the real underlying process issues while building a compelling case for expanded local control over R&D funding strategy.
