From Good to Great: Innovation Funding Support Session
Summary
The innovation funding workshop demonstrated significant potential as a collaborative platform, bringing together key stakeholders from the Manchester ecosystem including GMCA, Growth Hub, universities, and businesses. While participants valued the practical guidance and networking opportunities, several structural challenges emerged that require attention to maximize future impact.
Key Strengths and Successes
Practical content delivery
The workshop's greatest strength lay in its practical approach to bid writing guidance. Participants consistently highlighted the value of tangible, actionable advice over theoretical presentations. One attendee noted that they particularly appreciated "succinct advice" and understanding, "there's a reason that went down well" when discussing successful bid examples. This practical focus resonated strongly with businesses seeking immediate, applicable insights.
Ecosystem collaboration
The event successfully created a collaborative environment. The convergence of universities, businesses, and support organisations in a single forum provided valuable opportunities for cross-sector understanding and partnership development.
As one participant observed, 
"The strongest element was the whole Manchester ecosystem working together." 
Networking impact
The networking component proved highly effective, particularly for newer participants seeking to expand their professional connections. One attendee expressed appreciation for being, "pushed out of my comfort zone," while growing their network.
Post-event value
The sharing of presentation materials extended the workshop's value beyond the session itself. Participants found the slide sharing "useful" and actively distributed materials within their organisations, demonstrating the content's practical applicability and ongoing relevance.
Critical challenges 
Intellectual Property barriers
University IP policies were identified as a  barrier to university-business collaboration. Several attendees described scenarios where, "the SME would have had to give up all the IP and license it back," with one noting that "for an SME, that is just ridiculous.".
The complexity of IP negotiations was further highlighted by participants who had experience on both sides of these discussions, noting "there's a lot more nuance to that" than typically understood by either party.
Supply chain issues
The representative of a larger firm clearly represented the structural difficulties faced in engaging SMEs within their supply chain. The lower level of grant of on offer (50%) meant that this firm would lose money on every bid, making it very hard to make a business case for the investment; whilst the expectation that an SME could shoulder the responsibility of leading a bid in which they were a junior partner was seen as unrealistic.
Content timing and relevance
Some participants questioned the relevance of the timing of the session relative to their funding journeys. While the practical guidance was appreciated, some attendees found it would have been, "more useful before we had a bid," suggesting the need for better alignment between content delivery and participant readiness.
Technical and presentation issues
Technical challenges undermined content delivery. Presentation visuals were considered "too small" to be read effectively, with attendees noting that "people behind us must have had even more problems." Difficulties with projection equipment meant that some of the most valuable content only partially delivered, with one participant observing that while a presenter "was one of the most interesting, however the projector refused to cooperate."
Sectoral analysis
University engagement 
Universities emerged as critical but problematic partners in the innovation ecosystem. While their involvement is seen as essential, they are perceived as slow-moving organisations with complex internal processes that are difficult for SMEs to navigate. Participants suggested that universities, particularly larger institutions, lack "self-awareness of what it's like for SMEs," while smaller institutions appear "more agile" in their approach.
The need for clearer guidance on university engagement was clearly articulated, with participants seeking clarity on "who to contact" and how to navigate institutional processes effectively.
Business participation and marketing
Despite the quality of content and venue, business attendance remained disappointingly low. Participants identified several factors contributing to this challenge, including unclear communication of the event's value proposition and insufficient "allure beyond what's on the website." The consensus was that with improved marketing and clearer value communication, attendance could "easily have 20+ businesses."
Next steps
Structural Improvements
Future workshops should consider incorporating smaller breakout groups focused on specific themes such as risk management, partnering strategies, or sector-specific challenges. Participants suggested that."working on a specific bid area in a breakout would be really valuable," indicating demand for more targeted, hands-on activities.
Content enhancement
Real-world case studies featuring "clients talking about the whole process, from discovering the opportunity to making the application," were highly regarded by all attendees. Developing this approach would provide participants with comprehensive journey mapping rather than isolated advice points.
Post-event support 
Post-workshop support emerged as a critical need, with participants requesting, "one person designated to review applications before submission." This leads to two suggested improvements:
A “pre-qualification’ stage during which businesses could review ideas and get pointers for universities and business support partners 
An opportunity to extend the workshop's value through ongoing advisory services.
IP 
The persistent IP challenges require dedicated attention, potentially through facilitated discussions or myth-busting sessions specifically designed to address university-business collaboration barriers.
Strategic considerations
Pre-session preparation
Future workshops would benefit from enhanced pre-session transparency, including advance knowledge of attendee profiles and the opportunity for participants to bring in-progress applications for discussion. This preparation would enable more targeted and relevant content delivery.
Segmentation approach
Workshop content should be segmented by both experience level and business stage, ensuring that material is appropriately challenging and relevant for different participant groups. This would address the current mismatch between relative complexity of some of the content and different participant needs.
Marketing and positioning
The marketing approach for future workshops needs to better communicate value propositions and attract appropriate participants. This includes highlighting success stories and featuring notable speakers to enhance the event's perceived value and draw.
Conclusion
The workshop's success in bringing together diverse stakeholders provides a solid foundation for development, particularly in its practical approach and opportunities to develop collaboration across sectors and along supply chains.
However, if it is to realise its full potential as a well to drive applications and enhance their quality, the issues around perceived IP barriers and the difficulty of engaging larger companies within a supply chain need specific focus.
With focused improvements in these areas, combined with enhanced pre- and post-workshop support, future sessions could significantly increase their impact on the regional innovation ecosystem and better serve the complex needs of businesses navigating the innovation funding landscape.
