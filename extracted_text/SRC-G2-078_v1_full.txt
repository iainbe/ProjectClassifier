BEATLES TOURISM IMPACT STUDY – SUMMARY PROPOSAL
Prepared for: Beatles Legacy Group  Prepared by: The Fifth Sector  Date: April 2026
1. OBJECTIVE
The 2016 study said Beatles tourism generated £81.9m and 2,335 jobs. That relied on 2012 visitor survey data - now 14 years old - to establish attribution percentages:
30% of international overnight visitors
20% of domestic overnight visitors 
5% of day visitors
The critical issues are to distinguish Beatles tourists from people who would have come to Liverpool anyway; and to identify opportunities to generate additional revenue from Beatles-related attractions from both “intentional” Beatles visitors and those who experience some part of the Beatles-related visitor economy as part of a visit initiated for another reason. Get those wrong and the whole economic impact and attribution model falls apart.
Since 2014 there are new attractions (Mathew Street Beatles Museum, Strawberry Fields, British Music Experience), growing international markets, and stakeholders thought the 2016 figures were too low. With the Sgt Pepper anniversary (2027) and Sam Mendes’ films (2028) coming, Liverpool needs updated figures that will stand up to scrutiny.

3. INTERNATIONAL BENCHMARKS
Memphis, with its single artist heritage, is the closest comparison. Elvis/Graceland accounts for 35-40% of overall visitor numbers, up to 50%+ for international visitors. Liverpool has a stronger global Beatles brand and more Beatles attractions. The 2016 Beatles study’s assumption of 30% international attribution looks conservative.
4. WHAT THE FIELD TEAM ADDS
A. Higher attribution rates = higher economic impact
Stakeholders estimate conservatively. Visitors tell you what they actually did.
International overnight visitor spend - comparator data:
Liverpool's £402 is a blended average across domestic and international, day and overnight visitors. Memphis and Nashville data suggest Beatles-motivated international visitors likely spend significantly above this average. Field studies would help establish if that is the case and provide more accurate measures of impact.
B. Spatial evidence would show if Beatles tourists are distinct
What mobile phone data analysis would reveal:
Do Beatles visitors follow a distinct circuit (e.g., Beatles Story → Cavern → Mathew Street → Strawberry Fields → National Trust homes)?
How long do they spend in Cavern Quarter vs general tourists?
Do they visit outlier locations (like Strawberry Fields/Mendips/Forthlin Road) that general tourists skip?
What's the footfall difference in Mathew Street during International Beatleweek vs normal periods?
Why this matters:
If mobile phone data shows Beatles visitors have distinct movement patterns and concentrate around Beatles sites, it proves they're a separate market segment, not general tourists who happen to visit Beatles attractions. This validates high attribution rates.
Example from Memphis: Graceland visitors show distinct movement patterns - 78% visit Graceland as their primary destination, with limited movement to other Memphis attractions. This supports high attribution rates.
For Liverpool: Field surveys combined with spatial analysis would show whether Beatles tourists behave like Memphis music tourists (distinct circuit, primary motivation) or general tourists (Beatles sites as one of many stops).
This proves Beatles tourists are a distinct market, not general tourists who happen to visit Beatles sites.
C. Business surveys validate visitor data
What businesses tell you:
Hotels:
"Beatles Week bookings are 85% international vs 40% normally"
"Average Beatles tourist spends £45 more per night on F&B"
Restaurants (Cavern Quarter):
"60% of evening trade is Beatles tourists"
"Beatles tourists spend £32 average vs £24 general diners"
Retailers:
"Beatles merchandise is 75% of turnover"
"We source 55% locally" (validates/refines 40% assumption)
Supply-side validation of visitor survey findings with actual spend data.
D. Defensible for investors
Apple's question: "How do you know Beatles tourism is worth £X million?"
Option A: "We interviewed 20 stakeholders who estimate 40% of international visitors come for Beatles."
Option B: "We surveyed 400 visitors. 47% of international visitors said they wouldn't have visited without Beatles attractions. Mobile phone data shows 65% follow a distinct Beatles circuit. Business surveys confirm 38% of Cavern Quarter trade is Beatles-driven."
Which gets the investment?
E. Replicable for 2028 post-films assessment
2026 baseline (Option B):
Visitor survey instrument established
Spatial analysis methodology proven
Business survey framework tested
2028 post-films:
Repeat identical surveys
Measure actual impact of Mendes films
Calculate: "Beatles tourism increased from £X m to £Y m - a £Z m uplift attributable to the films"
Option A doesn't give you this - no baseline methodology to repeat.
F. International credibility
Memphis: Uses same counterfactual methodology, achieves 35-40% with weaker brand than Beatles - validates Liverpool's approach
Nashville: Annual surveys show attribution grew from 45% (2005) to 60% (2019) - shows what's possible
Hamburg: Lower attribution (15-20%) because Beatles compete with other offers - validates Liverpool's higher rates
For international investors, this benchmarking matters.
