Competitor analysis

8

Aim: To scope heritage-related activity at HEI’s identified by colleagues as being competitors and/or collaborators in our areas of strength to see where we can exploit gaps and add value

Core group of 8 HEIs: Hull, Leicester, Durham, Manchester, UCL, York, Leeds and Cambridge

Desk-Based Analysis of UK HEI Competitors

Competitor/collaborator is critical, as networking and collaboration will be critical to future success, particularly in areas such as GCRF and large RCUK cross-Council grants (anticipating UKRI). 8 HEIs suggested by Lin; based on a light-touch review of research at Liverpool; and on information flowing from previous Heritage events –networking in November, space, place, memory and landscape in January, international focus in March. Also includes digital as a key cross-cutting area. Stress that the competitor analysis is a living document that will be updated as the theme develops.

MedDive in the Past –

<http://meddiveinthepast.eu/welcome>

<https://www.interregeurope.eu/good-practices/enhancing-cultural-heritage-with-a-new-perspective-through-bluemed-the-underwater-museums>

used digital applications to support remote VR visits and AR supported dive visits supporting awareness, action and economic growth through tourism as well as archaeological research and conservation.

KAC and UM are linked collectively together with wider sites through a shared web-site and providing services for divers and access to dedicated locally provided services for all visitors. The network has remained financially durable post-project.

MedDive was established within the wider work of the BlueMED EU H2020 project 2016-2020 € 3M. Provided a delivery framework and shared vision for a more healthy, productive, resilient, better known and valued Mediterranean Sea, promoting the citizens’ social well-being and prosperity, now and for future generations, and boosting economic growth and jobs.

The work of BlueMED now continues within the current EC’s Sustainable Blue Economy Fund ( first call 2023).

Key Points

Digital applications (from social to volumetrics and beyond), integrate within a wider delivery framework focused on sustainable development goals, key audiences and their needs. Digital capability not an objective in itself – application designed with physical delivery in support of overall service objectives.

Digital use opens up key features for each audience/ interest e.g - Terrestrial VR and general awareness; Submarine AR enhanced physical experience and responsible use; Conservation audience reach and innovation; Local Economic opportunities; Policy contribution to delivery of UNESCO Convention

PNMP Application guides

An example of project level contribution to ongoing sustainable development.

Digital enabled the project to address everyone and provide services inclusively.

Created a digital platform supporting local and international collaboration in marine conservation and heritage use. Acts as an exemplar for wider Med.

Championed new methods of engaging new and existing audiences through application of novel technology as a driver of innovation in sustainable use of maritime culture and natural heritage.

Project design both helped different audiences unlock an array of underwater exploration and see the previously unseen.

What remains to be developed are mechanisms and digital operations that….

Establish ongoing financial durability and effective project/ programme governance.

Further project management and innovation attuned to both local and wider infrastructure, social and sustainable development as critically an evolving set of fresh, relevant experiences.
