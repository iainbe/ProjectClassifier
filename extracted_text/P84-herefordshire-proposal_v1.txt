Rushmoor Cultural Strategy (2022/23)
The Fifth Sector was commissioned by Rushmoor Borough Council and ACE South West to develop a Cultural Strategy and Cultural Compact for this district in North East Hampshire. Our extensive and inclusive process of research and engagement identified the following strategic priorities, which were carried forward into the strategy and compact:
	•	Community engagement: Celebrating and drawing on the distinctiveness and diversity of communities, heritage and lived experience across Aldershot and Farnborough, the strategy supports Rushmoor’s communities (especially the least culturally engaged) to express and develop their cultural identity – including Rushmoor’s rich aviation and military heritage - to both increase community pride and the visitor economy and celebrate these together. The strategy charges the new Cultural Compact, facilitated by Hampshire Cultural Trust, to develop programmes which deliver against wider social and environmental outcomes. It prioritises the development of projects engaging with marginalised communities and people from socio-economically disadvantaged backgrounds in order to provide equitable cultural participation and a cultural voice for those who are more marginalised; and ensures that culture plays an important role in the regeneration of Aldershot and Farnborough town centres.
	•	Cultural engagement: Twin priorities: (1) Invest in commissioning, mentoring and audience development to realise culturally diverse content and create a ladder of development opportunity for emergent community-based organisations, whilst linking to regional and national initiatives and activities to raise Rushmoor’s profile as a cultural destination for both residents and visitors; and, (2) Work with artists and the Createch sector to establish Rushmoor as a centre for innovation and excellence in creative media. Taken together,  these measures aim to create independent, sustainable cultural organisations with agency to create relevant community-based practice and, in turn, to create an authentic and vibrant home-grown cultural ecology in which amplifies Rushmoor’s diverse and distinctive people, places and heritage.
	•	Skills development: Working in partnership with Further and Higher Education to support skills and leadership development, providing new pathways to employment and addressing skills gaps and shortages. The strategy operates at all levels and in all sectors of the cultural and creative industries but prioritises creative digital skills development to capitalise on opportunities within Createch and give more young people in Rushmoor the opportunity of developing a sustainable career in the Creative Industries. It will build capacity amongst local creative employers, helping to attract new investment and raise economic output and productivity of Aldershot and Farnborough to match and better that of surrounding major towns.
	•	Building capacity in the cultural sector: The Council and its partners will create new permanent positions to support cultural engagement, skills development and event delivery and identify other investment to expand the cultural offer. These posts will have a specific remit to support the development of Rushmoor’s cultural ecology, catalysing and engaging with creative and cultural talent, supporting it through an inclusive growth framework to enable sustainable, viable organisations which in turn develop new talent to create a resilient cultural ecology. 
Derby Cultural Masterplan and Compact (2021/22)
We carried out creative economy mapping and visitor economy analysis as part of a cultural masterplan to develop a new Cultural Compact; come up with proposals for continued investment in cultural infrastructure and development of the cultural sector; and interventions to bring visitors back to the city centre. The resulting Culture Derby Manifesto built on the city’s unsuccessful bid for City Of Culture status to reimagine the city with culture at its heart and make Derby’s cultural offer representative of all its communities. It prioritised: Growing Derby’s Cultural and Creative Ecosystem; Reimagining Derby City Centre with Culture at its heart; Transforming social mobility of Derby’s Children and Young People; and Breaking down barriers to engagement by making space available for new forms of cultural production, supporting  cultural producers to make more successful bids for third party funds, establishing a structural fund for business support and investment in growth and diversifying Derby’s cultural workforce.
Somerset Cultural Strategy (2022/23)
Herefordshire represents a distinctive, predominantly rural context for cultural strategy. Our experience of working in other rural counties and rural/coastal areas of conurbations has sharpened our awareness of the central importance of place in developing strategies that leverage local strengths and address specific regional challenges - skills directly applicable to updating the Herefordshire Cultural Strategy.
Our experience of a ‘failed’ strategy is instructive here. In 2022/23, The Fifth Sector carried out extensive consultation with creative producers and stakeholders across Somerset as the county looked to bring together four ‘legacy’ local authority districts, two of which had no cultural strategy or investment at the time, under a single unitary authority. Somerset showed many similarities to the Herefordshire context: a cultural and creative sector dispersed across a large and diverse geography, operating mostly in small towns and villages, where the parish council was often as active a partner as the local district authority, with a higher proportion of ‘makers’, artists and touring performance companies and without a University campus offering creative arts degrees. What the county did have was a multi-million pound commitment from ACE to invest in refurbishment of a local venue, the Octagon in Yeovil, on the Southern edge of the county. However, our consultation emphasised that neither the county’s creative producers nor its audiences were focused on development of a new showcase theatre; they were more preoccupied with the preservation and advancement of the ecosystem of smaller, local venues and producers, and on building strength through better coordination of investment and training in the development of creative freelancers and microbusinesses. The new authority had set out with the ambition of making the publication of a new cultural strategy a unifying and trailblazing part of its launch. As the date of the launch of the new unitary authority drew nearer, the strains on its budgetary position became clearer; this was a body which was starting life with an unmanageable deficit, and the new Council found itself unable to accept the recommendations of our report. After eighteen months of internal reviews, and two changes of post holder with responsibility for culture, the new Somerset Council approved a much-abbreviated internal report which focused almost entirely on the benefits of securing ACE investment in the Yeovil venue. The opportunity to celebrate Somerset’s distinctive cultural ecology was lost – or at least shelved until such time as resource allowed it to be revisited. 
This experience serves as a warning that the process around developing a new cultural strategy for Herefordshire needs to demonstrate both ambition for the sector and commitment from stakeholders and funders sufficient to follow through on its recommendations. The precedent of the successful Place Partnership bid provides a helpful precedent of how those might be balanced on a larger scale, given a commitment to cross-sector working and outcomes embedded in an open and democratic process. 
 Question 2: Service delivery
Our approach to implementing and delivering the services outlined in the specification will be comprehensive, collaborative, and tailored to Herefordshire's unique context. It will build on our review of the current Herefordshire Cultural Strategy and creative industries mapping exercise carried out over 2023/24. This comprehensive analysis revealed several key findings that will inform our approach to updating the Cultural Strategy:
	•	The resilience of Herefordshire's creative business base, evidenced by growth in registered enterprises from 645 in 2020 to 695 in 2023, despite COVID disruptions.
	•	Significant growth in the cultural and creative sectors' GVA, increasing by 38% from £83.2m in 2020 to £114.7m in 2022.
	•	A doubling of the creative workforce when including freelance and self-employed workers, with our analysis identifying 4,100 creative workers compared to 2,120 reported in national statistics.
	•	The critical role of freelance and self-employed workers in driving innovation, enterprise, and growth in the rural creative economy.
	•	The need for a place-specific model of growth that reflects Herefordshire's unique rural creative economy.
Our approach 
It is important that the strategy development process can build, capture and maintain momentum. Creative freelancers and producers, cultural partners and other stakeholders need to feel that they can give a positive endorsement of any new strategy, not a defensive acknowledgement. 
We recognise that our engagement will need to reflect some distinctive characteristics of Herefordshire’s cultural and creative economy if the resulting strategy is to authentically, accurately and assuredly prioritise initiatives and align with local strategies.
	•	Use of language: creative industries terminology and use of jargon around ‘place’, and ‘clusters’ , whilst appropriate in context of defining outcomes, can be off-putting to some cultural producers, communities and audiences. Herefordshire and its cultural producers are familiar and comfortable with ideas of community arts and  participatory arts – we want to embrace that register of language as a way to open up people’s sense of the opportunity to build inclusive approaches to culture.
	•	The analysis and recommendations of the strategy need to reflect the scale of Herefordshire’s places: Hereford is a city of 60,000, its market towns have populations between 3,000 and 10,000, the Golden Valley and other areas are deeply rural with low population densities and significant gaps in transport and other public infrastructure. We will challenge assumptions that ‘models’ and ‘benchmarks’ adopted from urban and metropolitan areas will automatically apply in this context.
	•	We will reflect the success of HCP in developing cross-sector partnerships as agile, effective and sustainable mechanisms to build capacity, resilience and confidence amongst cultural producers, communities, audiences and stakeholders. The strategy will explore how the County can best amplify and expand the dynamic relationship of project activity and partnership working between different sectors rather than relying on time-limited, single-sector initiatives.
Throughout the project, we will ensure alignment with broader economic and social policy agendas, incorporating insights from national and international best practices in cultural strategy development. Our team's extensive experience in the sector allows us to bring a wide perspective to the work, helping to position Herefordshire's cultural strategy within the broader national and international context.
Methodology
	•	Project inception – (October 2024)
An initial project inception meeting with the project steering group will confirm the project method and timetable, establish project management milestones and regular contact points, identify key stakeholders to be addressed through consultation, and suggest locations and resource for the keynote workshops.
	•	Desk-based review of existing strategy and evidence base (November 2024)
We will refresh our review of existing Herefordshire cultural information, studies, plans, and strategies, including the existing Herefordshire Cultural Strategy (2019-2029). This review will focus on:
	•	Assessing progress against the strategy's five key outcomes
	•	Identifying areas where the strategy needs updating based on our 2023/24 evidence
	•	Evaluating the effectiveness of the strategy's priority objectives in light of recent developments
This initial analysis will provide a solid foundation for the strategy update and ensure continuity with previous work while addressing newly identified challenges and opportunities.
	•	Stakeholder engagement and consultation (November 2024 – February 2025)
We propose to ‘bookend’ the strategy development process with two facilitated stakeholder workshops, developed in partnership with and hosted by the Herefordshire Cultural Partnership (HCP) Board. The workshops will be complemented by ongoing stakeholder engagement through interviews and focus groups. This approach will ensure that the updated strategy reflects a wide range of perspectives and has broad buy-in from the cultural sector, the Council and other stakeholders.
	•	Exploration and Engagement Workshop (November-December 2024)
The first stakeholder workshop, which needs to be held no later than early December, will support the HCP Board in reviewing and developing the Cultural Strategy, reflect feedback from our August 2024 report and translate these into the main planks of the agenda. It will focus on the areas for future growth validated by discussion at the August meeting of HCP:
	•	Leveraging the strengths of the freelance and self-employed creative workforce and articulating the role of HCA, NMITE and other skills and business development support in building a sustainable base for current and future creative talent
	•	Developing co-production models that can build scale across dispersed cultural organisations
	•	Aligning place-based cultural initiatives (including the Place Partnership and other participatory arts activity) with broader economic development, health, and wellbeing agendas
	•	Addressing fragmentation in the cultural sector and increasing specialisation and collaboration between different cultural and creative sectors
	•	Improving investment coordination: we will explore mechanisms for better coordinating cultural investments, including potential for a Cultural Investment Board
5.	Stakeholder interviews and focus groups (November 2024 – January 2025)
Following the initial workshop, we will conduct stakeholder interviews and focus groups to derive deeper insights into specific issues and opportunities identified during the workshop, which may include:
	•	The potential to develop a county-wide co-production model, as suggested by the emerging model developed by Hereford Museum
	•	Opportunities to leverage the Herefordshire BID and Visitor Economy Plan Network to clarify the role of the cultural sector in servicing key visitor segments
	•	Lessons learned from the success of the 'Our Place' bid and how these can be applied more broadly across the county
	•	Strategies to address fragmentation in the cultural sector and audiences
6.	Review of Visitor Economy Plan (January 2025)
On its completion (which we expect to be in In January 2025), we will review the Visitor Economy Plan and integrate relevant insights into the draft strategy. This timing allows us to ensure complementarity and coherence between the two strategies.
7.	Synthesis and foresighting (February 2025)
We will compile a draft report including updates, recommendations, and template improvements for the Cultural Strategy based on the outcomes of the workshops, stakeholder engagement and review of the Visitor Economy and incorporating insights from national and international best practices in cultural strategy development, drawing on our team's extensive experience and networks. We will seek initial desk-based feedback from HCP to ensure the updated strategy aligns with and supports broader economic and social policy agendas in Herefordshire.
8.	Strategy and action planning workshop (February 2025)
A second workshop will review the draft report and recommendations for the updated cultural strategy and action plan. This workshop will be crucial in ensuring that the strategy is both ambitious and achievable, with clear ownership and timelines for key actions. We will use workshop findings to identify areas for update, prioritise key themes and objectives and refine the action plan.
9. 	Final report (March 2025)
The final report will take the form of an updated strategy for 2025-2030:
	•	Refreshing the vision for Herefordshire Cultural Partnership
	•	Recommendations to reflect any changes needed to ensure that HCP’s future governance and structures are suited to the emerging needs of the County’s creative economy (including consideration of a Cultural Investment Board)
	•	Creating an action plan and implementation roadmap with 1, 3, and 5+ year milestones, highlighting potential investment opportunities
	•	Identifying key differentiators that set Herefordshire apart from other parts of the West Midlands and competitor regions
The project team and their roles will be as follows:
- Iain Bennett (Project Director): Overall strategic oversight, liaison with HCP Board, integration of findings with broader policy context
- Lynne McCadden (Lead Facilitator): Management of stakeholder engagement, workshop design and delivery, focus on skills development strategies
- Steve Hunt (Associate) will draw on his experience of managing projects across creative and visitor economy in rural areas to bring focus to rural cultural development models
This timeline allows for the delivery of the final strategy by the deadline of 27 March 2025, while also accommodating the integration of insights from the Visitor Economy Plan.
