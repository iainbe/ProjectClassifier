Credibility, affordability and value for money

	•	R&D relating to the transition from 2D to 3D - one of the Labs’ key aims - requires volumetric capture that can be operationalised as soon as the lab becomes operational in Y2.
	•	Current high-quality approaches to volumetric capture utilise an array of static 2D cameras and depth sensors to capture performances from multiple angles, using advanced software algorithms to combine the multiple input streams into a single coherent volumetric scan. High quality volumetric capture facilities are available but very expensive (in the order of £2,000 per second), and cannot be produced live as they require significant post-processing time.
	•	Real-time capture solutions (e.g. building on LiDAR sensors) are now starting to come online, however, the produced quality and resolution is currently poor. Additionally, most volumetric capture solutions are limited to a relatively small volume - just sufficient to scan a single person - whereas future content format will require studio-scale solutions.
	•	To be useful for broadcast and ‘as live’ production in d10’s studios, any solution must be cost-effective, high quality, work in real-time and work at studio scale.
	•	While rapid progress in volumetric is expected over the course of the six-year programme, the only solution that currently meets those criteria and can be operationalised for high TRL R&D starting in Y2 leverages motion capture suits in conjunction with virtual avatars.
	•	To capture live experiences, real-time motion capture suits on performers produces real time skeletal and facial tracking data that can be fed into real-time graphics engine and mapped onto virtual avatars. This allows performers to effectively puppeteer virtual avatars in real-time. This is expected to be the primary workflow leveraged by the Labs.
	•	Avatars can either be created by 3D artists, or scanned from the actual performers (for instance, to represent celebrities). The project budget accommodates capacity for both technical artists and external photogrammetry services that support the R&D facilities in Y2 - 4.
	•	In parallel, the project budget includes installation of a spatially calibrated multi-camera array and storage solution to capture multi-angle vision data. This setup will enable the capture of training data to feed into AI and vision research from Y2 (opened to the wider community and Lot 1 Lab), and serve as future-proof infrastructure to apply future software-based volumetric capture solutions later in the project as they become available (e.g. through Lab 1 trickle down or commercial AI solutions). This is further supported by installation of AI compute facilities in Y5 (both offline and real-time compute) that enable the running of AI-based approaches within the infrastructure.
	•	Consequently, a portion of the equipment funding in Y5 is reserved for experimental and market-ready volumetric capture solution that can build, complement and work alongside the described motion-capture workflow.
