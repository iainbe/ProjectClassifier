Surrey+ Creative Corridor
Powering creative innovation and growth throughout the UK from Surrey

The corridor that stretches across Surrey, west along the M3 to Winchester and North to Pinewood can lay claim to being the UK's most dynamic creative region outside London. Almost unrecognised in national strategy, 115,000 creative and digital professionals and over 17,000 companies across Surrey+ are building the future of screen entertainment, games, digital innovation, and storytelling. Together, they are generating £7.2 billion annually – more than the creative economies of Bristol and Manchester combined.
This isn’t a statement of potential here; it’s the reality that's happening, right now, in studios and agencies and production facilities that people may drive past without knowing they exist. But here's the thing: most people have never heard of Surrey+ as a region.
To unlock this immense potential and ensure the Corridor’s sustained growth and resilience, this report presents four core policy recommendations for strategic investment and development:
Strengthening skills development & freelancer resilience
Enhancing business growth & commercial capability
Accelerating innovation through collaborative research & development (R&D)
Strengthening coordination across the surrey+ creative corridor
These recommendations arise directly from extensive sector consultation and rigorous mapping evidence. They are designed to transform Surrey+ from a dispersed region of fragmented potential into a resilient, out-performing cluster - a dynamic network of interconnected, specialised hubs whose collective power lies in their capacity for collaboration across conventional administrative and sectoral divides.

Surrey+ isn't one cluster; it's a network of creative powerhouses
Surrey+ doesn't fit the metropolitan model of a core city with a thriving creative quarter. It looks more like a constellation: different centres of excellence that shine brighter when they're connected. There is a clue to that in Nesta’s seminal 2016 report, which identified the games industry in the Guildford-Aldershot travel-to-work-area as one of the top 20 creative clusters in the UK.
Surrey+ offers a constellation of creative hubs, each with its own specialism, spanning urban centres and rural districts, connected by excellent road and rail links.


The distinctive strength of Surrey+ lies in making visible the connections between these places.

Academic Powerhouses: Research That Drives Industry
Surrey+ benefits from having world-class creative research institutions collaborating with industry and driving commercial activity and innovation:
Royal Holloway, University of London is leading the development of the £75m national CoSTAR R&D network for immersive, real time content for film, television, games and live entertainment
Investment in the Games & Innovation Nexus (GAIN) provides increased capacity for UCA's established Games Incubation Studio and key location for collaboration, incubation, R&D, and play through the University of Surrey's Games Innovation Zone, supported by Surrey County Council.
Winchester School of Art explores heritage-digital convergence
What makes this special: Unlike many regions where universities and industry operate separately, Surrey+ demonstrates genuine collaboration and knowledge exchange.

The investment landscape: untapped potential
Surrey+ currently attracts significant investment, but it's fragmented and often invisible:
Major studios continue expanding facilities
Games companies secure substantial development funding
Production facilities invest in new technology
Farnborough's aircraft hangers have been turned to sound stages and re translating aerospace technology into innovation in screen production
Examples of dynamic interaction with other high growth clusters, from Sony Professional Europe at Pinewood developing innovative new camera systems for the Warner Bros/Apple movie “F1’ to social media and broadcast content around the Maclaren FI team based at Woking.

The opportunity: Surrey+ catalyses investment activity to multiply the impact of innovation taking place across the region. When businesses can see the full picture, they will make different decisions about where to locate, who to partner with, and what projects to pursue.

The freelance economy connects the Corridor
What really makes Surrey+ tick is something you can't see on any map: 43% of our creative workforce operates as freelancers, contractors, and micro-businesses. These highly skilled and productive individuals aren't just statistics; they're the connective tissue that makes innovation flow across our region.

The average freelance creative professional working in Surrey+ might start the week at a games company in Guildford, spend Wednesday shooting a commercial at a production facility in Hampshire, and finish Friday mentoring students at a university campus. Each time they move between projects, they carry knowledge, techniques, and connections that spark new collaborations.
This mobility creates something powerful: 76% of freelancers actively share ideas and innovations between the businesses they work with. They're not just working for hire – they're cross-pollinating creativity across an entire region.

Strong community of other creative professionals
Access to world-class facilities and universities
What they find challenging:

Local skills improvement plans (LSIPs) in Surrey and Hampshire are laying the tracks for a new and coordinated environment for business skills, aligned to devolution plans. Elsewhere in the UK, BFI’s Screen Alliance North has shown that where strategic authorities and sector bodies have come together to invest in supporting their freelance workforce, they've achieved remarkable results.
The Business Reality: Small, Agile, and Growing
90% of creative businesses in Surrey+ employ fewer than five people. If you're thinking this sounds like a problem, think again.
Small creative businesses are actually perfectly adapted to today's market. They can respond quickly to new opportunities, form project-specific partnerships, and maintain long-term collaborations with their neighbours and customers.

The Challenge: moving from hidden strength to national catalyst
Surrey+ clearly possesses unequalled facilities, a diverse talent pool and a wealth of new ideas and techniques. But the lack of recognition for Surrey+ means that national strategy, and the investment that follows it, is focused on other, less productive areas, to the detriment of our region and the UK Creative Economy as a whole. 
How can we make our existing strengths more visible, connect our distributed excellence, attract the investment we need to turn promising innovations into profitable IP and give creative professionals the support they need to thrive?
Right now, we're operating within a thousand small ponds instead of amplifying our collective value. Brilliant work happens in isolation. Opportunities for collaboration get missed because people don't know what's happening in the next town over. Graduates leave for London because they can't see the career possibilities right here.
Surrey+ has all the pieces of something extraordinary. These challenges aren't insurmountable – they're opportunities. 
We just need to connect them.

Why now?
The UK’s creative industries are at an inflection point. 
Remote working has boosted flexibility but made it harder for businesses to form meaningful connections and transfer skills across teams.
Increased investment in High-End TV from US-based streamers has boosted demand for talent and production capacity but declining investment from domestic broadcasters and a tough commercial environment for independent film has introduced new volatility to the sector. 
AI and emerging technologies are creating new opportunities for those who can adapt quickly, but threaten the destruction of jobs and value for firms unable to keep up with the changes and individuals unable to learn new skills. 
The lines between gaming, film, heritage, and digital services are blurring in ways that favour regions like ours where these sectors already coexist.
At the same time, political changes are coming that will reshape how creative economies receive support. Both Surrey and Hampshire will have elected mayors by 2027, each with greater control over business support and skills development. Other devolved regions have had a head start and are already building stronger creative economy strategies; we have a window of opportunity to establish Surrey+ as the UK's most innovative cross-sector creative region outside London. But it requires us to act with intention, coordination, and shared ambition.

Setting the agenda: a policy framework for strategic growth
In consultation with Surrey County Council and an Advisory Group made up of businesses, universities and colleges from across the region, we have developed a policy framework for the Surrey+ Creative Corridor. Its four core recommendations are for strategic investment and development are:

1. Strengthening Skills Development & Freelancer Resilience
2. Accelerating Collaborative Research & Development (R&D)
3. Enhancing Business Growth & Commercial Capability
4. Strengthening Corridor Coordination & Governance


Bringing the plan to life
Examples of the kind of actions that could flow from this policy include:
Freelancer Skills Passport Programme Imagine having a portable record of your skills and development that's recognised across every college, university and creative business in Surrey+. You complete training with one company, and it counts towards qualifications you can use anywhere else. You learn new software on a film project, and that experience translates into better games work.
We're creating a system where:
Training is subsidised and delivered flexibly around project schedules
Skills gained in one sector count towards progression in others
Micro-credentials build up to recognised qualifications
Professional development is affordable and relevant
Regional Talent Network
A hybrid platform where freelancers can:
Showcase their work to potential clients across Surrey+
Find collaborators for cross-sector projects
Access shared workspace through a voucher scheme
Join quarterly meetups rotating between creative hubs
Innovation Fund for Freelancers Small grants (£2,500-£7,500) to help freelancers:
Access new technology and software
Develop cross-sector skills through job shadowing
Collaborate on experimental projects
Take time for R&D between commercial projects
Mesh Fluency Development 
Structured programmes helping creative professionals develop skills that work across sectors. Think: 
Games developers learning virtual production techniques for film and HETV
Designers joining immersive storytelling workshops
Filmmakers exploring interactive media

Securing investment in transformational growth
This framework is a practical blueprint based on extensive research with creative professionals, businesses, educators, and sector organisations across Surrey+.
We've talked to game developers and film producers, freelance designers and agency owners, university researchers and technology companies. We've looked at what works in comparable creative regions. We've crunched the numbers on investment, returns, and realistic timescales.
What emerges is both a compelling opportunity and a clear path forward.
Our research suggests that a £3-4 million strategic investment over three years could catalyse:
450-600 new high-value creative jobs
£15-20 million in additional economic value within five years
Over £30 million in private sector investment
A return of £4 for every £1 invested within five years
These aren't hopeful projections; they're conservative estimates based on what's happened when other global creative regions made similar investments in connecting their distributed strengths.
The costs of not acting
Let's be clear about what happens if we don't act decisively now.
Other regions will establish stronger creative economy strategies. They're already doing it. Combined Authorities across the North and Midlands are sharing in a £150m investment from the Creative Sector Fund – nothing of that kind is earmarked for the South East. Bristol has its long-established creative network. Other regions are having conversations with policymakers and investors that we're not part of. Investment will flow to regions that can articulate their value proposition clearly and demonstrate coordinated action rather than scattered activity.
Political changes will reshape regional economic development by 2027, with new Combined Authority mayors who will prioritise regions that have clear strategies and strong voices.
Talented creative professionals will continue to leave for London and for regions that offer better support networks, career development opportunities, and recognition of their contributions.
Innovation opportunities will be missed as brilliant ideas stay siloed within individual businesses or universities instead of catalysing broader regional transformation.
Standing still is not an option. Surrey+ needs to run faster to avoid falling behind regions that are already ahead in the coordination of and investment in their creative economies.
