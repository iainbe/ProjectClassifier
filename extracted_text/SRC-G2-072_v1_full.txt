Herefordshire Cultural Strategy 2025-2030
Executive Summary
Herefordshire stands at a pivotal moment in its cultural development. This strategy sets out an ambitious yet practical vision for how culture can drive economic growth, enhance wellbeing, and strengthen communities across our distinctive rural county over the next five years. The strategy builds on the achievements of the previous five years, notably the emergence of Herefordshire Cultural Partnership (HCP) as a cultural leadership  with a distinctive voice and widespread support within the county and beyond. In aligning with the county's Destination Management Plan and Festivals & Events Framework, it presents a coordinated approach to cultural development that celebrates Herefordshire's unique character while addressing its specific challenges.
The Rural Context: Challenges and Opportunities
Herefordshire's rurality presents both unique challenges and distinctive opportunities for cultural development. As one of England's most sparsely populated counties, we face significant challenges in cultural provision, with communities separated by considerable distances and limited public transport. Environmental factors, including seasonal flooding, regularly impact cultural participation and delivery, requiring adaptive approaches to programming and engagement.
Yet these rural characteristics also create opportunities. Our outstanding landscape provides both inspiration and settings for distinctive cultural experiences that cannot be replicated elsewhere. Our market towns and villages maintain strong local identities and community spaces that serve as cultural hubs. This strategy embraces these rural characteristics and develops bespoke approaches that conventional urban-focused strategies cannot address.

Pro-Herefordshire Mindset: A Distinctive Approach to Rural Cultural Development
The Pro-Herefordshire Mindset represents the overarching philosophy that guides our cultural strategy. Rather than a standalone priority, it is the lens through which all our cultural development activities are viewed and evaluated. This mindset celebrates Herefordshire's distinctive rural character while addressing its unique challenges, creating an approach to cultural development that is authentic, sustainable, and deeply rooted in place.
This mindset recognies that Herefordshire's cultural identity is inseparable from its landscape, its border history, its agricultural heritage, and the independent spirit of its communities. It acknowledges that conventional urban-focused cultural strategies often fail to address the specific needs and opportunities of rural counties like ours. Instead, we embrace a bespoke approach that turns our rural characteristics into strengths rather than limitations.
Key Principles
Place-Based Identity
Herefordshire's identity is shaped by its distinctive landscape and heritage. As the Destination Management Plan articulates, our county's authentic character is defined by "2000 Years of Border History and Heritage," a "Rich Tradition of Agriculture, Artisanal Food & Craft," and an "Unspoilt and Rugged Landscape." These elements form the foundation of our cultural identity and inform how we develop and promote our cultural offer.
The Pro-Herefordshire Mindset ensures that cultural development enhances rather than dilutes this distinctive identity. It encourages cultural organiations to draw inspiration from local stories, traditions, and environments, creating experiences that could not exist anywhere else. T
Rural Innovation
Rather than attempting to replicate urban cultural models, the Pro-Herefordshire Mindset embraces rural innovation. This means developing approaches to cultural engagement that work with, rather than against, our rural geography and demographics. Examples include:

Distributed Networks: Creating networks of cultural activity across market towns and villages rather than centraliing all cultural infrastructure in Hereford
Seasonal Programming: Developing a cultural calendar that responds to seasonal changes and challenges, particularly addressing the impact of flooding on cultural participation
Digital Connectivity: Using digital platforms to connect dispersed communities while recogniing and addressing digital exclusion
Mobile Delivery: Taking cultural experiences to communities through touring programs and pop-up events
Adaptive Reuse: Repurposing existing community spaces for cultural activities rather than relying solely on purpose-built cultural venues
Local Economic Resilience
The Pro-Herefordshire Mindset priorities local economic resilience by keeping cultural spending within the county whenever possible. 
With approximately 800 creative businesses in the county, there is significant potential to strengthen the local creative economy through strategic procurement and partnership approaches. This principle will be applied across all cultural development activities, from major capital projects like the new Hereford Museum & Art Gallery to community-led initiatives in market towns and villages.
Environmental Responsibility
Herefordshire Council has pledged to reduce carbon emissions to net-zero by 2030. The Pro-Herefordshire Mindset embraces this commitment, recogniing that our cultural sector has both a responsibility and an opportunity to contribute to environmental sustainability. This includes:

Sustainable Production: Supporting cultural organiations to adopt sustainable production practices
Green Travel: Developing approaches to cultural tourism that minimie environmental impact
Nature Connection: Creating cultural experiences that strengthen people's connection to the natural environment
Environmental Advocacy: Using cultural platforms to raise awareness of environmental issues affecting the county
Community Ownership
The Pro-Herefordshire Mindset recognies that sustainable cultural development must be owned and driven by communities themselves. This means moving beyond consultation to genuine co-creation, ensuring that communities have agency in shaping the cultural life of their places.

Children and Young People: Nurturing the Next Generation of Creative Talent
This priority focuses on connecting young people with cultural opportunities that reflect the realities of Herefordshire's rural creative economy, emphasiing portfolio careers, entrepreneurship, and place-based learning. By investing in young people's creative development, we not only enrich their lives and educational experiences but also build the foundation for a sustainable creative economy in Herefordshire.
Developing creative skills and pathways for children and young people is essential to Herefordshire's cultural future. Our approach recognies that conventional career pathways often fail to address the specific needs and opportunities of rural counties like ours. Instead of focusing solely on traditional progression routes, we embrace a more holistic approach that equips young people with the diverse skills, experiences, and connections they need to thrive in Herefordshire's creative landscape or to take their Herefordshire-nurtured talents to the wider world.

Key Principles
Place-Based Learning
Children and young people's cultural education should be rooted in Herefordshire's distinctive landscape, heritage, and creative traditions. This place-based approach ensures that young people develop a strong sense of cultural identity and belonging while gaining skills and knowledge that are relevant to local creative opportunities.
This principle aligns with the Pro-Herefordshire Mindset, encouraging educational institutions and cultural organiations to draw on local stories, traditions, and environments in their work with young people. By connecting young people with Herefordshire's cultural assetswe help them understand and value the distinctive character of their county.
Portfolio Career Development
The reality of Herefordshire's creative economy, with its approximately 800 creative businesses predominantly comprising micro-enterprises and freelancers, requires a different approach to career development. Rather than preparing young people for single-track careers within large organiations, we need to equip them with the diverse skills and entrepreneurial mindset needed for portfolio careers.

This approach recognies that many creative practitioners in Herefordshire combine multiple roles—perhaps working part-time in education, running their own creative business, and contributing to community projects. By exposing young people to this reality and helping them develop the necessary adaptability and enterprise skills, we prepare them for sustainable careers in the local creative economy.
Inclusive Participation
Every child and young person in Herefordshire should have the opportunity to participate in high-quality cultural experiences, regardless of their background, location, or circumstances. This principle recognies the specific barriers to participation faced by young people in rural areas, including transport challenges, digital exclusion, and limited local provision.
By taking a proactive approach to addressing these barriers—through outreach programs, digital engagement, targeted support for underrepresented groups, and collaborative approaches to transport—we can ensure that all young people can access and benefit from cultural opportunities.
Youth Voice and Agency
Young people should not be passive recipients of cultural provision but active participants in shaping Herefordshire's cultural life. This principle emphasies the importance of youth voice and agency, ensuring that young people have meaningful opportunities to influence decision-making, co-create cultural experiences, and develop their own cultural initiatives.
By empowering young people as cultural leaders and creators, we not only enhance their skills and confidence but also ensure that Herefordshire's cultural offer remains relevant and engaging to younger generations.

Evidence Gaps and Proposed Solutions
Despite the strength of existing provision, several significant evidence gaps limit our understanding of children and young people's cultural engagement and needs in Herefordshire:

Gap 1: Comprehensive Participation Data
There is currently no comprehensive data on children and young people's participation in cultural activities across Herefordshire. Existing data is fragmented across different organizations and often focuses on specific projects rather than providing a county-wide picture.
Proposed Solution: Develop a centralized data collection system that enables cultural organizations to share anonymized participation data while respecting privacy regulations. This could be integrated with the Creative Skills Passport to track engagement patterns while providing valuable information for young people themselves.

Gap 2: Creative Career Pathways
Limited evidence exists on the career pathways of young people who engage with Herefordshire's cultural education opportunities. We lack longitudinal data on how early cultural engagement translates into career choices and outcomes, particularly in the context of rural creative economies.
Proposed Solution: Commission a longitudinal study tracking the career journeys of young people engaged in cultural activities in Herefordshire. This should include both those who pursue creative careers within the county and those who take their skills elsewhere, providing insights into the long-term impact of cultural education.

Gap 3: Digital Engagement
While digital approaches are often proposed as solutions to rural access challenges, we have limited evidence on their effectiveness and the specific digital barriers faced by young people in different parts of Herefordshire.
Proposed Solution: Conduct a digital engagement audit across the county, mapping digital access, skills, and preferences among children and young people. This should inform the development of targeted digital engagement strategies that address specific barriers and build on existing strengths.

Gap 4: Educational Integration
There is limited evidence on how effectively cultural learning is integrated into formal education across Herefordshire's schools. While some schools have strong cultural partnerships, others may provide more limited opportunities, creating inequalities in access.
Proposed Solution: Work with Herefordshire Council's education department to map cultural provision across all schools in the county, identifying gaps and sharing best practices. This should inform the development of a more equitable approach to cultural education that ensures all children have access to high-quality opportunities.

Gap 5: Youth Voice
While youth voice is recognized as important, we have limited evidence on how effectively young people's perspectives are currently integrated into cultural decision-making in Herefordshire.
Proposed Solution: Commission a youth-led research project to explore young people's experiences of and aspirations for cultural engagement in Herefordshire. This should inform the development of more effective youth voice mechanisms and ensure that the Cultural Strategy responds to young people's actual needs and interests.

Cultural Democracy: Ensuring Culture is Shaped By and For Everyone
Cultural Democracy is about ensuring that all communities can shape and access cultural opportunities across Herefordshire. This priority recognizes that true cultural vibrancy comes not just from providing access to culture, but from enabling communities to define what culture means to them and to actively participate in its creation. 
In a rural county like Herefordshire, with its dispersed population and distinct local identities, Cultural Democracy takes on particular importance as a mechanism for ensuring that cultural development is inclusive, representative, and responsive to local needs.
Our approach moves beyond traditional models of cultural participation that focus primarily on increasing attendance at established cultural venues. Instead, we embrace a more radical vision of Cultural Democracy that redistributes power and resources, enabling communities to co-create cultural experiences that reflect their own identities, interests, and aspirations. This is particularly important in addressing the specific barriers to cultural engagement faced by rural communities, including transport challenges, digital exclusion, and limited local provision.
Key Principles
Co-Creation and Community Ownership
Cultural Democracy places communities at the heart of cultural development, shifting from a model where culture is delivered to communities to one where it is created with and by them. This principle emphasizes the importance of genuine co-creation processes that involve communities from the earliest stages of planning through to delivery and evaluation.
As the Festivals & Events Framework notes, 
"Herefordshire's communities will feel proud, motivated and able to participate in and host events and festivals across the county, all year-round." 
This sense of ownership and agency is essential to sustainable cultural development, particularly in rural areas where community-led initiatives often form the backbone of cultural life.

Equitable Distribution of Resources
Cultural Democracy requires a fair distribution of resources across Herefordshire's diverse communities and geographies. This principle recognizes the historical concentration of cultural investment in urban centres and seeks to redress this imbalance by ensuring that resources reach all parts of the county, including remote rural areas and market towns.
The Co-Creation Fund proposed in the Festivals & Events Framework represents an important step in this direction, providing resources for community-led cultural initiatives across the county. By prioritizing underserved communities and supporting "15 fringe events in underserved communities annually by 2027," we can begin to create a more equitable cultural landscape.

Diverse Representation and Inclusion
Cultural Democracy embraces the full diversity of Herefordshire's communities, ensuring that all voices are heard and represented in cultural governance, programming, and participation. This principle acknowledges that certain group, including young people, disabled people, people from lower socioeconomic backgrounds, and ethnically diverse communities, have historically been underrepresented in cultural decision-making and participation.
By taking proactive steps to address barriers to inclusion, from physical accessibility and transport to digital exclusion and cultural relevance, we can ensure that Herefordshire's cultural offer truly reflects and serves its diverse communities.
Hyperlocal Approaches
Cultural Democracy in Herefordshire requires hyperlocal approaches that respond to the specific needs, assets, and identities of individual communities. This principle recognizes that a ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach to cultural development is particularly inappropriate in a rural county with such diverse local contexts.
By supporting cultural development at the most local level, whether that's a village, a neighbourhood, or a specific community of interest, we can ensure that cultural provision is relevant, accessible, and sustainable. This approach aligns with the Festivals & Events Framework's emphasis on ‘localism’ and the importance of community-led initiatives.

The network will include representatives from market towns, villages, and specific communities of interest, ensuring comprehensive coverage across the county. Champions will receive training, support, and modest resources to enable them to fulfil their role effectively.

Support for market town cultural consortia, building on the model of the Leominster Cultural Consortium
Collaborative approaches to cultural planning and delivery across market towns

This approach recognizes the vital role that market towns play in Herefordshire's cultural ecology and ensures that their distinctive needs and opportunities are fully addressed in cultural strategy and delivery.

We will support the development and animation of community cultural spaces across Herefordshire. This will include:

Mapping existing community spaces with potential for cultural use
Supporting communities to adapt and enhance these spaces for cultural activities
Developing a network of "cultural living rooms" in communities with limited cultural infrastructure
Exploring innovative approaches to temporary and mobile cultural spaces

This approach recognizes that physical infrastructure is essential to cultural democracy, particularly in rural areas where purpose-built cultural venues may be limited. By maximizing the cultural potential of existing community spaces, we can create more accessible and sustainable platforms for cultural engagement.

While we know that certain groups face barriers to cultural participation, we have limited specific evidence on the nature and extent of these barriers in Herefordshire. This makes it difficult to develop targeted interventions that effectively address the root causes of exclusion.
Proposed Solution: Conduct a detailed barriers analysis, working with   underrepresented groups to understand the specific challenges they face in engaging with culture in Herefordshire. This should include both quantitative data on participation patterns and qualitative insights into lived experiences, informing the development of more effective inclusion strategies.
Amplify the Impact of Co-Creation
While co-creation is widely advocated as a principle, we have limited evidence on its impact in rural contexts like Herefordshire. We need a better understanding of what effective co-creation looks like in practice and how it contributes to cultural democracy outcomes.
Proposed Solution: Establish a co-creation action research program, documenting and evaluating co-creation processes across a range of cultural initiatives in Herefordshire. This should generate practical insights and tools for effective co-creation in rural contexts, building the evidence base for this approach while also enhancing practice.
Develop Community Cultural Leadership
There is limited evidence on the current state of community cultural leadership in Herefordshire, including the skills, resources, and support needed to enable effective community-led cultural development.
Proposed Solution: Conduct a community cultural leadership audit, identifying existing and potential cultural leaders across Herefordshire's communities and assessing their support needs. This should inform the development of targeted leadership development programs that build community capacity for cultural democracy.

Bridge the Divide in Digital Cultural Democracy
While digital approaches are often proposed as solutions to rural access challenges, we have limited evidence on their effectiveness in promoting cultural democracy specifically. We need a better understanding of how digital tools can support community cultural ownership and co-creation, not just access to existing cultural content.
Proposed Solution: Pilot a digital cultural democracy initiative, exploring how digital platforms can support community cultural ownership and co-creation in rural contexts. This should generate practical insights into effective digital approaches while also creating new opportunities for community cultural expression.

Measuring Success
The success of the Cultural Democracy priority will be measured through:

Community Engagement: Levels of community involvement in cultural governance, planning, and delivery
Geographic Distribution: Spread of cultural activities and resources across Herefordshire's communities
Diversity and Inclusion: Representation of diverse communities in cultural participation and leadership
Community Ownership: Evidence of community-led cultural initiatives and their sustainability
Cultural Infrastructure: Development of community cultural spaces and resources

Conclusion
Cultural Democracy is essential to creating a cultural landscape in Herefordshire that truly reflects and serves its diverse communities. By empowering communities to shape and create their own cultural experiences, we ensure that culture remains relevant, accessible, and sustainable across the county.
Through strategic initiatives like the Co-Creation Fund, Cultural Champions Network, and support for community cultural spaces, we will build a more equitable and inclusive cultural ecosystem that celebrates the distinctive character of Herefordshire's communities while addressing the specific challenges of rural cultural engagement.
By addressing identified evidence gaps and developing more comprehensive approaches to understanding and supporting community cultural needs, we will strengthen the foundations for cultural democracy in Herefordshire. This will not only enhance cultural participation but also contribute to wider goals of community cohesion, wellbeing, and local pride.
Embracing Cultural Democracy will demonstrate how Herefordshire's rural character can be a strength rather than a limitation in cultural development, creating unique opportunities for community ownership and hyperlocal approaches that ensure culture is truly shaped by and for everyone.
Creative Economy: Harnessing Herefordshire's Distinctive Creative Potential
The Creative Economy priority focuses on supporting Herefordshire's 800 creative businesses and approximately 4,100 creative workers to grow sustainably through targeted business support, collaborative approaches to skills development, and innovative workspace solutions that overcome rural isolation. This priority recognizes that Herefordshire's creative economy operates differently from urban models, with a distinctive pattern of distributed creative activity across the county and a significant reliance on highly-skilled freelance and self-employed workers who drive innovation and productivity.
Recent research reveals that Herefordshire's cultural and creative industries have demonstrated remarkable resilience and growth, with sector GVA increasing by 38% between 2020 and 2022 (from £83.2m to £114.7m) – far outpacing the county's overall economic growth. This growth has been driven primarily by productivity improvements rather than employment expansion, with GVA per capita in the sector growing by 31% to £54,100 – nearly double the average GVA per capita for all employment in Herefordshire of £27,278.
Our approach recognizes that conventional urban-focused models of creative economy development are not appropriate for Herefordshire's rural context. Instead, we embrace a place-specific model that leverages the county's distinctive assets, supports collaboration between dispersed creative businesses and practitioners, and creates the conditions for sustainable growth across the whole county.

Key Principles
Co-Production and Collaboration
Herefordshire's creative economy thrives through co-production and collaboration rather than through the physical agglomeration typical of urban creative clusters. This principle emphasizes the importance of creating mechanisms for creative businesses and practitioners to work together across geographic boundaries, sharing resources, knowledge, and market opportunities.
When Herefordshire's freelance and self-employed creative workforce (approximately 2,000 individuals) is included in economic analysis, the county demonstrates significant creative specialization in several sub-sectors, including Crafts (LQ of 54.79), Design and designer fashion (LQ of 4.89), and Photography (LQ of 4.57). This reveals that Herefordshire's creative economy is more specialized and productive than traditional statistics suggest, but requires different support mechanisms than urban creative clusters.
Rural Innovation and Enterprise
The Creative Economy priority embraces rural innovation and enterprise, recognizing that Herefordshire's creative businesses often operate differently from their urban counterparts. This principle emphasizes the importance of supporting business models that are appropriate to rural contexts, including home-based businesses, micro-enterprises, and portfolio careers that combine multiple creative activities.
Research shows that Herefordshire's creative businesses have demonstrated remarkable resilience, growing from 645 in 2020 to 695 in 2023 despite the disruptive impact of COVID lockdowns. This growth reflects the adaptability and entrepreneurial spirit of the county's creative sector, which has continued to innovate and develop despite challenging economic conditions.
Infrastructure for Growth
Appropriate infrastructure is essential for creative economy development in rural areas. This principle recognizes the need for both physical infrastructure (workspaces, production facilities, digital connectivity) and soft infrastructure (networks, business support, skills development) that are tailored to the specific needs of Herefordshire's creative businesses and practitioners.
Stakeholder consultation has consistently identified the lack of suitable production and showcasing infrastructure as a key barrier to growth. As the review notes, "Providing suitable production and showcasing infrastructure within the county has the potential to amplify the returns from co-production and collaborative working." This is particularly important given the distributed nature of Herefordshire's creative economy and the need to create focal points for collaboration and innovation.
Skills and Talent Development
The creative economy relies on skilled talent, and this principle emphasizes the importance of developing, attracting, and retaining creative skills within Herefordshire. This includes both formal education and training pathways and continuing professional development opportunities for existing creative practitioners.
The review identified significant gaps in local creative skills provision, particularly in continuing professional development opportunities for freelance and self-employed creatives. It also highlighted the challenge of retaining talent within the county, with many creative graduates drawn to opportunities in nearby cities like Bristol and Birmingham or to London.
Strategic Alignment
The Creative Economy priority aligns with and amplifies key strategic initiatives across the county:
Pro-Herefordshire Mindset 
The Creative Economy priority embodies the Pro-Herefordshire Mindset by embracing a place-specific model of creative economy development that celebrates the county's distinctive character and assets. By supporting local creative businesses and practitioners to develop sustainable business models based on Herefordshire's unique strengths, we ensure that economic development enhances rather than dilutes the county's distinctive identity.
Destination Management Plan 
This priority directly supports the DMP's Priority 2: "Grow the volume of overnight visitors" by strengthening the creative businesses that contribute to Herefordshire's distinctive visitor offer. It also supports Priority 4: "Reinforce the distinctive destination brand and identity" by developing creative products and experiences that reflect and celebrate Herefordshire's unique character.
Festivals & Events Framework 
The Creative Economy priority supports the Festivals & Events Framework's emphasis on "keeping spend local and amplifying local businesses by using local suppliers." By strengthening Herefordshire's creative businesses, we enhance their capacity to support the county's festivals and events sector, creating a virtuous circle of local economic development.
Implementation Approaches
Mountain Perspective Business Support Program
We recommend expanding the successful Mountain Perspective program, which has already supported over 120 creative enterprises to develop sustainable business models. An expanded program would:
Provide targeted business support for creative businesses at different stages of development
Offer specialized advice on intellectual property, digital transformation, and market development
Create peer learning opportunities for creative entrepreneurs
Develop specific support for freelance and self-employed creatives

This approach recognizes the critical role that freelance and self-employed creatives play in Herefordshire's creative economy, as highlighted in the review: 
"Freelance and self-employed creatives play a critical role in the rural creative economy by enabling businesses to manage and reduce entrepreneurial risk, de-risk market uncertainties, and reduce the amount of finance required for innovation and business start-ups."
Creative Workspace Strategy
Develop a county-wide Creative Workspace Strategy that addresses the specific needs of Herefordshire's creative businesses and practitioners, including:
Mapping existing workspace provision and identifying gaps
Supporting the development of creative hubs in market towns and rural areas
Exploring innovative approaches to shared and flexible workspace
Ensuring that workspace development is aligned with transport and digital infrastructure
This approach responds directly to the finding in the review that, 
"Expecting creative business owners to adapt to unsuitable premises in off-centre locations which require access to a private car places further barriers in the way of growth and investment."
Skills Exchange Programme
The review demonstrates need for a Skills Exchange Programme to enables creative businesses and practitioners to share skills, knowledge, and resources. Linked to the Elevate programme already in development by Mountain Perspective, this could:
Create a digital platform for skills exchange and collaboration
Facilitate mentoring relationships between established and emerging creative practitioners
Support collaborative projects that bring together different creative disciplines
Develop specific initiatives to address identified skills gaps
This approach builds on the review's finding that Herefordshire's creative economy thrives through co-production and collaboration rather than through physical agglomeration. By creating mechanisms for skills exchange, we can enhance the benefits of collaboration while addressing the challenges of geographic dispersion.
‘Local First’ Procurement Initiative
A Local First procurement initiative would encourage public and private sector organizations to prioritize local creative suppliers. This could include:

Creating a directory of local creative businesses and practitioners
Developing guidance on how to incorporate local creative services into procurement processes
Showcasing examples of successful local procurement
Monitoring and reporting on the economic impact of local procurement
This approach reflects success of similar initiatives in other communities, including the ‘Preston model’ which brought together the procurement heft of twelve large public sector agencies to support local economic development. It responds to the review's recommendation to,
"Better articulate the value of cultural investment to diverse stakeholders" by demonstrating the economic benefits of supporting local creative businesses.
Case Studies
Mountain Perspective
The tailored programme of sector-specific information, advice and guidance (IAG) funded by Herefordshire Council and delivered by Mountain Perspective exemplifies effective business support for rural creative enterprises. By providing tailored advice, mentoring, and networking opportunities, the programme has to date helped over 120 creative businesses to develop sustainable business models. One participant, a jewellery designer based in a remote part of the county, was able to transform her business by developing an effective online presence and connecting with other local creatives for collaborative marketing initiatives.
Hereford Make CIC
Hereford Make CIC demonstrates the potential of collaborative workspace models in supporting creative businesses. By providing affordable workspace, shared equipment, and a supportive community, the CIC has enabled numerous creative businesses to establish themselves and grow. The model could be replicated in market towns across the county, creating a network of creative hubs that support local economic development.
Rural Media
Rural Media shows how a creative organization can successfully operate in a rural context while achieving national and international impact. By combining local engagement with digital distribution, Rural Media has created sustainable business models that support both commercial and community-focused activities. Their approach to talent development, particularly for young people, offers valuable lessons for creative economy development across the county.

Evidence Gaps and Proposed Solutions
Despite the strength of recent research, there remain gaps in the evidence which limit our understanding of Herefordshire's creative economy:
Gap 1: Creative Business Needs and Growth Potential
While we now have better data on the scale and productivity of Herefordshire's creative economy, we have limited detailed understanding of the specific needs and growth potential of different creative sub-sectors and business types.

Proposed Solution: Commission a detailed creative business survey to gather information on business models, markets, growth ambitions, and support needs. This should include both registered businesses and freelance/self-employed creatives, providing a comprehensive picture of the sector's needs and potential.
Gap 2: Creative Workspace Demand
There is limited evidence on the specific workspace needs of creative businesses and practitioners across Herefordshire, making it difficult to develop targeted workspace solutions.

Proposed Solution: Conduct a creative workspace audit and demand assessment, mapping existing provision against current and future needs. This should include both quantitative data on space requirements and qualitative insights into the types of workspace that would best support creative collaboration and growth.
Gap 3: Creative Supply Chains
We have limited understanding of how creative businesses in Herefordshire interact with each other and with businesses in other sectors, both within and beyond the county.
Proposed Solution: Map creative supply chains across Herefordshire, identifying opportunities for strengthening local connections and addressing gaps. This should include analysis of both business-to-business relationships and connections between creative businesses and other sectors such as tourism, agriculture, and manufacturing.
Gap 4: Digital Maturity
While digital technologies offer significant opportunities for rural creative businesses, we have limited evidence on the current digital maturity of Herefordshire's creative sector and the specific barriers to digital adoption.

Proposed Solution: Conduct a digital maturity assessment of Herefordshire's creative businesses, identifying current capabilities, barriers to adoption, and opportunities for digital transformation. This should inform the development of targeted support programs that address the specific digital needs of different creative sub-sectors.
Gap 5: Investment Readiness
There is limited evidence on the investment readiness of Herefordshire's creative businesses and the specific barriers they face in accessing finance for growth.

Proposed Solution: Develop an investment readiness program that assesses the specific needs of creative businesses and provides targeted support to address barriers to investment. This should include both traditional finance options and alternative models such as community shares, crowdfunding, and social investment.

Conclusion
Herefordshire's creative economy has demonstrated remarkable resilience and growth potential, with significant increases in productivity and output despite challenging economic conditions. By embracing a place-specific model of creative economy development that leverages the county's distinctive assets, supports collaboration between dispersed creative businesses and practitioners, and creates the conditions for sustainable growth, we can build on this success and position Herefordshire as a leader in rural creative economy development.
The evidence review demonstrated how Herefordshire's rural character can be a strength rather than a limitation in creative economy development, creating unique opportunities for innovation, collaboration, and sustainable growth that reflect and enhance the county's distinctive identity.
Building a sustainable creative economy in a rural context requires long-term commitment and investment. Through continued investment in strategic initiatives like sector-specific business support, a Creative Workspace Strategy, Skills Exchange Programme, and Local First procurement initiative, the strategy will go further and faster to address the specific needs and opportunities of Herefordshire's creative businesses and practitioners. 
This evidence-based approach to measuring success would, for the first time, provide clear targets for each phase of implementation while allowing for flexibility and adaptation as the strategy progresses. By establishing specific, measurable objectives that reflect the distinctive character of Herefordshire's creative economy, it would track progress effectively and demonstrate the impact of strategic interventions.
Focusing on co-production and collaboration rather than physical agglomeration, the strategy supports the development of a more resilient and innovative creative economy that contributes to the county's wider economic, social, and cultural goals. In first recognising and seeking to underpin existing strong foundations of the creative economy, then realizing the potential of these foundations, and finally capitalizing on previous investments, the strategy sets out a vision of lasting change that positions Herefordshire as a leader in rural creative economy development.
