EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report analyses City St George's Performing Arts School using contemporary value chain models and benchmarks against leading institutions. Our analysis reveals that traditional linear value chains are being replaced by platform ecosystems where value is co-created through dynamic stakeholder interactions. 
Key Value Proposition
City St George's has a unique opportunity to position itself at the centre of a "river narrative" ecosystem connecting creative practitioners, health providers, cultural institutions, and communities. By strategically reconfiguring its value chain around this platform model, the School can generate multiple revenue streams while establishing a distinctive identity as a creative health and performance hub. 
Key recommendations include:
	•	Developing a platform ecosystem connecting performing arts with creative health practitioners
	•	Creating a digital-physical hub leveraging the Mermaid Theatre opportunity
	•	Implementing unbundled certification programmes for creative practitioners
	•	Positioning social impact as a core value output


1. CONTEMPORARY VALUE CHAIN CONTEXT
1.1 Evolution of Higher Education Value Chain Models
Traditional higher education value chains as described by Pathak & Pathak (2010) and Hutaibat (2011) presented linear models where activities flowed sequentially from inputs to outputs. Contemporary research and practice reveal a fundamental transformation in how value is created and delivered:
Table 1 Evolution of Higher Education Value Chain Models
Traditional Value Chain (Pre-2015)
Contemporary Value Ecosystem (2020-Present)
Linear sequence of activities
Networked platform connecting multiple stakeholders
University as service provider
University as ecosystem orchestrator
Students as service recipients
Students as co-creators of value
Technology as support function
Digital transformation of entire value chain
Integrated institutional delivery
Unbundled provision across partners
Social impact as peripheral activity
Social impact as core value output

As PwC notes in "The Future of Higher Education" (2022): 
"Universities are evolving from content providers to curators of learning ecosystems where value is created through facilitated connections between students, industry, communities and knowledge resources."
1.2 The Platform Ecosystem Model for City St George's
Based on McKinsey's "Education-to-Employment Value Chain" (2023) and New York University's "Arts & Culture Blueprint" (2022), we propose reconceptualising City St George's value chain as a platform ecosystem.
 At the centre lies City St George's as the ecosystem orchestrator, connected to:
	•	Creative Practitioners & Students (co-creating educational experiences)
	•	Health Providers & Researchers (developing creative health interventions)
	•	Cultural Institutions (connecting through the "river narrative")
	•	Community Organisations (delivering social impact)
	•	Industry Partners (providing employment pathways and resources)
 Each connection point represents opportunities for value creation through education, research, creative output, and social impact.

4. STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS
4.1 Platform Ecosystem Development

Recommendation
Position City St George's as the orchestrator of a "river narrative" ecosystem connecting creative practitioners, health providers, cultural institutions and communities.

Implementation Actions
	•	Map stakeholder relationships across the "river narrative" from South Bank to City institutions
	•	Establish governance structure for ecosystem partnerships following NYU Arts model
	•	Create digital platform connecting stakeholders based on Edinburgh Strategy 2030 framework
	•	Develop value proposition for each stakeholder group with clear benefits

Key Performance Indicators
	•	Number of active ecosystem partners
	•	Revenue generated through ecosystem activities
	•	Student engagement with external partners
	•	Social impact metrics across ecosystem
 
 4.2 Creative Health Co-Creation

Recommendation
Develop co-created programmes with students, health providers and creative practitioners to address the £17k-salary practitioner certification market. 
Implementation Actions
	•	Establish advisory board combining health, arts and community perspectives
	•	Implement student co-creation framework based on Dollinger et al. (2018) model
	•	Develop unbundled certification programme aligned with practitioner needs
	•	Create impact measurement framework following King's College London model
 Key Performance Indicators
	•	Number of certified practitioners
	•	Revenue from certification programmes
	•	Health outcomes from creative interventions
	•	Research outputs and external funding
 
 4.3 Digital-Physical Performance Hub

Recommendation
Create integrated digital-physical hub at Mermaid Theatre location combining educational, performance and community spaces. 
Implementation Actions
	•	Develop mixed-use space plan based on UAL case studies
	•	Implement digital infrastructure throughout facility following Oxford digital strategy
	•	Create flexible space utilisation model balancing academic, community and commercial uses
	•	Develop funding model combining university, public, philanthropic and commercial sources
 Key Performance Indicators
	•	Space utilisation rates across user groups
	•	Revenue from external space users
	•	Community engagement metrics
	•	Digital engagement extending beyond physical space
 
 4.4 Unbundled Certification Programmes

Recommendation
Implement unbundled education model providing flexible entry points for diverse learners. 
Implementation Actions
	•	Develop tiered entry pathways similar to Loughborough model
	•	Create stackable credentials based on Deloitte "Higher Education Remade" framework
	•	Establish industry validation mechanisms for credentials
	•	Implement digital delivery platform for accessible learning
 Key Performance Indicators
	•	Enrolment in unbundled programmes
	•	Revenue from non-traditional programmes
	•	Progression rates to traditional programmes
	•	Employability outcomes

5.2 Medium-Term Actions (6-18 months)

5.3 Long-Term Actions (18-36 months)

6.3 Return on Investment
	•	Year 1: Net investment required

	•	Year 3+: Positive contribution with 20-30% margin on new activities
 

7. CONCLUSION
City St George's Performing Arts has a unique opportunity to transform its value chain from a traditional linear model to a contemporary platform ecosystem centred around the "river narrative." 
By orchestrating connections between creative practitioners, health providers, cultural institutions and communities, the School can create distinctive value while generating diverse revenue streams.
The recommendations in this report align with contemporary higher education models where universities serve as ecosystem orchestrators rather than simply service providers. This approach positions City St George's at the forefront of innovation in performing arts education while creating sustainable financial models and meaningful social impact.
