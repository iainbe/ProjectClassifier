Amgen : Creative R&D Connecting Live Performance, Technology and Infrastructure 
Across Wales  
Summary  
Amgen – meaning alternative, other, better – will be a transformative Creative Industries 
Cluster for Wales, driving R&D, innovation and sustainable economic growth in the live 
performance sectors across hard -to-reach geographies. As an industry -led, chall enge -
driven cluster, Amgen directly addresses systemic barriers that constrain productivity, 
scale -up potential and inclusive growth within the Welsh creative economy.  
Amgen will operate a bespoke accelerator for innovation combining live performance 
and creative technology, driving collaborative R&D across microclusters in rural, 
coastal and post -industrial Wales. Amgen will embed capacity within place -based 
creative ec osystems, transforming them into testbeds for production processes and 
audience experiences.  
Amgen's HEI -industry partnership team (USW, BU, AU, RWCMD, CMU, Eisteddfod 
Genedlaethol Cymru, Theatr Clwyd, Wales Millennium Centre, CULTVR, and Frân Wen) 
will catalyse development of new products and live experiences through a challenge -
led, commercially  focused R&D programme with direct routes to market.  
Amgen aims to:  
• Drive economic growth by accelerating innovation in a connected live 
performance ecosystem enabled by regional R&D to support delivery of 
commercial, societal and cultural value;  
• Transform innovation from creative studios into compelling, marketable live 
experiences for diverse audiences;  
• Stimulate investment through the adoption and innovation of new performance -
related technologies;  
• Enhance skills pipelines and talent retention by advancing creative business and 
touring infrastructure;  
• Strengthen regional creative capacity through a strongly inter -connected live 
performance ecosystem, reflecting Wales’s linguistic, cultural and socio -
geographical contexts;  
• Embed equitable access to innovation opportunities.  
Amgen will position Wales as a global leader in next -generation live performance 
innovation, demonstrating the power of place -based R&D to deliver transformational 
economic impact.

 Outline Vision  
Amgen will super -charge the creative economy in Wales by ensuring that socio -
geographic context is no longer a barrier to participation, productivity or growth. Our 
vision is to bridge cutting -edge live performance (theatre, music, dance, festivals) with 
creative -technology R&D to generate scalable, real -world applications in site -specific 
testbeds across Wales, enabling emerging hybrid forms of creation and performance. 
We will re -position non -urban centres as drivers of technological, cultural and inclusi ve 
innovation, intersecting with industrial sectors facing similar challenges.  
Amgen will broker reciprocal and co -equal R&D partnerships between technology -led 
and live performance creators to develop immersive and interactive performance 
experiences that are both place -based and distributed across/between microclusters.  
We will transform the live performance ecosystem in Wales through translational R&D 
in live performance. Amgen builds on the interdependencies and fast -emerging 
technological innovations (including Immersive, XR, AI and speech/language 
technologies) that u nderpin creative ecosystem across rural, coastal and post -
industrial microclusters. Research by EticLab (2026)  maps these interconnections 
across Wales, revealing a network of creative microclusters whose productive 
stickiness is rooted in the specificity and shared values of Welsh language and culture. 
This is exemplified by Eisteddfod Genedlaethol Cymru, Europe' s largest touring cultural 
festival, which demonstrates the capacity of non -urban microclusters to host world -
leading productions.  
However, creative businesses across Wales face persistent barriers to innovation 
capability, talent retention and infrastructure access compared to the Welsh capital 
(McElroy and Davies, 2021). The urgent need to reformulate the creative economy in 
terms o f distributed microclusters calls for a new framework in which growth and 
development happen fairly and ethically (dos Santos et al., 2021).  
Addressing these challenges aligns with Wales’s Wellbeing of Future Generations Act 
(2015), which mandates for social cohesion, equity, environmental responsibility and 
sustainable economic development, and recognises the interdependence of cultural, 
socia l, environmental and economic value.  
Amgen’s vision is to embed an ethical, fair and place ‑responsive approach to creative 
innovation. This means acknowledging the tensions between economic drivers and the 
rapid adoption of new technologies, ensuring that cultural, social and economic value 
develop in balance. Amgen adopts a reciprocal model of distributed creative 
economies in which place ‑based identity, language and cultural knowledge actively 
shape technological development. Rather than exporting innovation, existing 
technologies adapt to l ocal creative contexts, and local creative ecosystems drive the 
creation of new digital tools, formats and markets. This approach offers CICP a unique 
opportunity to test how microclusters can safeguard jobs, leverage co ‑investment, 
catalyse cross ‑industrial innovation and deliver social and cultural benefits beyond 
metropolitan centres which have historically received the greatest investment.  
As an exemplar of how the interconnectivity of microclusters can enable the creative 
economy to succeed outside super -urban areas, Amgen will transform distinct 
microclusters into a network of Innovation Labs, increasing long -term capacity for

Outline Approach  
Amgen will be led by USW and a consortium of HEI -industry partners. We will innovate 
through coalescing microclusters in non -urban locations to harness the unique R&D 
potential of live performance. While creative R&D is concentrated in major 
metropolitan h ubs, significant talent, cultural identity and economic potential remain 
under -connected to technological innovation. To address this imbalance, we will build 
a networked ecosystem where digital tools, immersive technologies, responsible AI and 
new product ion models are embedded within place -based creative communities, 
empowering – not replacing – live experiences. In turn, these communities will shape 
the development of next -generation distributed live performance. This reciprocal model 
ensures that innova tion is not exported from urban centres but co -created across 
regions and in response to the place’s unique cultural identity (Roberts & Lowe 2024).  
Wales -wide creative industry roundtable discussions revealed that the major obstacles 
to growth and sustainability faced by creative microclusters across Wales is the lack of 
a comparable R&D infrastructure akin to that found in urban clusters (Hurford & L ewis 
2025). Research undertaken by  EticLab (2026)  surfaces the inter -connectedness of a 
range of live performance organisations in Wales. The unique infrastructure challenges 
across Wales have hampered research expertise and cross -sectoral innovation from 
coalescing effectively.  
Amgen is ideally placed to drive cross -industrial collaboration precisely because of the 
challenges of our geographies and the mobilities of creative practitioners in their daily 
working lives. Firstly, by creating a robust, connected vehicle for fostering , 
matchmaking, and driving innovation across Wales' creative microclusters. Secondly, 
by enabling working across other sectors, including transport and connectivity, that 
influence capacity to produce work and enable growth, and to integrate with sectors 
that crosshatch with the arts, such as health and tourism.  
Five creative organisations across Wales will provide the core R&D infrastructure for 
Amgen, each offering distinct capabilities in innovation, production and audience 
engagement.  Theatr Clwyd  in the north and  Wales Millennium Centre  in the south will 
act as major hubs with committed routes to market for collaborative R&D.  CULTVR  and  
Frân Wen  bring specialisms in immersive technology and inclusive community 
practice, ensuring that experimentation is both technologically advanced and socially 
grounded. The  National Eisteddfod of Wales  will serve as an itinerant platform for 
annual dissemination and showcase.

We will adopt cross -sector  translational R&D to strengthen regional capacity and  to

Cardiff benefitted from first -round CICF (‘Clwstwr’) and Strength in Places (‘Media 
Cymru’) funding, centred on screen innovation. Amgen will capitalise on the knowledge 
and experience gained from these successful projects, extending their benefits to 
dist ributed microclusters and applying them to the live performance ecosystem, which 
operates in a more dispersed way and with distinct challenges that CICF rounds have 
not yet addressed.  
Wales is entering a new era of AI infrastructure development through two designated AI 
Growth Zones, with £10m of UK Government investment to develop local AI business 
and expertise. Amgen will provide microclusters with the critical mass necessary to be 
active contributors to AI capability -building rather than downstream adopters, enabling 
reciprocal flows between AI research and applied creative production (spanning 
creative development, audience insight, accessibility, language technologies and 
productio n optimisation). This will promote responsible use of AI within our 
microclusters by developing critical understanding of AI -related issues such as bias, 
transparency, accountability, authorship and ‘liveness’.  
Developing Wales's creative economy depends on expanding authentic place -based 
live experiences, widening access and participation, and empowering communities and 
supply chains across a broader range of locations. Centralisation hinders sustainability 
and resilience and often leads to homogenisation. Distributed economies, however, 
hold a ‘selective share of production’ in ‘regions where activities are organised in the 
form of small scale, flexible units that are synergistically connected with each other’ 
(Johansson et al. 2005), via shared products, resources, knowledge and other services. 
Such a network can be connected to nearby networks, resulting in a distributed 
economy network.  
Amgen will develop an inter -dependent network of microclusters enabling them to 
function as innovation centres collaborating across a diverse geography, allowing 
engagement with diverse audiences. This diversity holds economic value; audiences 
value conten t that reflects their own identities, languages and lived experience. Amgen

Thematic challenge areas , as defined by the sector (Hurford & Lewis, 2025) : 
• Connecting  – strengthenin g communities of practice  across microclusters to 
stabilise and grow the ecosystem by strengthening knowledge exchange 
pathways ; 
• Participating – developing located, hybrid and distributed models of co -creation 
that reshape how companies, users and audiences engage, with technology 
augmenting and evolving traditional expectations ; 
• Making – new forms of work  emerging from a translational R&D framework 
involving bringing technology to places and places to technology ; 
• Moving  – supporting the physical and digital infrastructures that underpin 
sustainable and inclusive creative practice across live performance 
microclusters, delivering economic, social and cultural benefits.  
These themes are shaped by a set of cross -cutting considerations: EDI and 
accessibility; sustainability; creative business/commercial capabilities; and language 
and culture, e.g. making the Welsh language a focal point means foregrounding 
translation/inter pretation, integrating AI -driven language technologies with the 
expertise of translators and other practitioners who mediate cultures/languages. 
Translation becomes a relational practice connecting people and places, extending 
beyond language into intersem iotic processes where one sign system or art form is 
transformed into another. This thinking is vital as the sector navigates new technologies 
and reimagines the possibilities of art forms.

 Sector awareness and outreach activity  includes a series of events to build a 
connected ecosystem, bringing people and organisations together to better understand 
the opportunities available and explore innovation trends impacting the sector in the 
short -to-medium term to support experimentati on with performance -related 
technologies, access and inclusion, sustainable practice, supply chain and business 
practices.  
The R&D framework for industry includes : 
1. Innovation  Labs  curated by sector experts (primarily based in Amgen partner 
organisations). The Labs will prime participants in responding to sector and 
intersectional challenges before progressing to small or large R&D grants, with a 
period of mentored experimentation an d training. These will range from 
innovation processes (how to undertake a structured R&D project), specific 
theme challenges (EDI, Sustainability) and include hands -on sessions with 
technology, and business/market awareness. By bringing to gether a cohort of 
individuals with complementary skills, collaborative projects will form naturally 
and strengthen applications to the R&D grants. Freelancers/individuals will 
receive small bursaries to attend. Applicants for R&D grants must have 
undertak en Innovation Labs, ensuring a strong foundation in related skills and 
capabilities and interdisciplinary cohorts across projects.  
2. Creative R&D Grants  - Small and Large  
Each R&D project will be supported by a producer, an academic expert, 
consortium partners, facilitated by PDR. This will ensure an iterative design 
approach including co -design and testing with audiences. Throughout the R&D 
phases we will be working with s trategic partners to ensure that there are routes 
to market both within the Amgen ecosystem and beyond (nationally and 
internationally).  
- Small R&D grants : up to £12k for 3 -month projects  based on  early -stage 
ideas that are high ly experimental . Small grants will be suited to 
freelancers/individuals and micro -organisations . 
- Large R&D grants : up to £75k for 9 -month projects allow ing for more 
ambitious R&D t hat is large r scale and more complex. Large grants will 
require a collaborati ve approach to leverage knowledge sharing  and skills .  
3. The Residencies Programme  will pair practitioners (artists, performers, 
producers, technologists, and academics) with a host organisation to undertake 
a 3-to-6-month knowledge exchange residency. Residencies will be based on a 
hyper -local challenge, e.g. sustainability or EDI. Resi dencies will offer industry 
mentorship and a bursary to cover time and costs. The Amgen residency 
network will share experiences and provide peer support. A specific strand of the 
Residencies Programme will focus on Youth Associates for young creatives age  
18-25 who will receive additional mentoring from host organisations and feed 
into Amgen’s Steering Committee.  
4. R&D Dissemination . All projects will share R&D outcomes through 
opportunities provided across the project duration and with all partner 
organisations, including a public showcase at Eisteddfod Genedlaethol Cymru, 
which covers all creative industries in one site. Funded proje cts will be 
encouraged to share their R&D learning to drive innovation across the entire 
sector.

The greatest sustainability challenge for live performance is the movement of people to

experiences of liveness and proximity across in situ and distributed performance.  
Skills and Talent  training  will be integrated into the R&D activities during awareness

Training via the Innovation Labs will be multi -faceted, covering R&D and innovation 
processes, how to involve participants/audiences, technology and craft practices with 
hands -on workshops, access to equipment and facilities, and business development

Evaluation and Impact  
Monitoring and evaluation plans for capturing the performance and impact of Amgen, 
including the leveraged funding and project alignment with policy, will happen at 
multiple data capture points. Governance and Steering Boards will be responsible for 
the im plementation and oversight of tracking economic growth and social and cultural 
impact. A dedicated team of specialist research organisations, including EticLab, Jones 
the Numbers and the Audience Agency, will develop an evaluation framework capable 
of meas uring both traditional economic metrics and wider social and cultural value.  
This work will be supported by Research Fellow Rebecca Hutcheon (USW) who will help 
gather and interpret evidence against these indicators in order to assess return on 
investment and leverage findings for policy. Findings will feed directly into programme 
decision‑making, enabling the consortium to refine delivery and respond to emerging 
sector needs.  
Key economic indicators include the number of people/organisations trained, 
competencies achieved, jobs secured or created and organisation turnover, and 
audience/participant numbers reached. Key cultural and social indicators will include 
measure of envir onmental and sustainability impacts, EDI data to ensure equitable 
participation, and progress against the wellbeing goals of the WFG Act, including an 
innovative, productive, low carbon society; the promotion of a vibrant culture and 
thriving Welsh languag e; the support for well -connected communities; and society that 
maximizes people's physical and mental well -being. The framework will distinguish 
between Amgen’s direct outputs and its contribution to wider sectoral change, with 
longitudinal tracking to un derstand how skills, networks and innovations continue to 
develop beyond the life of the programme.
