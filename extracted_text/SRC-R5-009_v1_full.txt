SALARY & TOTAL REWARD
Creative Scotland
Salary Review 2026
A decision-ready benchmark using cached Lightcast evidence, public-body disclosures and freelance rate data



Confidential working review. Public evidence is cited; cached provider data is summarised at aggregate level.

Executive decision
Recommendation: retain Creative Scotland's public-body comparator position, then correct only evidenced role-specific gaps.
Creative Scotland's 2023-24 workforce median of £41,911 is almost identical to the National Lottery Heritage Fund's 2024-25 median of £41,979, above Arts Council England (£35,225 in 2023-24) and Arts Council Northern Ireland (£38,283 in 2024-25), and below the older Arts Council of Wales figure (£47,861 in 2023-24). These are workforce medians, not equivalent job matches, but they do not support a general claim that Creative Scotland is under-market.
The cached Scottish creative-occupation evidence is lower: strong family medians run from about £26,464 to £36,720. This is relevant to recruitment and funded-sector context, but it is not a like-for-like substitute for a public body's pension, leave, job accountability or national remit.
Actions
Use public-body peer ranges as the primary pay-policy lane for Creative Scotland employees.
Use the Lightcast family/role evidence as a recruitment-risk cross-check, with digital/software and scarce specialist roles reviewed first.
Do not apply a blanket market supplement. Require an evidenced role match, a sufficiently strong sample, and a documented recruitment or retention problem.
Make pension, leave, flexibility and progression visible as total reward; comparator pension contributions can be material and are not present in Lightcast.
Refresh Creative Scotland's own anonymised payroll/job-evaluation data before final grade decisions. This review cannot test equal pay, grade compression or individual positioning without it.

1. Scope and evidence architecture
The review answers four different questions with four distinct evidence lanes. Keeping them separate is essential because the units and coverage are not interchangeable.

What the Lightcast cache actually contains
44,326 deduplicated UK employer–occupation observations across 14,775 employers; 927 observations and 385 employers classified to Scotland.
Each salary is a provider-supplied employer × occupation median. It is not a row of individual vacancies and is not posting-weighted.
Creative scope uses the governed DCMS SOC2020 creative-occupation registry and the repository's Lightcast-to-SOC crosswalk.
Responsibility is inferred from occupation names. Organisation size uses the company registry employee estimate. Scottish location is the employer's operating or registered local authority, not necessarily the vacancy workplace.
Benefits, contracted hours, protected characteristics, individual job evaluation and incumbent salaries are absent.

2. Creative Scotland's current published position

This is the latest Creative Scotland workforce pay disclosure located for the review and relates to 2023-24. It should be refreshed against the 2025-26 payroll before any implementation decision.
3. Public-body comparator findings

The comparator table intentionally leaves unavailable measures blank. Missing disclosure is an evidence gap, not a zero value. Senior-pay bands are not mixed into workforce medians.
Interpretation
Creative Scotland's published median is effectively level with the Heritage Fund median and above the ACE and ACNI medians captured here.
Creative Scotland's 2023-24 CEO band (£125k–£130k) is below ACE, BFI, UK Sport, Sport England and Heritage Fund, but above sportscotland, Sport Wales, ACNI and Museums Galleries Scotland in the available disclosures.
Differences reflect organisational scale, remit, geography, pension design and job mix. They are context, not automatic pay recommendations.
Sport Northern Ireland remains a live evidence gap and should be completed in the next scheduled refresh.
4. Scottish advertised-pay evidence

What matters
Digital/software/web has the highest strong Scottish family median (£36,720) and the widest upper-quartile pressure (£57,127). This is the first recruitment-risk lane to test against Creative Scotland grades.
Creative leadership/management is £34,500 at the family median; this broad group mixes stage management, creative direction and music direction, so named-role evidence should be preferred.
Arts/screen/production (£30,308), marketing/comms/advertising (£30,000), design (£29,486), and visual arts/crafts (£26,464) sit well below Creative Scotland's workforce median. That difference is plausible given public-body responsibility and total reward; it is not proof of overpayment.
Performance/presenting is insufficient at n=6. Architecture, music and publishing are indicative only. Use the UK lane or obtain fresh role quotes before decisions in those families.
5. Responsibility-level and named-role signals


6. Organisation size
The workbook provides the requested micro/small/medium/large cuts for every family and responsibility level. The Scottish cells are often small, so organisation size should act as a diagnostic rather than a mechanical pay multiplier.
Digital/software/web remains elevated across every size band with strong evidence: medians range from £35,000 to £38,750 in the observed Scottish cells.
Marketing/comms/advertising size medians range from £28,750 to £32,800 with strong evidence; the spread is smaller than the role-level spread.
Arts/screen/production has usable medium, micro and small observations around £29,500–£32,000, but the large-organisation cell is insufficient.
Do not infer causal size premiums from this descriptive cache. Organisation size is correlated with occupation mix and employer type.
7. Benefits and total reward

Public-body pensions can be worth more than a small base-salary difference. Creative Scotland should therefore publish a standard total-reward statement for every grade and use total reward—not salary alone—when testing offer competitiveness.
Fair Work floors
The 2025-26 UK real Living Wage outside London is £13.45 per hour. Annual equivalents depend on contracted weekly hours: approximately £24,479 at 35 hours, £25,874 at 37 hours, or £26,228 at 37.5 hours.
Some cached annual salary values fall below those equivalents, but hours are absent. They may reflect part-time work or provider normalisation and must not be labelled non-compliant without job-hour evidence.
SCVO's 2025 evidence reports a 23% median hourly pay gap between the voluntary and public sectors (£16.84 versus £21.77) and substantial workforce pressure. Creative Scotland should not use financially constrained sector pay as its sole employee benchmark.
8. Freelance and contractor benchmarks


9. Recommended framework
The recommended tool is a decision sequence rather than a single universal number.

Decision corridors—not new grades
General support/officer roles: use Creative Scotland's evaluated grade and public-body peer range; check the real Living Wage hourly floor.
Creative/programme/communications roles: use the internal grade first; cached Scottish P50 is usually around £26k–£35k, so any higher public-body salary should be explained through responsibility and total reward rather than suppressed to sector pay.
Digital/data/software: test against named-role P50 and P75; where a grade maximum falls below strong market P50 and recruitment evidence confirms pressure, consider a governed supplement.
Heads/directors/CEO: use public-body senior-role evidence and job size. Do not use broad creative-market titles, which contain classification and seniority errors.
Freelancers: use day-rate tables plus Creative Scotland fair-pay guidance, scoped by deliverables and rights.
10. Implementation and refresh

11. Limitations and non-findings
This is a robust external benchmark review, but not an equal-pay audit, job-evaluation exercise, affordability model or payroll implementation plan.
No incumbent-level Creative Scotland data, job descriptions, recruitment outcomes or contracted hours were supplied. Individual underpayment or overpayment cannot be concluded.
The Lightcast cache is not posting-level and cannot support vacancy-weighted estimates because corresponding posting counts are zero.
The creative occupation definition follows a DCMS SOC2020 registry that was still marked in development in the repository. Culinary and some broad craft/manufacturing occupations should be treated as contextual, not automatically within Creative Scotland's employee universe.
Public disclosures vary by year and measure. Workforce medians, grade ranges and senior bands are shown separately and never averaged together.
Public-body benefit evidence is incomplete. Missing disclosure means unknown, not no benefit.
Appendix A. Method
Map Lightcast six-digit occupations to SOC2020 via the repository crosswalk; retain one deterministic best-fit SOC per Lightcast occupation.
Restrict to 31 governed DCMS creative SOC2020 codes; group into 12 review families.
Filter annual salary values to £10,000–£250,000; deduplicate company number × occupation, keeping the latest fetched row.
Calculate P25, median and P75 across employer-level occupation medians. No posting weighting or inflation uplift is applied.
Classify size from employee estimate: micro 1–9, small 10–49, medium 50–249, large 250+, unknown otherwise.
Classify Scotland from employer operating or registered local-authority code. Responsibility comes from deterministic title tokens.
Evidence labels: strong n≥20, indicative n=10–19, insufficient n<10.
