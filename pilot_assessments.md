### Analytical lens findings

**/skin:** Workshop/consultation evidence cited — practitioners report cultural life affects retention. No formal post-project learning transfer data.
**/thad:** Convergence theory predicts that connecting the three communities would create trading relationships. The report identifies the mechanism (brokerage) but cannot test whether it has occurred.
**/deepthink:** The report itself flags that Group B GVA is not claimed as additional creative output. East London comparator is "structural rather than quantified" and "not independently verified." Tier 4 estimate relies on penetration-gap modelling, not direct survey.
**/blindspot:** The convergence analysis identifies a "trading relationship waiting to be made" — a capability that exists but has no institutional mechanism to activate it. This is an option value finding the original tender did not ask for.

### Evidence gaps
- No raw survey instrument, full interview transcript, or participant list in the extracts
- No post-intervention tracking; outcomes are modelled/estimated, not observed after the report
- The 200+ firms claim from the methodology brief is not directly evidenced in the readable extracts; the report identifies ~90 Group B adjacent firms, not 200+

### Fifth Sector contribution
Fifth Sector (Iain Bennett, Lynne McCadden) authored the report, built the four-tier model, designed the convergence analysis, and authored the options assessment. ## Cross-project synthesis

### Patterns identified

1. **Convergence-economy analysis** appears as a distinctive Fifth Sector method across multiple projects (CDEC 2012, NE Scotland 2025, MITIH 2025, Liverpool DCI 2024). This is a genuine methodological thread, not a retrospective label.

2. **Strategic option value / gated sequencing** is explicitly applied in NE Scotland (options assessment with year 1-7 sequencing), MITIH (three-phase investment sequence), and implicitly in ManMet FGTG (pilot-as-option) and CoSTAR (bid-stage infrastructure options). This is a methodological strength, though the option value is rarely quantified monetarily.

3. **LinkedIn-based workforce mapping** (via Curator Technologies partnership) is a repeatable method deployed across GBSLEP (2017), WMCA (2020), Kirklees (2021/2024), Liverpool DCI (2024), and NE Scotland (2025). This is a product/method spillover within Fifth Sector's own practice — the method developed for one client has been reused for many others.

4. **Social network analysis** is explicitly applied only in MITIH (2025). It is a newer addition to the method set.

5. **Counterfactual benchmarking** is applied in NE Scotland (Nottingham comparator) and implicitly in Kirklees (ONS vs multi-source comparison). Formal counterfactual impact evaluation is not evidenced in any project.

6. **Most claims are REPORTED (deliverer's own account) with DESCRIPTIVE attribution.** No project provides independently corroborated outcomes or causal estimates. This is the primary evidence gap across the portfolio.

7. **Fifth Sector's role varies:** Designer+Deliverer (NES, MITIH, Kirklees, Liverpool DCI), Deliverer as BOP associate (GBSLEP, WMCA), Designer for bids (CoSTAR, Creative City), Advisor (CDEC). Attribution claims must reflect this variation.

8. **Product spillovers of Fifth Sector's own methods** are evidenced: the LinkedIn/curator methodology, convergence-economy framing, and four-tier workforce model have all been iterated across projects. This is a legitimate competitive advantage claim — the methods are reusable and have been reused.

9. **The 200+ firms claim from the methodology brief** is not directly evidenced in any pilot project. NE Scotland identifies ~90 Group B adjacent firms. The 200+ figure may come from a different analysis or a broader definition. This requires validation.

10. **Null/negative findings exist:** Liverpool DCI found "no evidence of effective clustering." NE Scotland found "absence of durable connection infrastructure." These are important findings that should be preserved, not hidden.

### Codebook calibration issues identified

1. **Direct outcome vs spillover boundary** is unclear for method reuse across Fifth Sector projects. Is the LinkedIn method being used in Kirklees after development for GBSLEP a product spillover or just internal method development? The codebook should distinguish "internal method reuse" from "external product spillover."

2. **Option value claims** are often implicit rather than explicit. The codebook should add guidance on when to classify something as OPTION vs DIRECT (the mapping itself is a direct output; the future decisions it enables are options).

3. **Attribution when Fifth Sector worked as an associate** needs clearer guidance. GBSLEP and WMCA were BOP Consulting contracts. The codebook should specify how to record and qualify these relationships.

4. **Bid-stage claims** need a distinct status. FORECAST is in the codebook but needs clearer guidance that bid-stage propositions are not evidence of delivered outcomes.

5. **The "Fifth Sector role" axis** needs to capture the associate/subcontractor distinction more clearly.
