# Approach summary: UK_1412 (British Council)

Top-line proposition, drafted to seed both the Methodology & Approach (MA01) response and
the "Who" that resolves the specialist-gap language in `evidence-blockers.md` §6.

**WHAT.** A Green Book-aligned Economic Valuation Toolkit and technical evidence base that
prices the strategic and economic return of international artist-led innovation to the UK.
It is anchored in a formal econometric counterfactual, using the four live commissions as
case studies for a defensible, wider sector valuation.

**WHY.** This is hard to deliver well, for six specific reasons.

- British Council has ruled out a qualitative-only or Green Book-adjacent exercise:
  Clarification #11 asks for *formal econometric* analysis. That means a defensible
  counterfactual capable of surviving Treasury and FCDO scrutiny of a Benefit-Cost Ratio.
- The four named commissions are still running. A counterfactual built on incomplete data
  would be weak, so the formal econometric work has to run separately, at sector level,
  against comparator programmes and existing datasets (Clarification #12), while the
  commissions themselves are used as live case studies (Clarifications #7, #10).
- Spillovers, option value and multipliers, all named in RFP §7, are three separate
  measurement problems. Multipliers have a proven technique behind them (see Where, below);
  spillovers and strategic option value do not have an agreed technique for creative-tech
  outputs, where market prices mostly do not exist.
- The sector itself has no agreed causal direction between technology and art practice.
  The Creative Industries Policy and Evidence Centre's own createch definition is tech-push
  framed: *"when a piece of technology, either existing or novel, is used to innovate in a
  creative setting."* The Creative Industries Council's broader framing treats the interface
  as bidirectional: it *"captures both the transformation of the creative industries by
  technology, and the co-creation of new tech products and services for creative outputs,
  processes, products and services."* British Council's own title, *Why Technology Needs
  Artists*, names the artist-to-technology direction alongside the technology-to-artist one.
  A counterfactual that assumes one direction risks asserting a causal story it cannot
  defend.
- Welfare measurement in LMICs, named explicitly in §7, is a specialist technique that most
  UK creative-economy consultancies, including Fifth Sector's existing team, do not hold
  in-house.
- The toolkit has to be usable by non-economists across British Council without the delivery
  team in the room (Clarification #15). That constrains how the technical work itself has to
  be built, from the outset.
*(Sources: PEC/Creative Industries Council createch definitions, via web search. The
primary PEC PDF could not be accessed directly in this session. Verify page references
before citing in the actual submission.)*

**HOW.** Two established capabilities and one new one, combined, answer the difficulties
above. Iain Bennett's delivered Green Book monetisation method (applied on Wakefield CDF)
gives a working non-market valuation technique. Jon Bains' Places platform gives an
evidenced comparator-group and network-mapping capability. Sam Collett's causal-DAG design,
new to this bid, gives the identification method that lets the other two support a causal
claim, with the confounders controlled for. Six phases across the 12-month programme, each
mapped to a named deliverable in RFP §7:

1. **Mobilise and scope the valuation frame (M1).** Confirm the UK-primary, sector-secondary
   framing with the Digital Innovation Team; secure access to the prosperity framework, ROI
   measures and FCDO M&E data British Council confirmed at Clarification #13; stand up a live
   data-capture protocol on the four running commissions.
2. **Build the Economic Valuation Toolkit (M2 to M4).** Apply the Green Book five-case
   model, covering the strategic, economic, commercial, financial and management cases, to
   spillovers (knowledge, product, network) and strategic option value. Within this:
   network spillovers are tracked through Places' invisible-network layer, built from shared
   directorships and company-formation links, measured before and after each commission
   against a matched comparator set. Product spillovers use Places' cluster detection
   (density-based, LSOA-resolution) to check whether similar activity emerges near a
   commission's output at a different rate than in matched comparator areas. Knowledge
   spillovers are the weakest fit for platform data, so the qualitative interview strand
   carries most of that weight. Strategic option value uses real-options valuation, anchored
   to a live, unresolved uncertainty: the Lords Communications and Digital Committee's
   licensing-first recommendation on AI and copyright (6 March 2026). A decision tree sets
   out named policy states (licensing-first adopted, status quo, a broader exception
   reintroduced), British Council's payoff under each with and without this investment, and
   the resulting option value. The toolkit is designed for non-economist, self-service use
   from the outset, as a first design principle.
3. **Counterfactual, additionality and field verification (M4 to M7).** Collett specifies
   the causal DAG, with confounder nodes for general sector growth, national trend and
   pre-existing local trajectory, so that an edge only supports a causal reading once those
   are controlled for. Places' existing comparator set becomes the control group for a
   matched difference-in-differences design against non-commission comparators, following
   Clarification #12's steer toward comparator programmes and existing data over new primary
   collection. Artist and tech-partner interviews sense-check the quantitative findings, and
   a deadweight and market-failure gap analysis runs alongside.
4. **Gap analysis and investment framework (M7 to M9).** Benefit-Cost Ratio modelling and an
   investment framework scoped to British Council's own commissioning decisions, per
   Clarification #16.
5. **M&E integration and technical report (M9 to M11).** Map the metrics into British
   Council and FCDO's existing Return on Influence reporting; write the technical research
   report and executive briefing on human-capital and trade-connectivity impact.
6. **Investment deck, handover and enablement (M11 to M12).** A Treasury/FCDO-facing slide
   deck, a toolkit walkthrough session for non-economist users, and advisory input on future
   commission strategy.

Full model detail for the spillovers and option-value strand, including what still needs
checking before submission, is in `methodology-spillovers-option-value.md`.

**WHERE (track record).** RFP §7 sets seven essential requirements. No one Fifth Sector
project answers all seven, so the case is built requirement by requirement, the same way
`evidence-blockers.md` §2 tracks it.

- **Green Book economic valuation of non-market goods.** Iain Bennett built and applied a
  Green Book-compatible monetisation methodology, DCMS Culture & Heritage Capital benefit
  transfer, direct cash-flow monetisation, WELLBY held strictly to a quarantined sensitivity
  line, and led the Wakefield Cultural Development Fund
  value-for-money assessment. This bid carries that method from heritage and place assets,
  where it has been delivered, into art-tech innovation, where its application is new.
- **Mixed-methods research.** Wakefield CDF, Wakefield Our Year and Liverpool Film Fund each
  combined quantitative tracking with qualitative and stakeholder research.
- **Policy alignment and international cultural engagement.** Fifth Sector already delivers
  for British Council: three unpublished British Council-commissioned reports on the
  creative economy in the Western Balkans. A workforce analysis of the games, VFX and
  animation industries; a skills-development framework built on those findings and aligned
  to ESCO; and an initial study in Albania and Kosovo establishing a method to develop
  baseline economic evidence for the creative economy across all six Western Balkans nations.
  Bennett's Honorary Visiting Professorship, his FACT board Trustee role, and current fluency
  with two live House of Lords Communications and Digital Committee reports sit alongside
  this existing relationship.
- **Investment framework and gap analysis development.** Liverpool Film Fund included
  counterfactual analysis and scenario planning; a 2019 AOTF evaluation and a 2022 SYMCA ARG
  evaluation sit in Fifth Sector's portfolio records. The Western Balkans skills-development
  and ESCO-alignment framework adds a further, live example of turning sector research into
  an actionable framework for an institutional client.
- **Technical writing for high-level and policy stakeholders.** Final reports and case
  studies for councils and combined authorities, and the three Western Balkans reports,
  written for British Council itself, the same institutional audience this tender is for.
- **ODA and international development.** The RFP asks for understanding of ODA frameworks
  and the nuances of measuring economic welfare in LMICs, applied to two of the four
  commissions (Argentina and Senegal are ODA-eligible; China and the UK are not). The
  Western Balkans reports give Fifth Sector direct, current experience building economic
  evidence for British Council in ODA-eligible countries. A named ODA/international
  development advisor (see Who, below) reviews the Argentina and Senegal case studies
  specifically, applying the LMIC welfare conventions those two commissions require on top
  of the causal-DAG and Places-based methodology used across all four.
- **Economic modelling of spillovers, option value and multipliers.** Multipliers are
  directly evidenced: the Liverpool City Region Music Economy Report, designed and
  implemented by Iain Bennett with the University of Liverpool's Institute of Popular Music,
  converted the music economy's £406m GVA into a £780m total economic impact figure, a
  genuine, published, worked multiplier calculation in a creative-economy context.
  Spillovers and strategic option value have no past Fifth Sector project behind them. Sam
  Collett's causal-DAG design and the model set out in
  `methodology-spillovers-option-value.md` answer those two directly, as a proposed method.

Exact titles, completion dates and how the three Western Balkans reports are best described
in a public tender response need confirming with British Council's own records before MA01
drafting; this does not change what the evidence shows.

**WHO.** Fifth Sector delivers this through a senior team, built around the two people who
carry the core method and platform, plus two named specialists engaged for the two specific
capabilities the brief names that sit outside that core team.

Iain Bennett owns valuation design and the technical report: the Green Book monetisation
methodology, delivered on Wakefield CDF; Honorary Visiting Professor; Board Member and
Trustee of FACT, the Foundation for Art and Creative Technology. Jon Bains owns the toolkit
and data architecture: Places, the evidence platform that supplies the comparator-group and
network-mapping capability the methodology runs on.

Sam Collett, Chief Innovation Officer at Eventus.do, is confirmed at 10 days, £650 a day. He
proposed the causal-DAG approach this methodology uses and specifies and validates the
causal-identification layer on top of Places (see `evidence-blockers.md` §6), as a full
partner on this bid.

Two further specialists complete the team, each brought in for a named capability:

- **An ODA/international development advisor**, to review the Argentina and Senegal case
  studies against ODA-eligible LMIC welfare conventions specifically (see Where, above). Not
  yet named.
- **An art and technology specialist**, to ground the economic analysis in genuine
  art-and-technology practice. Two candidates approached directly: **Dr Nicola Triscott**,
  CEO/Artistic Director of FACT since 2019, founder of Arts Catalyst (1994), with 25+ years
  at the intersection of art, science, technology and society, a warm-contact route through
  Bennett's existing FACT board membership; and **Graeme Gerard Halliday ("Hallidonto")**, a
  Scottish/London-based artist (Duncan of Jordanstone College of Art and Design) whose
  practice, including the 2010 "Cyborgia Manifesto," VR-integrated drawing and performance,
  and human-machine themes, sits thematically close to the brief's named interests in AI
  ethics, decentralised tools and sustainable technology.

**WHEN.** A 12-month initial programme from September/October 2026, phased per the
megaplan's timeline, with the live-baseline work starting in month one specifically because
it can only happen while the commissions are still running. The RFP's separate 2×6-month
extension options (§3.1.3) exist but aren't detailed or priced in this budget.

**WITH WHAT RESULT.** A toolkit usable by non-economists across British Council; a
gap-analysis and investment framework scoped to British Council's own commissioning
decisions; economic metrics integrated into existing ROI/FCDO M&E reporting; a technical
report evidencing human-capital and trade-connectivity impact; and a Treasury/FCDO-ready
investment deck.
