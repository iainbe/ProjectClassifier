# Draft response to Pete

Updated 2 October 2026 after reviewing the supplied report, three companion CSVs, the call with Pete and the subsequent internal discussion. Draft for Jon and Iain to approve and send. No message has been sent.

Pete,

Thanks for sharing the report and the underlying files. You have assembled a substantial starting point, including the company base, research assets and technology capability within established employers. That changes the useful next piece of work: we should build on this evidence to understand what it means for jobs and growth, and which connections could create further commercial and social value.

Our suggested question is: **how can Liverpool City Region use its digital and technology capability to create and sustain better jobs, grow valuable businesses and realise distinctive commercial opportunities?** Resilience to economic shocks would be part of that, alongside growth and access to wider markets. We would connect the work to the existing Growth Plan and test the priorities with you and Simon.

The accompanying revised brief proposes one bounded study, organised into four connected work packages:

- Compare and enrich the decision-relevant records you have supplied, with definitions, company/group identities, operating locations and measures kept consistent.
- Examine available evidence of jobs and business trajectories, including capability within technology users as well as technology producers.
- Investigate up to two promising commercial pathways through a small number of targeted discussions with firms, customers and research partners.
- Use the findings to test the assumptions behind Drift, Conversion and Fusion, and produce a concise proposition, priority actions and the specification for further consultation and governance.

Running the supplied records through our analytical workflow would add metadata, dated evidence and cross-source checks. It would support those questions and a usable handover, while retaining the work you have already done. We would agree how much independent checking is useful for the decision, rather than commission an exhaustive re-audit.

The hidden-capability register is particularly useful for selecting cases. We would ask where research institutions, established employers and smaller technology firms already help each other create commercial and social value, and which specific changes could strengthen those relationships. Participation, local demand and employment benefits would need checking with the organisations involved.

We have retained your distinction between this work and a broad AI-adoption study. Existing defence and health research would be reused where relevant. A full workforce survey, extensive international benchmarking, intervention delivery and ongoing platform operation would be separately scoped.

This is a different scope from our initial discussion about a GBP 20,000 mapping exercise. The brief sets out a bounded method and indicative person-days; we would settle the fee against the agreed depth, reusable evidence and authorised data access, rather than assume the earlier figure buys the expanded work.

For the next conversation, could we settle:

- What decision Simon needs to make, and which combination of jobs, earnings, business growth and productivity the first piece should support?
- How much independent checking you want of the mapping, and whether we should compare the files you have shared with The Data City's evidence?
- Whether LCRCA and The Data City can authorise project-specific API access under your licence for the sectors our evaluation licence does not cover, including the required outputs and reuse rights? We should also confirm which location, historical and investment-round fields can be accessed through that route.
- Which firms, employers and research partners you can introduce for the selected cases, and who could act on the resulting priorities?

That would let us finalise the deliverables, responsibilities, timetable and cost, following the sequence we discussed: scope, proposition, consultation and governance.
