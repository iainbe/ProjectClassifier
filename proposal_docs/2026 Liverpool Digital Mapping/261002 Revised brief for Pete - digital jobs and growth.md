# Liverpool City Region digital and technology

## Proposed brief: better jobs, growth and commercial opportunity

Discussion draft for Pete Woodbridge, 2 October 2026. Prepared by The Fifth Sector following the supplied analytical report, companion datasets and our conversations. This is a proposed commissioning scope, not an accepted tender, fee quotation or commitment by any partner.

### 1. Purpose

Build on LCRCA's existing digital and technology evidence to identify how the City Region can create and sustain better jobs, grow valuable businesses and realise distinctive commercial opportunities. The work should connect digital capability to the Growth Plan's objectives and examine resilience to economic shocks alongside growth, productivity and access to wider markets.

The first commission should produce a concise, evidence-backed proposition that Pete and the relevant decision-makers can use to choose their next actions and further research.

### 2. Starting material and additional value

LCRCA has supplied its analytical report, cluster database, AI-business register and hidden-capability register. These provide the starting evidence and candidate organisations.

The work will compare, reconcile and enrich the records that matter to the agreed decision. Reusable classifications, sources and findings should be retained. Necessary identity, location, metadata and historical checks support analysis and handover; they should be explained and costed rather than presented as a replacement census.

The additional value is to test the connections between technology firms, established employers and research institutions, and to determine what the available evidence supports about jobs, business trajectories and practical commercial opportunities.

### 3. Principal questions

- Which digital capabilities and business trajectories offer credible routes to good jobs and sustained activity in the City Region?
- Where do technology suppliers, established employers and research institutions already create commercial and social value together?
- Which local connections could help firms develop products, win customers and reach wider markets?
- How exposed are the selected opportunities to changes in demand, ownership, automation or economic conditions?
- Which actions can LCRCA and its partners realistically take, and what further evidence or commitment is needed?

The study will distinguish jobs located in the City Region from benefits to its residents. It will examine who can access the opportunities where evidence allows, without promising a new representative workforce survey.

### 4. Work package A: a consistent decision evidence base

Agree the decision, outcomes, geographic scope and records required. Reconcile the relevant definitions, company and enterprise identities, cohort dates and evidence sources.

Compare selected supplied records with authorised independent or provider evidence, including The Data City where access is agreed. Record additions, discrepancies and unresolved cases, with their effect on the decision. Give priority to material employers, the chosen pathways and ambiguous records.

Distinguish registered offices from operating locations; legal entities from enterprises and brands; technology developers from adopters and adjacent capability; and company-wide from local-site measures. Retain missing and uncertain values explicitly.

Output: a dated, reviewable evidence base and a short statement of the definitions, coverage, discrepancies and claim limits relevant to the commission. This is targeted checking, not certification of every source or a new national census.

### 5. Work package B: jobs and business trajectories

Examine available dated evidence for the selected firms and employers: employment, hiring, growth, ownership and locally based functions. Seek relevant historical comparisons where comparable records exist, rather than relying only on a snapshot.

Use official context, supplied records and authorised source data on compatible populations and dates. Company filings, provider estimates and job advertisements will be reported according to their actual scope. Missing records will remain unknown; zero reported employees will not automatically mean no economic activity.

Consider technology capability within other industries as well as the technology sector. Identify where customer diversity, transferable capability, local functions or entry routes could support growth and resilience.

Output: a concise jobs and business-trajectory assessment, with coverage and limitations, questions for employers, and a small set of outcome measures suitable for further monitoring. Causal estimates of employment effects and individual worker tracking are outside this initial study.

### 6. Work package C: two potential commercial pathways

Select up to two pathways with Pete, using the supplied registers and the significance of the decision. Candidate domains are leads to investigate, not predetermined winners.

Trace the connection between a customer problem, technical capability, research or domain knowledge, and a route to paying customers. Undertake up to six focused external discussions across the selected firms, customers and institutions, subject to introductions and participation. Seek evidence from both sides of material relationships where possible, including unsuccessful or stalled examples.

Test the commercial opportunity, potential local employment contribution, wider-market route, principal constraints and alternative explanations. A co-location, shared label or organisation listed in a register does not establish collaboration or demand.

Output: up to two pathway assessments with the evidence, potential benefits, barriers, prospective delivery owners and next test. The study will not operate a brokerage service, guarantee partner participation or deliver an intervention.

### 7. Work package D: proposition, scenarios and next decisions

Use the findings to examine the assumptions behind the report's Drift, Conversion and Fusion scenarios. These are indicative planning cases, not forecasts. Reconcile the relevant starting populations and measurement bases before using them.

Explain how the selected opportunities fare under stronger or weaker demand and plausible changes in ownership or technology. Identify the assumptions that would change the recommendation and the evidence needed to resolve them. Additional jobs, productivity and GVA benefits will not be inferred from firm counts alone.

Produce a concise preferred proposition, credible alternative, priority actions and dependencies. Each proposed action should identify a problem owner, evidence, intended benefit, delivery route and next decision. Two working sessions with LCRCA are included for inception and the final proposition review.

Output: a decision brief and stakeholder presentation, supported by the evidence and pathway assessments, plus a specification for further consultation, appraisal and governance.

### 8. Deliverables and acceptance

- A dated decision evidence base, with a permitted-use and field-level handover schedule.
- A jobs and business-trajectory assessment with explicit coverage and measurement limits.
- Up to two commercial-pathway assessments.
- A concise proposition and action/dependency schedule, with scenario assumptions and alternatives.
- A stakeholder presentation and specification for the next stages.

Acceptance will be agreed at inception against usefulness for the named decision, traceability, compatible definitions and transparent treatment of uncertainty. Qualified or negative findings should change the proposition rather than be replaced by unsupported claims.

Any reusable database or platform output will be limited to agreed fields and permissions. Full interactive deployment, indefinite platform access and ongoing refresh are separately scoped.

### 9. Access and responsibilities

LCRCA will identify the decision owner, confirm the supplied material's reuse permissions and facilitate introductions. The supplier will describe and document the analysis, relevant source checks and limits.

Any Data City access must be authorised by the licence holder and provider for the named contractor, sectors, API use, record allowance, processing and required outputs. Project-specific approved credentials are required. Allocation of a seat or possession of a key does not itself establish these rights.

If access or evidence cannot support the agreed design, stop the affected work at the initial review, report the constraint and agree a narrower output or revised scope before using the remaining allowance.

Available investment and grant fields may inform the selected cases where their meaning, coverage and permissions support use. Grants, share allotments and external equity investment will remain distinct. A complete investment-flow or regional export study is outside this commission.

### 10. Effort, timing and cost

For this deliberately bounded study, the provisional planning allowance is 30-38 specialist person-days: evidence and access 6-8; jobs and trajectories 8-10; two pathways and focused discussions 10-12; proposition, sessions and quality review 6-8.

This is an estimate to refine, not a fixed-price offer. Allow approximately six to eight elapsed weeks after access and the scope are agreed, subject to participant availability. Source checks and strategic discussions can run in parallel; unreconciled numbers cannot become accepted findings.

The actual fee, VAT basis, provider costs, expenses and any partner inputs will be agreed before commissioning. This scope differs from the initial GBP 20,000 mapping discussion. Existing work will be credited through reuse rather than rebuilt unnecessarily.

### 11. Boundaries and progression

Reuse existing defence and health studies where relevant. Broad economy-wide AI adoption remains outside scope, as discussed with Pete.

The study does not include a representative employer/worker survey, a complete ten-year workforce panel, a full investment/export analysis, extensive international benchmarking, causal policy evaluation, an approved full strategy or delivery of a new programme.

Follow the agreed sequence: scope, proposition, consultation and governance. The present study concentrates on scope and a tested initial proposition, with focused evidence discussions. Wider consultation, investment appraisal, governance choices and implementation are subsequent decisions, with their own resources and procurement arrangements.

The result should be a useful choice: proceed with a specific opportunity, narrow or change the approach, obtain named further evidence, or defer unsupported action.
