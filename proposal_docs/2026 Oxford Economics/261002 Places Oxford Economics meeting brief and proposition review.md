# Places: Oxford Economics meeting brief and proposition review

Prepared 2 October 2026 for the meeting on Monday 5 October 2026. Internal discussion paper.

## The decision in one minute

Go into Monday seeking agreement to design and cost one bounded validation study, with named responsibilities and access conditions. Preserve the ambition for joint client work and a possible PEC proposal. Treat those as subsequent decisions, rather than promises that Oxford Economics will validate Places, incorporate it into its models, or help obtain funding.

The current method note has useful foundations: evidence-state labels, explicit limitations, an out-of-sample comparison in principle, a recognisable benchmark and exclusion of GVA from the first round. The current deck is a broad client introduction. Its central gap is the absence of an Oxford Economics-specific offer, test scope, resource request and decision.

Recommended proposition:

"Places brings together classified organisations, operating locations and contextual evidence to support reviewable analysis of creative economies. We propose a bounded joint study with Oxford Economics to test the reliability of selected measurements and their additional value for a defined local decision. The study would establish what can be used, what needs development and whether there is a credible basis for joint client work."

The most important question is: "What is the smallest study you would consider both analytically credible and commercially worth doing, and what would you need from us to scope it?"

## 2. Questions OE may ask us, and how to answer

These are anticipated questions, not a claim to know OE's intentions. The strongest answer is the evidence plus its boundary. Where the reviewed pack is insufficient, agree a specific follow-up rather than improvise.

### "What does Places add to what we already offer?"

Answer: organisation-level classification, operating-location evidence, explicit treatment of adjacency and inferred relationships, and a reviewable path from a local question to a finding are the proposed contribution. The joint study would test their accuracy and usefulness.

Bring: one worked client question and a side-by-side account of the existing workflow and the proposed addition. OE already offers subnational data, forecasts, scenarios, dashboards, APIs and consultancy. Visualisation or richer evidence in general is insufficient differentiation. [O1-O3]

### "Which parts work today?"

Answer: distinguish what the team can currently demonstrate from what the deck claims and what the method note lists as developing. The deck contains interface screenshots; it does not provide executed method tests, current data coverage or external validation.

Bring: a dated, rehearsed demonstration; a source/coverage record; and one finding carried through review to an actual output. If these are unavailable by Monday, say exactly which evidence will be supplied afterwards.

### "What is the model you want us to validate?"

Answer: Places is an evidence and decision environment with multiple analytical methods. We propose validation of specified measurements and a bounded analytical use, rather than a platform-wide approval.

Bring: a method inventory showing input, definition, output, uncertainty, intended use, implementation status and validation status. Define the conventional and enriched models only after agreeing the outcome and eligible data.

### "What is your ground truth?"

Answer: a published cluster map is a benchmark under a particular population, source and method. Agreement tests comparability, rather than proving complete truth. Selected classifications and locations need independent evidence checks; predictive and causal claims need their own validation.

Bring: exact PEC table/data version, definitions, geography and vintage; source-overlap assessment; and an approach for explaining differences without automatically treating every additional Places record as an improvement.

### "Why 709 microclusters, and are you reproducing or updating them?"

Answer: the count originates in Creative Radar 2020; the 2023 report places microclusters in a broader cluster/corridor analysis. We need to agree whether the study reproduces a historical result, compares methods on matched evidence, or investigates current differences. Those are different exercises. [B1-B3]

Bring: precise citations and underlying geometry/data if obtainable. Avoid treating a UK count match as proof of correct local membership, boundaries or economic outcomes.

### "What are the outcome, sample and holdout?"

Answer: phase one is a measurement/benchmark exercise. A later enriched-model test needs an independently defined outcome, compatible unit/time and a frozen evaluation design agreed before the results.

Bring: an honest availability inventory. The current note supplies no specified dependent variable, exact sample, model or holdout design. Do not describe that as already settled.

### "How do you handle endogeneity or confounders?"

Answer: graph links and before/after change remain descriptive unless a particular causal design supports a stronger claim. We would specify treatment, outcome, estimand, confounders, mediators, spillovers and comparison strategy separately.

Bring: one candidate question if available, with actual history and comparison coverage. Do not control indiscriminately for mediators, or imply that forecasting accuracy establishes an intervention effect.

### "Can you legally share and commercialise these data?"

Answer: permissions must be checked per source and use. Access to The Data City or Lightcast does not by itself establish permission for OE review, new modelling, derivative publication, redistribution or a joint product.

Bring: signed source terms or a rights schedule, with owner, permitted uses, expiry, sharing, derived-output rights, charges and fallback. Where terms are unresolved, narrow the first study to authorised data.

### "How reliable are the hidden-economy and freelance estimates?"

Answer: the note identifies these as modelled and the Census calibration as open. Freelancing, self-employment, jobs and people are different concepts. A higher result from a model does not establish that national estimates materially undercount a like-for-like population.

Bring: definitions, denominators, model inputs, uncertainty and independent checks. Keep these estimates out of first-phase acceptance claims unless they are the specifically scoped research question.

### "Are small counts and commercially sensitive values protected?"

Answer: the note expressly says suppression is inconsistent. Treat that as a disclosure-control gate before external firm-level or small-cell sharing, rather than a routine later enhancement.

Bring: an approved controlled-access or aggregate demonstration and the actual policy to be reviewed. Public company data may still have licence constraints; repeated querying can expose small groups even where a single output is suppressed.

### "Who is the customer, and how would this make money?"

Answer: propose a first job, such as a local authority's creative-cluster evidence brief, and test whether OE and that buyer value it. Research testing, a bespoke supported analysis and a recurring service are distinct offers.

Bring: any actual paid work, references, repeat demand and delivery-cost records the team can verify. The supplied OE pack does not establish paid Places demand, a price or a licence renewal case.

### "Who funds the test and what do you want from us?"

Answer: ask for a bounded scope, cost and role decision. The September email proposes seeking PEC funding with OE and The Data City; that route needs eligibility, budget, partner approval and a defined deliverable.

Bring: actual affordable scoping capacity and a proposed cost structure. Do not reuse the CIRCUIT day-rate scenarios as an OE quotation or assume a PEC funding mechanism exists.

### "Who owns the IP, clients and resulting product?"

Answer: distinguish TFS background platform IP, provider data and methods, new study outputs, and each partner's pre-existing models. Agree licences, publication, attribution, client relationship and reuse before joint delivery.

Bring: existing agreements and exclusions. A future joint approach or shared study does not create a partnership, distribution agreement or licence by itself.

### "What happens if your data add no value?"

Answer: publish or record the appropriately qualified result, identify where the approach remains useful, and stop unsupported expansion. Measurement reliability can have value even if enriched prediction fails; an attractive map alone would leave the client case unfinished.

Bring: stop/narrow/retest options and who decides them. Keep a negative result distinct from a commercial endorsement.

### "Why now, and why should we allocate our people?"

Answer: make the case around a relevant client problem and a bounded piece of work with possible reusable benefit. Invite OE to reject an unimportant use case. The commercial upside is a hypothesis to test, alongside the methodological learning.

Bring: a short scope, required team roles, access conditions and a clear approval path. An open-ended request for scrutiny transfers our development risk and unpriced labour to them.

## 3. Changes to the proposition

### Material finding 1: a plausible benefit to OE has become too close to a promise

Method-note purpose says scrutiny would give OE a tested approach that could enter its models. Its conclusion envisages a joint PEC approach. The email explicitly seeks funding to develop the test case. These are possible outcomes with different approvals; no cost, commitment or delivery agreement is supplied. [P1, P3]

Change: offer a defined first-phase deliverable and a costed decision path. OE's potential gain is reusable evidence or a joint client capability, subject to the test and rights. Ask which form of collaboration they want.

Recommended wording: "We propose jointly scoping a measurement and benchmark study. Its results would inform a separate decision on further analytical development, a PEC proposal and possible joint client work."

### Material finding 2: the benchmark can establish less than the proposal implies

The 709 microclusters originate in the 2020 Creative Radar study, using web-derived organisations and spatial clustering; the 2023 Geographies of Creativity report examines microclusters alongside larger clusters and corridors. A present-day Places comparison could differ because of dates, coverage, geography, definitions, identity or method. [B1-B3]

Change: select the exact benchmark object and classify the exercise as historical replication, matched-method comparison, or contemporary extension. Check access to original geometries and data before promising national coverage.

National agreement alone can conceal local errors. Local divergence can be informative without proving added accuracy or value. Shared source inputs can make agreement partly circular. Check source lineage rather than assuming independence or a shared provider.

### Material finding 3: one review request covers several distinct research projects

Section 7.1 proposes model comparison but gives no outcome, unit, sample or evaluation design. Its list ranges from predictive performance and classification stability to treatment heterogeneity and network spillovers. Section 8 also asks about causal before/after methods, source changes, skills and earnings. [P1]

Change: use three stages, each with its own deliverable and go/narrow/stop decision.

- First: measurement reliability and benchmark comparability, including classification, location, source lineage, coverage and disclosure controls.
- Second: additional analytical value for one defined outcome or client task, compared with a competent existing workflow. If predictive, use appropriately withheld data and independent outcome measurement.
- Third: causal evaluation for a named intervention only where timing, history, comparators and rights support it.

Retain the first-round GVA exclusion. Treat hidden-economy totals, transaction-level supply chains, causal impact and model integration as separately scoped options. Around ten years of data is a claim about available history, rather than a verdict on feasibility; frequency, gaps, stability, interventions and sample matter.

Start with one agreed subnational area where the evidence is sufficiently covered and authorised. Keep a UK-level comparison as a separately priced extension, contingent on benchmark access and comparable coverage. The existing UK-plus-local promise otherwise expands the test before those conditions are known.

### Material finding 4: disclosure control is a prerequisite for the chosen external use

The note's inconsistent suppression is an explicit known limitation. A partner test does not waive confidentiality, provider terms or statistical disclosure risk. [P1, section 6]

Change: permit the first study only through an authorised controlled-access dataset or appropriate aggregate outputs. Define who may see which inputs and results. Require source-specific rights and an approved disclosure policy before the relevant external release.

This need not halt all collaboration. Scoping can begin with specifications, authorised examples and a rights inventory while sensitive access is resolved.

### Material finding 5: "missing evidence" is too sweeping

"Productivity: no settled definition" confuses a familiar output-per-input concept with difficulty defining and measuring a creative subset. "Exports: unavailable at regional level" is too broad: ONS publishes subnational trade-in-services estimates. Those data do not automatically resolve the required creative subsector, local-authority or organisation-level question. [P1, section 3; D1]

Change the measurement paragraph to:

"Official and commercial sources provide important regional economic measures. For particular creative activities and local decisions, sector definitions, geographic detail, workplace allocation, coverage and timing can limit their use. Places aims to expose and test those gaps, while retaining established measures as a reference."

Replace productivity wording with: "Measuring productivity for a consistently defined creative subset is difficult where output and labour inputs do not share the required population, geography and time basis."

Replace exports wording with: "Subnational trade estimates exist; their coverage and sector detail need checking against the creative activity and geography required for this decision."

### Material finding 6: labour and relationship claims need tighter boundaries

The note's suggestion that national self-employment figures significantly understate freelance working lacks a supplied like-for-like comparison. Freelancers, self-employment, occupations, jobs and people have different denominators. Census country and year coverage also varies. [P1]

Change: describe an unresolved measurement question, with dated, defined sources for any numerical comparison. ESCO and occupation crosswalks support interpretation; they do not measure the number of workers by themselves. Job adverts indicate advertised demand rather than total employment or filled jobs.

Graph proximity, shared classifications and inferred relationships do not establish purchases, collaboration, supply chains or economic spillovers. Preserve edge meaning, source, date and evidential status. Ask OE which relationships are useful inputs and which require separate evidence.

### Competitive finding 7: the current deck does not establish additional value to OE

OE publicly offers city/regional forecasts, local economic audits, dashboards, scenarios and API delivery. Its own analyst tools already support selection, visualisation and comparison. Liam's published remit is Cities & Regions research and subnational analysis. [O1-O4]

Change: position Places as a potentially complementary organisation/activity-level evidence and review layer. Demonstrate a specific gain beyond OE's existing workflow. Avoid presenting OE as an economics data supplier to a novel dashboard, or as a source of credibility for everything Places produces.

A stronger commercial hypothesis is a jointly delivered creative-economy evidence brief for a named type of local decision. Only move to a subscription/integration claim after demand, licensing, maintenance and repeat delivery are demonstrated.

### Presentational finding 8: universal claims outrun the supplied proof

Deck slide 11 says activity is placed where it happens; the note says registered office and operating location are held separately. Holding both fields does not establish verified operating-location coverage. Slide 13 claims a broad working foundation without a dated capability/validation record. [P1, P2]

Change: "Registered and operating locations are distinguished; location confidence and coverage are part of the proposed review." Label screenshots with capture date, place, scope and purpose. Separate demonstrated workflow, available source coverage, modelled quantities and validation required.

This is a proof boundary, rather than evidence that the platform does not work. The supplied material is insufficient for a runtime verdict.
