## 2. Delivery model: Places-led

### 2.1 No economist required

The Belfast brief asks for three things under 6.6:
- "Contribute to the wider evaluation framework aligned with Heritage Place outcomes"
- "Identify appropriate KPIs and monitoring approaches"
- "Provide practical tools to measure participation, reach, impact and learning"

This is a **measurement architecture and KPI framework** task. Places supplies the measurement architecture (source tagging, denominators, evidence labelling, comparator construction); Bennett supplies the valuation and appraisal design. The brief does not ask for formal statistical methods to prove that the programme caused particular outcomes (techniques like difference-in-differences analysis or causal directed acyclic graphs), and it does not ask for a benefit-cost ratio. The Places methodology makes costing, evaluation and prioritisation rigorous through its evidence architecture, which does the work that would otherwise require a dedicated economist.

## 3. Methodology

Grounded in the Spillover & Strategic Option Value codebook, the methodology rests on four disciplines directly relevant to costing and evaluation.

**3.1 Claim typed costing.** Every figure we produce is labelled by what it is: a baseline (CONTEXT), a recommended cost (DESIGN, `timing=FORECAST`), or an observed spend (EFFECT). This prevents the common waterfront plan error of presenting a modelled visitor economy figure as a benefit *created* by the programme. Belfast's existing millions of visits are an `ECONOMIC_BASELINE`: context for the case, separate from any benefit the programme creates.

**3.2 Strategic option value / gated sequencing.** Our most distinctive pattern (NE Scotland year 1 to 7 sequencing; MITIH three phase investment sequence; Blackburn two stage pilot). We structure the animation programme as **sequenced investment decisions with explicit gates**: pilot, then evaluate, then scale. Each phase is an option (DEFER / STAGE_LEARN / EXPAND). This directly answers 6.5's "phase delivery over short, medium and long term timescales" and gives Maritime Belfast decision rights at each gate rather than one upfront commitment. It also makes 6.9 prioritisation a decision tool that supports active choices at each gate.

**3.3 Honest value basis.** Per the codebook's `value_basis` ladder, each cost and benefit is tagged: `OBSERVED_AMOUNT` (actual comparable spend), `DESCRIPTIVE_ESTIMATE` (modelled scale), `SCENARIO_ESTIMATE` (what if programme). `amount_role` separates COST from BENEFIT from ECONOMIC_BASELINE. Uncertainty is disclosed; we do not invent false precision. This is the discipline that lets 6.6's KPIs survive external scrutiny and NLHF review.

**3.4 Comparator benchmarking.** The 8 to 12 case studies (6.2, lead's scope) and Places' comparator waterfronts feed our cost and performance benchmarks. Where data permits, comparator performance frames audience and footfall expectations; where the evidence is too weak to draw a conclusion we say so. Null and negative findings are preserved (the Liverpool DCI "no evidence of effective clustering" precedent), a differentiator under the 40% methodology weighting.

## 4. Approach (what we actually deliver)

### 6.5 Costing, Funding & Delivery Models
- **Tiered indicative costings** by initiative class (pilot animations, seasonal programme, annual Maritime Signature Festival, temporary and public art, permanent installations, lighting), each with a low to high range, `value_basis`, and an **option register**: option holder (Maritime Belfast / partner / sponsor), exercise trigger, exercise/maintenance/delay cost, constrained alternative. The codebook's option register repurposed as a commissioning tool, held in Places.
- **Funding source map** per initiative: NLHF Heritage Place (delivery phase), Arts Council NI, Belfast CC, sponsorship, earned income, partnerships, with a gated funding model so each phase's funding is confirmed before commitment.
- **Delivery and governance options** as A/B/C: (A) Maritime Belfast led with commissioned delivery partners; (B) place based animation partnership or anchor institution model; (C) arms length delivery vehicle. Each with decision rights, risk and cost.
- **Phasing** as a gated roadmap: short (Y1 pilots), medium (Y2 to 3 seasonal + signature festival scaling), long (Y4+ permanent art and lighting), with a decision gate and evaluation checkpoint before each scale up.

### 6.6 Evaluation & Impact Framework

**Scope assumption:** our pricing assumes we contribute **KPI and measurement architecture** to a theory of change that the lead consultant builds as part of the wider CAP framework. We do not design the theory of change itself. If the lead asks us to take on theory of change and contribution analysis design, this is additional scope. We have delivered this on Wakefield CDF and are committed to it on True North, so the work is grounded in delivered practice. Priced at **£3,250** (5 days Bennett @ £650/day). See §6.

- **Aligned to Heritage Place outcomes**: the four NLHF investment principles named in the brief (saving heritage, protecting the environment, inclusion/access/participation, organisational sustainability). KPIs will be refined against the Heritage Place outcome framework once confirmed during the development phase. KPIs cover the brief's four measurement dimensions (participation, reach, impact, learning), each specified as a measurement row in Places: metric, numerator, denominator or eligible population, baseline, geography, time horizon, comparison logic, data source, consent needs.
- **Evidence and attribution labelling** so the framework does not overclaim: `NOT_ASSESSED → REPORTED → CORROBORATED`; `DESCRIPTIVE → CONTRIBUTION`. (No CAUSAL_ESTIMATE is claimed without the economist layer the brief does not require.) Comparator performance frames expectations; where the evidence is too weak to draw a conclusion we say so.
- **Double counting control**: direct, indirect and induced effects, leakage, displacement and overlap groups recorded in Places, so festival GVA and footfall are counted once across initiatives.
- **Practical tools**: a Places based monitoring dashboard, footfall and audience tracking approach, annual KPI review, and a decision register feeding the next gate. Usable by people who are not economists.

### 6.9 Costing & Prioritisation Framework (Phase Two)
- **Multi-criteria prioritisation** of the animation programme: impact, feasibility, cost, risk, and alignment with the Conservation Management Plan and Biodiversity Plan, producing a ranked, costed pipeline held in Places.
- **Detailed costings** building on 6.5, refined with Phase One findings.
- **Coordinated investment framework**: our costings integrated with the CMP and Biodiversity Plan into a single waterfront investment framework, using the option register to sequence spend and avoid double counting across workstreams. (Spatial mapping itself is 6.10, the lead's scope; we provide the costed prioritisation that the lead's GIS then displays.)
